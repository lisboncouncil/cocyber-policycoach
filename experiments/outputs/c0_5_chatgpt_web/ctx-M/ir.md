## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish mandatory requirements for preparing for, detecting, analysing, reporting, containing, eradicating, recovering from and learning from cybersecurity incidents affecting the detachment’s NATO, national and coalition information systems.

The policy is designed for a 2,000-person multinational military detachment operating in Romania under NATO Allied Command Operations operational control, with classified and unclassified domains, accredited cross-domain solutions, COMSEC material, SIGINT support functions, 24/7 theatre SOC monitoring and formal reporting obligations to NATO and applicable host-nation authorities.

This policy aligns incident response with NATO operational and information security requirements, the NATO Cyber Defence Pledge’s commitment to cyber-aware, cyber-trained, cyber-secure and cyber-enabled forces, and recognised international references including NIST CSF 2.0 / NIST SP 800-61r3, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A incident controls, GDPR and Romanian NIS2-transposition requirements where legally applicable. NATO’s Cyber Defence Pledge calls for stronger network defence, improved threat understanding, stakeholder interaction, skills, education, training and exercises. ([ccdcoe.org](https://ccdcoe.org/uploads/2018/11/NATO-160708-CyberDefencePledge.pdf))

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope

This policy applies to incident response for:

1. **All detachment network domains**
   - NATO UNCLASSIFIED (NU)
   - NATO RESTRICTED (NR)
   - NATO SECRET (NS)
   - MISSION SECRET

2. **All relevant technology and information assets**
   - 1,200 classified workstations
   - 800 unclassified workstations
   - servers, storage, network devices, boundary devices, sensors and management systems
   - accredited cross-domain solutions (CDS)
   - COMSEC equipment, keying material management systems and audit records
   - PKI, smart-card authentication infrastructure and domain-specific identity services
   - SCIF-room systems, TEMPEST-controlled areas and classified removable media
   - logging, SIEM, EDR, NDR, IDS/IPS, malware analysis and forensic platforms
   - contractor-provided systems connected to, supporting or administering detachment systems

3. **All incident types**
   - state-sponsored intrusion or attempted intrusion
   - malware, ransomware or destructive cyber activity
   - credential compromise or misuse
   - insider-threat activity
   - spillage between classification domains
   - COMSEC compromise or suspected compromise
   - CDS failure, bypass or unauthorised transfer
   - SIGINT system compromise or exposure of sources/methods
   - supply-chain compromise
   - unauthorised physical access, TEMPEST-relevant compromise indicators or tampering
   - personal data breach involving NATO civilian staff, contractors or other protected persons
   - cyber activity connected to kinetic operations or regional contingency

4. **All personnel**
   - military active personnel
   - NATO civilian staff
   - cleared contractors
   - national support elements and liaison officers
   - visitors or exercise participants with authorised CIS access

### 3.2 Out of scope

This policy does not replace:

- NATO, national or coalition classification guides;
- national COMSEC authority directives;
- SIGINT handling rules and national SIGINT caveats;
- rules of engagement, operational cyber tasking orders or intelligence authorities;
- disciplinary, counterintelligence or criminal-investigation procedures;
- physical security and force-protection orders.

Where such authorities apply, this policy governs the cybersecurity response interface only.

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Cybersecurity event** | Any observable occurrence in a system, service, network, facility or user activity that may affect cybersecurity. |
| **Adverse cybersecurity event** | An event with potentially negative impact on cybersecurity. NIST SP 800-61r3 uses this concept in its incident-response profile. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf)) |
| **Cybersecurity incident** | One or more adverse cybersecurity events that have been declared by authorised incident-response personnel as requiring coordinated response. |
| **Major incident** | An incident that materially affects mission assurance, classified information, COMSEC, cross-domain integrity, operational planning, SIGINT support, command and control, coalition interoperability or legal/regulatory reporting obligations. |
| **Spillage** | The introduction, transfer, processing, storage or display of information on a system not authorised for that information’s classification, caveat, originator-control marking or need-to-know restriction. |
| **COMSEC incident** | Any actual or suspected loss, compromise, unauthorised access, unauthorised handling, unauthorised disclosure, accounting discrepancy or procedural violation involving COMSEC equipment, keying material or related records. |
| **Personal data breach** | A breach of security affecting personal data as understood under GDPR; GDPR Article 33 requires supervisory-authority notification without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours when the breach is likely to result in risk to individuals. ([gdprinfo.eu](https://gdprinfo.eu/en-article-33)) |
| **Incident lead** | The person formally appointed by ISSM or SOC Battle Captain to coordinate response for a declared incident. |
| **Two-person integrity (TPI)** | Requirement for two authorised persons to be present and accountable for specified high-grade COMSEC, keying or operational-release actions. |

## 5. Roles and responsibilities

NIST SP 800-61r3 maps incident response to CSF 2.0 and states that cybersecurity roles, responsibilities and authorities should include incident response and be documented in policy; this policy assigns those roles for the detachment. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf))

### 5.1 Detachment Commander

- Owns mission risk arising from cybersecurity incidents.
- Approves major incident declarations where mission, operational or diplomatic effects are significant.
- Authorises extraordinary operational risk acceptance, unless reserved to SHAPE, framework nation or national authority.
- Approves public affairs posture and external disclosure, subject to NATO, national and ORCON restrictions.
- Ensures incident response is resourced commensurate with the detachment’s threat profile.

### 5.2 ISSM, J6 chain

- Owns and maintains this policy and the Incident Response Plan.
- Acts as senior incident-response authority for detachment CIS.
- Ensures incident response is integrated across NU, NR, NS and MISSION SECRET domains.
- Maintains reporting interfaces to theatre SOC, NCIRC/NATO Cyber Security Centre processes, NCSC channels, host-nation CERT/DNSC where applicable, and national COMSEC authorities.
- Ensures lessons learned are converted into corrective actions, playbook changes and training updates.

### 5.3 J6 / CIS Operations

- Maintains network, system and identity-service availability during response.
- Executes containment, isolation, rebuild, restoration and configuration changes.
- Maintains domain-specific administration; no cross-domain identity federation may be introduced as an incident workaround.
- Provides system diagrams, asset inventories, privileged-access logs and restoration evidence to incident responders.

### 5.4 Theatre SOC / SOC Battle Captain

- Provides 24/7 monitoring, triage and alerting.
- Opens incident tickets and appoints an interim incident lead until ISSM confirms or replaces the lead.
- Coordinates with boundary sensors, SIEM, EDR/NDR platforms and malware-analysis functions.
- Escalates suspected major incidents immediately to ISSM and J6.

### 5.5 Security Operations / Cyber Incident Response Team

- Conducts technical analysis, containment, eradication and recovery support.
- Preserves evidence and chain of custody.
- Maintains playbooks for priority threats: APT intrusion, insider misuse, COMSEC anomaly, cross-domain spillage, CDS malfunction, ransomware/destructive malware, supply-chain compromise, TEMPEST/physical-cyber indicators and personal data breach.
- Provides recommendations on mission impact and technical risk.

### 5.6 S2/G2 / Counterintelligence

- Leads insider-threat, HUMINT recruitment, foreign-intelligence and adversary-attribution coordination.
- Advises on need-to-know, compartmentation, ORCON, SIGINT and intelligence-source protection.
- Coordinates with national CI channels as authorised.

### 5.7 COMSEC Custodian and Alternate Custodian

- Leads COMSEC-specific incident handling with national COMSEC authority involvement.
- Enforces two-person integrity for high-grade keying material.
- Maintains COMSEC chain-of-custody evidence.
- Advises ISSM and Commander on cryptographic compromise risk.
- Ensures COMSEC reports are handled under applicable NATO/national COMSEC channels. NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 notes that separate provisions for compromise of cryptographic material are issued by military authorities to COMSEC authorities. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

### 5.8 Data Protection Officer / Privacy Lead

- Determines whether an incident involving civilian or contractor personal data is a GDPR-reportable personal data breach.
- Coordinates with Legal Adviser before notification to supervisory authorities or data subjects.
- Ensures personal data breach records are maintained.
- GDPR Article 33 requires breach documentation including facts, effects and remedial action. ([gdprinfo.eu](https://gdprinfo.eu/en-article-33))

### 5.9 Legal Adviser

- Advises on NATO SOFA, GDPR, Romanian cybersecurity law, national jurisdiction, evidence handling, contractor obligations and lawful disclosure.
- Reviews all external notifications where legally required or operationally sensitive.
- Ensures host-nation reporting respects SOFA, classification and originator-control restrictions.

### 5.10 Public Affairs Officer

- Coordinates incident-related public statements only after Commander approval.
- Does not confirm classified systems, operational impact, attribution, sources, methods or vulnerabilities unless specifically authorised.

### 5.11 System Owners and Data Owners

- Identify mission criticality, authorised users, classification, ORCON caveats and restoration priorities.
- Validate recovery and approve return to operational service.
- Participate in lessons-learned activities.

### 5.12 Contractors and vendors

- Must report suspected incidents immediately through contractually defined channels and the SOC.
- Must preserve evidence, logs and potentially affected equipment when instructed.
- Must not independently notify external parties about detachment incidents unless legally compelled and approved by contract, Legal Adviser and ISSM.
- Must support supply-chain investigation and provide software bills of materials, maintenance records, remote-access logs and relevant vulnerability disclosures where contractually required.

### 5.13 All users

- Must immediately report suspicious activity, lost or stolen devices, possible compromise, spillage, unauthorised access, phishing, insider concerns and COMSEC anomalies.
- Must not investigate beyond preserving the situation and reporting.
- Must not move data between domains, use personal devices, use unauthorised media or attempt self-remediation of classified-system incidents unless directed by authorised responders.

## 6. Incident-response principles

1. **Mission assurance first.** Response decisions must protect NATO mission execution, command and control, operational planning, intelligence support and force protection.
   - **Authority:** AJP-3.20; NIST CSF 2.0 Govern/Respond; NIST SP 800-61r3.

2. **Classification boundaries are non-negotiable.** Incidents must be handled within the affected classification domain unless an accredited CDS, approved releasability decision and need-to-know basis exist. NATO classifications indicate sensitivity and require protection proportionate to damage from unauthorised access or disclosure. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))
   - **Authority:** C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure E; AC/322-D(2017)0009 reference; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.12, A.5.13, A.5.15, A.5.33.

3. **Need-to-know and ORCON apply during incidents.** Incident urgency does not override clearance, mission role, originator control or releasability limits.
   - **Authority:** C-M(2002)49-REV1 Article 1; coalition information-sharing agreements; NATO classification regime.

4. **Default-deny access.** Emergency access may be granted only through approved break-glass procedures, time-bounded authorisation and post-use review.
   - **Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.15, A.5.16, A.5.18, A.8.2.

5. **Evidence preservation.** Responders must preserve the integrity, provenance and chain of custody of logs, images, memory captures, malware samples, CDS audit records and COMSEC evidence.
   - **Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 RS.AN-06, RS.AN-07; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.28.

6. **Coordinated reporting.** Internal and external notifications must follow defined reporting paths and classification rules. NIST SP 800-61r3 states that incident notification should follow applicable laws, regulations and established procedures. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf))
   - **Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 RS.CO-02; NATO/NCIRC processes; Romanian GEO 155/2024 Article 15 where applicable; GDPR Articles 33–34.

7. **Two-person integrity for COMSEC and designated operational releases.** No single individual may access, move, load, destroy or account for high-grade keying material or release designated operational orders where dual-key procedure is required.
   - **Authority:** National COMSEC authority directives; detachment COMSEC SOP; organisational profile. **Precise public clause unsupported.**

8. **Least-disruptive effective containment.** Response must balance rapid containment and mission continuity against intelligence-gain/lawful-observation requirements, especially for state-sponsored APT activity.
   - **Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 RS.MA-03; NIST SP 800-61r3 notes trade-offs between rapid recovery and observing attacker behaviour. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf))

9. **Continuous improvement.** Every major incident, exercise and red-team engagement must produce lessons learned and tracked corrective actions. NIST SP 800-61r3 identifies lessons-learned meetings and follow-up reports as sources of improvement. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf))
   - **Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 ID.IM-01 to ID.IM-04; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 10, A.5.27.

## 7. Incident severity classification

| Severity | Name | Criteria | Required escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S0** | Crisis / strategic incident | Confirmed or strongly suspected compromise of NS, MISSION SECRET, SIGINT sources/methods, COMSEC keying material, CDS integrity, command-and-control systems, or coordinated cyber activity linked to kinetic operations; potential NATO-wide or multinational operational impact | Immediate: SOC Battle Captain, ISSM, Commander, J6, S2/G2, COMSEC Custodian if relevant, Legal Adviser, theatre SOC, NCIRC/NATO Cyber Security Centre process and SHAPE reporting chain |
| **S1** | Major incident | Significant mission degradation; confirmed adversary presence; high-risk spillage; privileged-account compromise; supply-chain compromise; destructive malware; reportable personal data breach; significant cross-border or host-nation impact | Within 15 minutes internally; theatre SOC and higher headquarters as directed |
| **S2** | Serious incident | Localised compromise of classified workstation/server; failed but sophisticated intrusion; suspected insider misuse; malware contained to limited enclave; unauthorised removable media on classified domain | Within 30 minutes to SOC and ISSM duty representative |
| **S3** | Moderate incident | Confirmed malware on NU endpoint, phishing compromise without privileged access, policy violation with limited impact, suspicious but contained activity | Same duty shift to SOC; ticketed and triaged |
| **S4** | Low / event of interest | False positive, benign anomaly, unsuccessful commodity attack, user-reported suspicious email without execution | Logged, monitored and closed or escalated based on analysis |

**Control authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 RS.MA-02, RS.MA-03, RS.MA-04; NIST SP 800-61r3 recommends triaging, validating, categorising and prioritising incidents based on scope, impact, urgency and resources. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf))

### 8.10 Special incident categories

#### 8.10.1 Cross-domain spillage

- Immediately stop transfer path, disable affected workflow and preserve CDS logs.
- Notify ISSM, data owner, security officer and CDS administrator.
- Determine source domain, destination domain, classification, caveat, ORCON and recipients.
- Sanitise affected systems only after evidence capture and classification-authority direction.
- Review CDS rules, filters, release workflows and user training before resuming transfer.

**Authority:** C-M(2002)49-REV1 Article 1 / Enclosure E; AC/322-D(2017)0009 reference; NIST CSF 2.0 RS.AN-03, RS.AN-07, RS.MA-03; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.12, A.5.13, A.8.10.

#### 8.10.2 COMSEC incident

- Enforce TPI immediately.
- Secure and account for equipment, keying material and audit records.
- Notify COMSEC Custodian and ISSM immediately.
- Notify national COMSEC authority through approved channel.
- Do not copy, transmit, photograph or discuss keying material outside authorised COMSEC channels.
- Execute rekey/zeroise/destruction/replacement only under authorised procedure.

**Authority:** National COMSEC authority directives; NATO COMSEC references; C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure E para. 41 reference to separate cryptographic compromise provisions. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) **Precise public clause unsupported.**

#### 8.10.3 SIGINT-related incident

- Compartment incident knowledge to authorised SIGINT and cyber personnel.
- Protect sources, methods, tasking, collection equities and partner caveats.
- Coordinate with national SIGINT authority and S2/G2.
- Release technical indicators only after sanitisation and caveat review.

**Authority:** C-M(2002)49-REV1 notes that SIGINT requires stringent rules beyond the policy baseline. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) MC 101 / NACSI references. **Precise public clause unsupported.**

#### 8.10.4 Insider-threat incident

- Do not alert the suspected insider unless necessary to prevent further harm.
- Coordinate SOC, ISSM, S2/G2, CI, Commander, Legal Adviser and national chain.
- Preserve user activity, authentication logs, endpoint evidence, badge data and communications metadata where lawful and authorised.
- Apply least-disruptive containment compatible with CI objectives and mission assurance.

**Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 DE.CM-03, RS.AN-06, RS.AN-07; AC/35-D/1029 reference; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.6.1, A.6.5, A.6.8.

#### 8.10.5 Supply-chain compromise

- Suspend affected product, update channel, vendor remote access or maintenance activity.
- Preserve vendor communications, hashes, update packages, administrative logs and affected asset list.
- Check NATO-approved equipment list status and procurement approvals.
- Coordinate with framework nation, host-nation contractor authority if applicable, NATO channels and vendor.
- Resume only after technical validation and ISSM approval.

**Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC, ID.RA-09, ID.RA-10; Romanian GEO 155/2024 Art. 13 supply-chain security for covered entities. ([legislatie.just.ro](https://legislatie.just.ro/public/DetaliiDocument/293121)) ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.19–A.5.23.

#### 8.10.6 Personal data breach

- Immediately involve DPO and Legal Adviser.
- Determine controller/processor role, affected persons, data categories, number of records, risk to individuals, encryption status, cross-border implications and notification obligations.
- Preserve 72-hour GDPR decision timeline from awareness.
- Notify supervisory authority and/or individuals only through approved legal and command process.

**Authority:** GDPR Articles 32–34; GDPR Article 32 requires risk-appropriate measures including confidentiality, integrity, availability, restoration ability and testing of controls. ([gdprinfo.eu](https://gdprinfo.eu/en-article-32))

#### 8.10.7 Kinetic-cyber convergence

- Treat cyber activity correlated with regional contingency, physical attack, sabotage, jamming or force-protection events as at least S1 unless ISSM and Commander downgrade.
- Coordinate with operations, intelligence, force protection and theatre SOC.
- Preserve cyber evidence without delaying life-safety or mission-critical action.

**Authority:** AJP-3.20; NIST CSF 2.0 RS.MA-03, RC.RP-04; NATO operational command authority.

## 9. Exceptions

1. **Permitted exception grounds**
   - immediate life safety;
   - urgent operational necessity approved by Commander or delegated battle captain;
   - higher NATO, SHAPE, national or COMSEC authority direction;
   - legal compulsion confirmed by Legal Adviser;
   - technical impossibility during degraded operations.

2. **Exception requirements**
   - Exceptions must be time-bounded, risk-assessed and documented.
   - Exceptions involving classified information, COMSEC, CDS, SIGINT or ORCON require specialist authority review.
   - Emergency exceptions must be reported to ISSM within 24 hours and reviewed within 5 working days.
   - Exceptions must not authorise unauthorised disclosure to non-NATO parties or violation of originator control.

3. **Non-exceptionable controls**
   - Need-to-know.
   - Classification marking and handling.
   - TPI for high-grade COMSEC.
   - Evidence preservation for S0/S1.
   - Legal review before external public disclosure.
   - Prohibition on unauthorised cross-domain transfer.

**Authority:** NIST CSF 2.0 ID.RA-07; C-M(2002)49-REV1 Article 1; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.36.

## 10. Monitoring, metrics and review

### 10.1 Monitoring

ISSM shall monitor policy implementation through:

- SOC ticket review;
- incident severity and closure dashboards;
- exercise results;
- red-team findings;
- after-action reports;
- audit of evidence handling;
- COMSEC incident reconciliation;
- CDS transfer and rejection review;
- personal data breach register review;
- contractor incident-reporting compliance;
- corrective-action tracking.

### 10.2 Minimum metrics

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| S0/S1 escalation to ISSM | Immediate, target under 15 minutes |
| S2 escalation to ISSM duty representative | Target under 30 minutes |
| Incident ticket creation for user-reported event | Same duty shift; immediate for classified, COMSEC or suspected compromise |
| Completion of initial triage | S0/S1: 30 minutes; S2: 2 hours; S3/S4: 1 duty day |
| After-action report | S0/S1: within 10 working days of recovery; S2: within 15 working days if required |
| Corrective-action assignment | Within 5 working days after after-action approval |
| Corrective-action closure | Risk-based; overdue items reported monthly to Commander |
| Exercise frequency | At least 6 per year |
| Red-team frequency | At least 2 per year |
| Personnel cyber training | 40 hours per staff member per year, including incident-reporting content |

### 10.3 Review

- ISSM shall review this policy at least annually.
- Review shall also occur after:
  - S0/S1 incident;
  - major exercise or red-team engagement;
  - material change to NATO security policy;
  - change in Romanian cybersecurity or GDPR obligations;
  - change in mission, framework nation, network architecture, CDS accreditation, COMSEC procedures or threat profile.
- The review shall verify alignment with NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-61r3, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A, NATO security policy and applicable host-nation law.

NIST SP 800-61r3 states that incident response is part of cybersecurity risk management and should be integrated across organisational operations, with lessons learned used to improve governance and risk management. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-61r3.pdf))

## 11. Compliance and enforcement

- Violation of this policy may result in removal of system access, disciplinary action, contract remedies, clearance review, counterintelligence referral, COMSEC sanctions or legal action.
- Contractors are subject to contract terms, clearance requirements, NATO security obligations and host-nation screening requirements.
- Failure to report incidents, concealment, unauthorised investigation, unauthorised disclosure or unauthorised cross-domain transfer shall be treated as serious security violations.
- Where Romanian cybersecurity law applies to an entity or function, GEO 155/2024 provides administrative sanction mechanisms for non-compliance, including penalties for essential and important entities. ([legislatie.just.ro](https://legislatie.just.ro/public/DetaliiDocument/293121)) Applicability to the detachment must be assessed through Legal Adviser due to NATO SOFA and mission-specific status.
