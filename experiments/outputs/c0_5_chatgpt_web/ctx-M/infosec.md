## 1. Purpose

This General Cybersecurity Policy establishes the mandatory cybersecurity governance, risk-management, technical, operational and compliance requirements for all information, communication and cyber-defence activities of the Detachment.

The policy is tailored to a multinational NATO military deployment conducting cyber defence, CIS operations, SIGINT support and interoperability exercises across NU, NR, NS and MISSION SECRET domains. It implements NATO security requirements for classified information and CIS; supports NATO’s cyber-defence commitment to strong, resilient, well-resourced, trained and exercised cyber defences; and uses NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 / 27002:2022 as common technical reference frameworks for multinational interoperability. NATO Security Policy establishes basic principles and minimum standards for classified information protection across NATO Nations and NATO civil and military bodies; NATO’s Cyber Defence Pledge commits Allies to strengthen cyber defences, resource cyber capabilities, share threat understanding, and enhance training and exercises. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

1. **Personnel:** all assigned military personnel, NATO civilian staff, cleared contractors, visitors, augmentees and mission partners using or administering Detachment systems.
2. **Information:** all NATO, national, coalition, mission, operational, intelligence, COMSEC, administrative, logistics and personal data handled by the Detachment, regardless of format or medium.
3. **Systems and networks:** NU, NR, NS, MISSION SECRET, management networks, sensors, SOC interfaces, endpoint fleets, servers, cryptographic systems, PKI services, accredited cross-domain solutions, tactical CIS, removable media, backup systems, logging platforms and security tooling.
4. **Facilities:** headquarters areas, server rooms, SCIFs, registry areas, COMSEC areas, exercise facilities, contractor workspaces and deployed CIS locations.
5. **Third parties:** suppliers, service providers, host-nation contractors, support personnel and coalition partners with access to Detachment information, CIS or facilities.

NATO classified information and CIS handling it are subject to NATO Security Policy and supporting CIS Security directives. NATO UNCLASSIFIED and releasable-to-public information are to be handled under NATO information-management policy and the policy on non-classified NATO information. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

## 3. Policy Authority and Compliance Hierarchy

Where requirements conflict, the stricter or higher-authority requirement applies unless formally resolved by the competent NATO, national, host-nation or mission authority.

Compliance hierarchy:

1. NATO Security Policy, SHAPE direction, NATO CIS Security directives, NATO accreditation decisions and NCIRC/NCSC direction.
2. Applicable national security, COMSEC, SIGINT, personnel-security and mission-specific coalition rules.
3. Host-nation legal obligations applicable under SOFA, Romanian law, EU law and bilateral/multilateral arrangements.
4. This policy and subordinate Detachment SOPs, SecOPs, annexes and technical standards.
5. Civilian-overlay references: NIST CSF 2.0, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 / 27002:2022, GDPR and NIS2-aligned Romanian cybersecurity obligations, where applicable.

The NATO SOFA defines “force,” “civilian component,” sending State and receiving State roles, requires respect for receiving-State law, and allocates jurisdiction for offences including security offences; therefore the Detachment must coordinate cybersecurity, evidence, personal-data and contractor matters through legal and command channels rather than treating host-nation law as either fully displaced or fully controlling in all cases. ([nato.int](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/official_texts_17265.htm))

## 5. Cybersecurity Principles

The Detachment shall apply the following principles across all domains:

1. **Mission assurance first:** cybersecurity exists to enable NATO operations, command and control, intelligence support and coalition interoperability.
2. **Classification-domain separation:** NU, NR, NS and MISSION SECRET are separate environments; no cross-domain identity federation, trust assumption or uncontrolled transfer is permitted.
3. **Need-to-know and default deny:** clearance is necessary but not sufficient; access requires validated mission need.
4. **Least privilege and separation of duties:** privileged roles, release authorities, COMSEC functions, operational order release and CDS release functions must be segregated.
5. **Defence in depth:** controls shall be layered across personnel, physical, CIS, cryptographic, procedural and monitoring domains.
6. **Accreditation-led security:** systems, interconnections and cross-domain transfers operate only within approved accreditation and SAA/designated authority conditions.
7. **Resilience under contested conditions:** systems must be able to continue, degrade safely or recover during APT activity, insider compromise, supply-chain disruption, kinetic-cyber convergence or regional contingency.
8. **Auditable accountability:** security decisions, exceptions, transfers, privileged actions, COMSEC custody and incident actions must be logged or otherwise documented.
9. **Privacy and proportionality:** civilian and contractor personal data shall be processed lawfully, minimally and securely, consistent with GDPR, SOFA and mission necessity.
10. **Continuous improvement:** exercises, red teams, SOC findings, incidents, audits and vulnerability assessments feed a tracked improvement programme.

NATO CIS Security principles explicitly include security risk management, minimality, least privilege, self-protecting CIS, defence in depth, up-to-date security posture, resilience, assurance and compliance monitoring. ([gns.gov.pt](https://www.gns.gov.pt/docs/ac-35-d-2004.pdf))

## 7. Exceptions and Waivers

1. Exceptions are not permitted for:
   - unauthorised cross-domain connections;
   - unauthorised release of classified or ORCON information;
   - unapproved COMSEC handling;
   - bypass of accreditation conditions;
   - operation of unapproved cryptographic mechanisms for classified information;
   - deliberate suppression of incident reporting.

2. All other exceptions require:
   - written mission justification;
   - impacted systems, data and users;
   - classification and privacy assessment;
   - threat, vulnerability and mission-impact analysis;
   - compensating controls;
   - residual-risk owner;
   - expiry date;
   - ISSM review;
   - approval by Commander or SAA/designated authority as applicable.

3. Emergency operational deviations must be reported to the ISSM and Commander as soon as operationally feasible and documented within 24 hours.

**Control basis:** AC/35-D/2004-REV3 section 6.2; NIST CSF 2.0 ID.RA-07, GV.RM-06; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 6.1.3, 8.1.

## 8. Monitoring, Metrics and Review

The ISSM shall maintain a cybersecurity dashboard reviewed monthly by command and quarterly with relevant functional leads. Metrics shall include:

- accreditation status and open conditions;
- critical vulnerabilities by domain and age;
- patch compliance by mission tier;
- privileged-account review status;
- CDS transfer volume, rejection and exception statistics;
- incident counts by severity and category;
- NCIRC/NCSC reporting timeliness;
- COMSEC inventory and incident status;
- log-source health and SOC sensor coverage;
- backup test success;
- training completion and exercise findings;
- red-team findings and POA&M closure;
- supplier risk status;
- privacy/security breach events.

NIST CSF 2.0 treats GOVERN, IDENTIFY, PROTECT, DETECT, RESPOND and RECOVER as concurrent functions, with GOVERN informing implementation of the others, and provides categories for oversight, improvement, monitoring, response and recovery. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29))

## 9. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in suspension of access, removal from privileged duties, contractor remedies, disciplinary action under applicable military or civilian authority, referral to security/counterintelligence authorities, COMSEC incident action, legal action or host-nation coordination as appropriate.

Security incidents, suspected insider activity, suspected espionage, cryptographic incidents, unauthorised disclosure, spillage and significant operational impacts must be escalated immediately through the security and incident-response chain.
