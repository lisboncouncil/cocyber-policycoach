## 2. Purpose

This policy defines how the detachment protects operational, classified, mission, intelligence, administrative, contractor, civilian staff, and personal data across all detachment information systems and mission processes.

It is designed for a multinational military environment operating in Romania, under NATO operational control, with strict separation between NATO UNCLASSIFIED, NATO RESTRICTED, NATO SECRET, and mission-specific SECRET domains. It implements NATO security requirements for classified information and CIS, host-nation/EU data protection obligations for personal data, and recognised cybersecurity control references including NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

NATO’s public security directive states that NATO classifications are used to ensure protection proportionate to the damage caused by unauthorised access or disclosure, and distinguishes COSMIC TOP SECRET, NATO SECRET, NATO CONFIDENTIAL, and NATO RESTRICTED by severity of damage to NATO. This policy applies those principles to the detachment’s deployed data environment. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6885/ac_35-d_2002_rev5.pdf))

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope

This policy applies to:

1. All military personnel, NATO civilian staff, cleared contractors, temporary augmentees, liaison officers, visitors granted system access, and supporting host-nation personnel.
2. All data created, received, stored, processed, transmitted, displayed, discussed, archived, destroyed, or transferred by or for the detachment.
3. All classification domains:
   - **NU / NATO UNCLASSIFIED** — administrative, logistics, coalition coordination.
   - **NR / NATO RESTRICTED** — restricted operational planning.
   - **NS / NATO SECRET** — operational planning, intelligence, command and control.
   - **MISSION SECRET** — national or coalition SECRET mission-specific operations.
4. All workstations, servers, mobile devices, removable media, printers, collaboration tools, tactical CIS, COMSEC-related systems, cross-domain solutions, backups, logs, audit records, and paper records.
5. All personal data concerning NATO civilian staff, contractors, host-nation personnel, visitors, and other identifiable individuals where GDPR or Romanian host-nation law applies.
6. All data transfers between domains, coalition partners, NATO bodies, host-nation authorities, national authorities, suppliers, and contractors.

### 3.2 Out of scope

This policy does not replace:

1. NATO classification guides, originator release instructions, security accreditation decisions, or NCIRC incident handling procedures.
2. National COMSEC authority directives.
3. National caveats or bilateral/multilateral intelligence-sharing agreements.
4. Lawful operational orders issued by competent command authority.

Where there is a conflict, the more protective applicable NATO, national, coalition, or host-nation rule shall apply unless competent legal and command authority determines otherwise.

## 5. Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Data** | Any information, whether digital, verbal, visual, paper, magnetic, optical, removable, logged, archived, or transmitted. |
| **Classified data** | Information classified under NATO, national, or coalition classification regimes. |
| **Personal data** | Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, where GDPR or host-nation privacy law applies. |
| **Special category personal data** | Personal data requiring heightened GDPR protection, including health, biometric, or other Article 9 categories. |
| **Mission data** | Data generated for operational planning, command and control, intelligence support, exercises, or coalition mission execution. |
| **Originator control / ORCON** | Requirement that the originator controls further dissemination, release, extraction, reproduction, or downgrading. |
| **Spillage** | Data placed, transmitted, stored, displayed, or processed on a system or domain not accredited or authorised for that classification, caveat, originator restriction, or need-to-know group. |

| **Need-to-know** | Access is granted only where the individual has a valid mission requirement in addition to appropriate clearance and authorisation. |

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

### 6.1 Detachment Commander

The Commander shall:

1. Approve this policy and ensure command enforcement.
2. Accept residual data protection risk only within delegated authority.
3. Ensure sufficient resources for data protection, SOC integration, accreditation, COMSEC, training, incident response, and exercises.
4. Ensure data protection requirements are integrated into operational planning, exercises, mission change control, and coalition information sharing.

**Control references:** NATO Cyber Defence Pledge ¶5.I–VII; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.OC, GV.RM, GV.RR, GV.PO, GV.OV; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 5.1, 5.2, 6.1, 9.3.

### 6.2 ISSM / J6

The ISSM is the policy owner and shall:

1. Maintain this policy, supporting standards, control matrices, and evidence.
2. Ensure domain-specific security controls are implemented and maintained.
3. Coordinate accreditation, risk assessments, exception requests, and corrective actions.
4. Ensure technical controls support classification separation, least privilege, encryption, monitoring, backup, and incident response.
5. Report significant non-compliance to the Commander and relevant NATO security authority.

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§3, 6, 7.5–7.7; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RR, ID.RA, PR.DS, DE.CM; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 5.3, 6.1, 8.1, 9.1; ISO Annex A 5.2, 5.4, 5.8, 5.36.

### 6.3 Data Protection Officer / Privacy Adviser

For GDPR-relevant civilian, contractor, visitor, and host-nation personal data, the DPO or Privacy Adviser shall:

1. Advise the Commander, ISSM, J1/J4/J6, and processors on data protection obligations.
2. Monitor compliance with this policy and GDPR-relevant procedures.
3. Advise on DPIAs and privacy-by-design measures.
4. Act as contact point for data subjects and supervisory authorities where applicable.
5. Escalate unresolved privacy risks to the Commander and Legal Adviser.

The DPO shall be involved in a timely manner in personal-data matters and must be able to operate independently for DPO tasks; EDPB guidance states that a DPO informs and advises, monitors compliance, advises on DPIAs, cooperates with the DPA, and acts as a contact point. ([edpb.europa.eu](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme-data-protection-guide/data-protection-officer_en))

**Control references:** GDPR Articles 37–39; ISO Annex A 5.34; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RR, GV.PO, GV.OV.

### 6.4 Legal Adviser

The Legal Adviser shall:

1. Advise on NATO SOFA, host-nation law, GDPR applicability, Romanian cybersecurity obligations, and coalition information-sharing agreements.
2. Review international transfers of personal data and operational disclosures to non-NATO or host-nation entities.
3. Support breach notification decisions where personal data is involved.

**Control references:** GDPR Articles 5, 6, 9, 30, 32–35, Chapter V; NIS2 Articles 21 and 23; NATO SOFA; ISO Annex A 5.31, 5.32, 5.34.

### 6.5 Information Owners / Originators

Information owners and originators, including J2, J3, J4, J6, and authorised mission cells, shall:

1. Assign and review classification, caveats, ORCON markings, retention requirements, and dissemination restrictions.
2. Authorise release, extraction, reproduction, downgrading, or transfer of their data.
3. Confirm mission need-to-know for access requests.
4. Ensure shared intelligence products comply with originator and coalition agreements.

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§2–6, 36–38, 70–72; ISO Annex A 5.12, 5.13, 5.14, 5.33; NIST CSF 2.0 ID.AM, PR.DS.

### 6.6 System Owners / CIS Operational Authorities

System owners shall:

1. Maintain domain-specific system security plans, accreditation evidence, asset inventories, data-flow diagrams, and risk assessments.
2. Ensure systems process only data for which they are accredited.
3. Implement change control and security impact assessment before mission, software, network, or architecture changes.
4. Maintain backup, restoration, logging, monitoring, and vulnerability remediation plans.

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§7.6–7.7 and Appendix 2; NIST CSF 2.0 ID.AM, ID.RA, PR.PS, PR.IR, DE.CM; ISO Annex A 5.9, 5.10, 5.37, 8.8, 8.9, 8.13, 8.15.

### 6.7 Security Office

The Security Office shall:

1. Verify clearances and need-to-know before access to NATO CONFIDENTIAL and above, NATO SECRET, MISSION SECRET, SCIFs, registries, and classified meetings.
2. Maintain briefing, acknowledgement, visitor, badge, and debrief records.
3. Coordinate insider-threat referrals and security investigations.
4. Support records of security breaches and corrective action tracking.

NATO personnel security requires individuals accessing NATO information classified NATO CONFIDENTIAL or above to have an appropriate valid personnel security clearance, need-to-know, security briefing, and acknowledgement of responsibilities. ([jftc.nato.int](https://www.jftc.nato.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/AC_35-D_2000_REV8.pdf))

### 6.8 COMSEC Custodian

The COMSEC Custodian shall:

1. Maintain auditable chain of custody for COMSEC material.
2. Enforce national COMSEC authority procedures.
3. Enforce two-person integrity for high-grade keying material.
4. Ensure COMSEC incidents are immediately escalated through national and NATO channels.

**Control references:** National COMSEC authority directives; NATO COMSEC directives where applicable; ISO Annex A 5.10, 5.14, 8.24. **Two-person integrity requirement is based on elicited internal operational requirement; precise public clause citation not provided.**

### 6.9 Theatre SOC / NCIRC Liaison

The SOC liaison shall:

1. Ensure continuous monitoring at domain boundaries and accredited sensors.
2. Correlate alerts with mission context while preserving classification boundaries.
3. Coordinate incident reporting to NCIRC/NCSC and host-nation CERT where applicable.
4. Provide detection, response, and lessons-learned evidence to the ISSM.

**Control references:** NIST CSF 2.0 DE.CM, DE.AE, RS.MA, RS.AN, RS.CO, RS.MI; ISO Annex A 8.15, 8.16, 5.24–5.28; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§84–93.

### 6.10 All Personnel and Contractors

All personnel and contractors shall:

1. Protect data according to classification, marking, caveat, need-to-know, and domain rules.
2. Use only authorised systems and storage locations.
3. Report suspected spillage, compromise, loss, unauthorised access, phishing, malware, insider activity, or privacy breach immediately.
4. Complete required annual and role-specific training.
5. Comply with debrief, media, travel, removable-media, and personal-device restrictions.

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2000-REV8 §2; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§70–72, 84–93; ISO Annex A 6.3, 6.6, 6.8; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AT, RS.CO.

## 7. Data Protection Principles

The detachment shall apply the following principles:

1. **Mission necessity and legality** — Data shall be processed only for authorised mission, administrative, security, legal, or contractual purposes.
   - **References:** GDPR Articles 5 and 6; ISO Annex A 5.31, 5.34; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.OC, GV.RM.

2. **Classification first** — Classification, caveats, ORCON, and national/coalition release restrictions determine the minimum protection baseline.
   - **References:** AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§2–6, 36–38.

3. **Need-to-know and default deny** — Clearance alone is not sufficient; access requires mission role and explicit authorisation.
   - **References:** AC/35-D/2000-REV8 §2; ISO Annex A 5.15–5.18, 8.2–8.3; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA.

4. **Domain separation** — NU, NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET shall remain technically, administratively, and procedurally separated unless an accredited CDS and authorised release process is used.
   - **References:** AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§3, 6, 7.6–7.7; ISO Annex A 8.22, 5.14; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS, PR.IR.

5. **Data minimisation** — Personal data, intelligence extracts, operational annexes, and mission data shall be limited to what is required for the authorised purpose.
   - **References:** GDPR Article 5(1)(c); ISO Annex A 5.12, 5.34; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS.

6. **Accountability and auditability** — Access, transfer, release, destruction, exception, and incident decisions shall be recorded at a level appropriate to classification and mission sensitivity.
   - **References:** GDPR Article 5(2), Article 30; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§19, 70–72, 90; ISO Annex A 5.33, 8.15; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.OV, DE.CM.

7. **Security by design and by default** — New systems, mission applications, exercises, and data-sharing processes shall be designed to enforce classification separation, least privilege, logging, retention, and privacy requirements from inception.
   - **References:** GDPR Article 25; AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§7.6–7.7; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clause 6.1; ISO Annex A 8.25–8.31; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.PO, ID.RA, PR.PS.

## 8. Mandatory Data Protection Requirements and Controls

### 8.1 Data inventory, ownership, and records of processing

1. Each domain shall maintain an inventory of major datasets, information stores, applications, databases, registries, shared drives, collaboration spaces, backups, and logs.
2. Every dataset shall have an identified information owner/originator, classification, releasability, retention rule, authorised user group, and approved storage location.
3. Personal-data processing shall be recorded in a Record of Processing Activities, including purpose, categories of data subjects, categories of personal data, recipients, retention, and security measures.
4. Data inventories and RoPAs shall be reviewed at least quarterly and after mission changes, exercises involving new partners, or new contractor onboarding.

**Control references:** GDPR Article 30; ISO Annex A 5.9, 5.12, 5.13, 5.33, 5.34; NIST CSF 2.0 ID.AM-01 through ID.AM-08, GV.OC, GV.OV; AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §7.6.

### 8.2 Classification, marking, caveats, and originator control

1. NATO classified information shall be marked and protected according to NATO classification and handling requirements.
2. Mission SECRET and national/coalition SECRET information shall carry the required national, coalition, caveat, and release markings.
3. ORCON or intelligence-originator restrictions shall be applied where required and shall not be bypassed by copying, extracting, summarising, translating, briefing, or transferring information.
4. Aggregated products shall be reviewed for classification elevation before release or posting.
5. Downgrading, declassification, extraction, reproduction, translation, or release shall occur only with originator authority or applicable classification-guide authority.

NATO’s classified-information directive explicitly addresses security classifications and markings, control and handling, storage, reproduction, dissemination and transfer, receipts and records, disposal/destruction, and security incidents. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6885/ac_35-d_2002_rev5.pdf))

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§1–6, 19, 36–38, 70–72; ISO Annex A 5.12, 5.13, 5.14, 5.33; NIST CSF 2.0 ID.AM, PR.DS.

### 8.3 Lawful, fair, and transparent personal-data processing

1. Personal data shall be processed only where a valid legal basis exists, including mission administration, employment/contract management, security vetting, access control, safety, medical support, legal obligation, public-interest task, vital interests, or contract performance as determined by the Legal Adviser/DPO.
2. Special category personal data shall not be processed unless an Article 9 GDPR condition or other applicable legal basis exists.
3. Personal-data collection notices shall be provided where operationally feasible and legally appropriate.
4. Personal data shall not be repurposed for unrelated monitoring, intelligence, disciplinary, or host-nation disclosure purposes without legal review.
5. Personnel security, counterintelligence, and force protection processing may be subject to national security or defence limitations; such limitations must be documented by Legal and Security.

**Control references:** GDPR Articles 5, 6, 9, 12–14, 23; ISO Annex A 5.31, 5.34; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.OC, GV.RM, GV.PO.

### 8.4 Need-to-know access control and identity management

1. Access to all systems shall be default-deny.
2. Access shall require:
   - verified identity;
   - appropriate clearance or authorisation;
   - mission role;
   - need-to-know;
   - completed security briefing;
   - accepted user responsibilities.
3. Smart-card / CAC-equivalent authentication shall be enforced per classification domain.
4. There shall be no cross-domain identity federation unless specifically accredited and authorised.
5. Privileged accounts shall be separately approved, time-bound where feasible, logged, and reviewed monthly.
6. Access reviews shall occur at least monthly for NS and MISSION SECRET, quarterly for NR and NU, and immediately upon role change, redeployment, clearance change, contractor offboarding, or insider-risk concern.
7. Shared accounts are prohibited except for approved break-glass accounts with compensating controls, dual approval, and after-action review.

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2000-REV8 §2; AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§6, 7.6–7.7; ISO Annex A 5.15, 5.16, 5.17, 5.18, 8.2, 8.3, 8.5; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01 through PR.AA-06.

### 8.5 Domain separation and cross-domain transfer

1. NU, NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET data shall be processed only on systems accredited for that classification and caveat.
2. Cross-domain transfer shall occur only through accredited NATO CDS or approved manual release procedures.
3. CDS rulesets shall be formally approved by the ISSM, system owner, release authority, and information owner/originator.
4. Bidirectional CDS channels shall receive enhanced monitoring, malware scanning, content filtering, release logging, and periodic ruleset review.
5. Manual transfer between domains requires documented release authority, media control, malware scanning, classification review, and recipient need-to-know.
6. Spillage shall be treated as a security incident and contained immediately.

NATO CIS security policy is mandatory and binding for CIS handling NATO classified information, and NATO CIS handling classified information must be subject to security accreditation. ([nsa.valisluureamet.ee](https://nsa.valisluureamet.ee/doc/regul/nato/Primary_Directive_on_CIS_Security_AC-35-D-2004-REV3.pdf))

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§3.1, 6.1–6.2, 7.6–7.7; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §46; ISO Annex A 5.14, 8.22, 8.23, 8.24; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS, PR.IR, DE.CM.

### 8.6 Cryptography, COMSEC, and key material

1. Data in transit between authorised endpoints shall use cryptography approved for the classification and mission context.
2. Data at rest on portable systems, removable media, backups, and mission repositories shall be encrypted using approved mechanisms unless prohibited by accreditation constraints.
3. NATO-approved COMSEC equipment shall be used for classified communications requiring COMSEC protection.
4. COMSEC key material shall be controlled through auditable chain of custody.
5. High-grade keying material shall be handled under two-person integrity.
6. COMSEC loss, suspected compromise, unauthorised access, or chain-of-custody discrepancy shall be reported immediately.

**Control references:** ISO Annex A 8.24, 5.10, 5.14; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS-01, PR.DS-02, PR.DS-10; national COMSEC authority directives. **Two-person integrity requirement is based on elicited internal operational requirement; precise public clause citation not provided.**

### 8.7 Storage, removable media, printing, and local copies

1. Classified data shall be stored only in accredited repositories, registries, approved collaboration spaces, or approved physical containers.
2. Local storage on endpoints shall be minimised and technically restricted where feasible.
3. Removable media shall be disabled by default and enabled only by exception for mission need.
4. Removable media shall be labelled, inventoried, encrypted where appropriate, malware scanned, and tied to a classification/domain.
5. Printing of classified material shall be limited to operational need, logged where feasible, marked correctly, and controlled under registry or local handling rules.
6. Personal devices shall not store, process, photograph, record, or transmit official data.
7. Screens, whiteboards, maps, and briefing materials in SCIF or classified spaces shall be cleared or covered before unauthorised entry.

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§19, 70–72; ISO Annex A 5.10, 7.7, 7.10, 7.14, 8.1, 8.10, 8.12; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS, PR.PS.

### 8.8 Data transfer, release, and disclosure

1. Release of operational, classified, intelligence, or coalition data shall require information-owner/originator approval and verification of recipient clearance, need-to-know, and releasability.
2. Release to non-NATO entities, host-nation entities, contractors, or coalition partners shall comply with applicable bilateral/multilateral agreements and caveats.
3. Transfers of personal data outside the EU/EEA or to international organisations shall be reviewed by the Legal Adviser/DPO for GDPR Chapter V applicability, NATO SOFA constraints, and mission necessity.
4. Release of certain operational orders requiring dual-key procedure shall not occur until both authorised officials approve release.
5. The preferred transfer method for NATO Classified Information is secure electronic means where available and accredited; physical transfer shall use authorised courier/hand-carriage procedures.

The NATO classified-information directive states that, where possible, secure electronic means are preferred over physical transfer and that CIS handling NATO Classified Information shall be subject to security accreditation. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6885/ac_35-d_2002_rev5.pdf))

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§36–38, 46–47; GDPR Chapter V; ISO Annex A 5.14, 5.31, 5.32; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS, GV.SC. **Dual-key operational-order requirement is based on elicited internal operational requirement; precise public clause citation not provided.**

### 8.9 Logging, monitoring, and audit

1. All domains shall generate security logs sufficient to support detection, investigation, accountability, and damage assessment.
2. Logs shall include, at minimum, authentication, privileged access, access-control changes, data-transfer events, CDS actions, malware detections, security-tool alerts, and administrative actions.
3. Logs shall remain within their classification domain unless transferred through accredited and authorised channels.
4. SOC monitoring shall operate continuously at domain boundaries.
5. Monitoring of personnel activity shall be proportionate, mission-justified, authorised, and reviewed for GDPR/host-nation implications where personal data is involved.
6. Audit logs shall be protected against unauthorised modification and retained according to domain-specific retention rules.

**Control references:** GDPR Article 32; AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§7.6–7.7; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §90; ISO Annex A 8.15, 8.16, 8.17, 5.28; NIST CSF 2.0 DE.CM, DE.AE, RS.AN.

### 8.10 Retention, archiving, downgrading, and destruction

1. Data shall be retained only as long as required for operational, legal, evidential, archival, intelligence, accountability, or contractual purposes.
2. Personal data shall be subject to documented retention periods and deletion or anonymisation where no longer required.
3. NATO Classified Information at end of life shall be reviewed for retention, archival storage, downgrading, declassification, or destruction.
4. Destruction of classified paper, media, and devices shall use approved methods that prevent reconstruction.
5. Destruction records shall be maintained for accountable classified material.
6. Backups shall follow the same classification and retention rules as source data.

NATO’s classified-information directive states that proper management of NATO Classified Information extends throughout its lifecycle, including disposal and destruction, and that hard copy no longer required shall be destroyed so it cannot be reconstructed. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6885/ac_35-d_2002_rev5.pdf))

**Control references:** GDPR Article 5(1)(e); AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§70–72; ISO Annex A 5.33, 8.10, 8.13; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.DS, RC.RP.

### 8.11 Data breach, spillage, and security incident response

1. Suspected data compromise, spillage, loss, unauthorised disclosure, unauthorised access, malware infection, insider misuse, COMSEC incident, or personal-data breach shall be reported immediately through the incident reporting chain.
2. The first responder shall preserve evidence, avoid further dissemination, and follow domain-specific containment instructions.
3. Classified spillage shall be contained by isolating affected systems, preserving logs, preventing further access, identifying scope, and notifying the ISSM/SOC/Security Office.
4. Reportable compromise of NATO Classified Information shall be escalated through the appropriate NATO security authority channels.
5. Personal-data breaches shall be assessed by the DPO/Legal Adviser for GDPR notification obligations. Where GDPR notification is required, the supervisory authority notification shall be made without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after awareness.
6. Incidents affecting host-nation cybersecurity obligations shall be reviewed for Romanian NIS2/NIS reporting applicability, subject to NATO SOFA and defence/national-security limitations.
7. Incident records, investigation reports, corrective actions, and lessons learned shall be retained according to classification and legal requirements.

GDPR Article 33 requires notification of a personal data breach to the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours after awareness unless unlikely to result in risk to individuals; NATO’s classified-information directive requires records of security breaches, investigations, and remedial/corrective actions to be kept for 10 years and available for inspection. ([gdpr-text.com](https://gdpr-text.com/read/article-33/?utm_source=openai))

**Control references:** GDPR Articles 33–34; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§84–93; ISO Annex A 5.24, 5.25, 5.26, 5.27, 5.28; NIST CSF 2.0 RS.MA, RS.AN, RS.CO, RS.MI, RC.RP, RC.CO; NIS2 Article 23 where applicable.

### 8.12 Privacy by design and DPIA

1. Any new or materially changed processing of civilian, contractor, visitor, biometric, health, access-control, monitoring, CCTV, personnel security, or host-nation personal data shall be reviewed for privacy impact.
2. A DPIA shall be completed where processing is likely to result in high risk to individuals, including systematic monitoring, sensitive data, biometric processing, or large-scale personnel analytics.
3. Privacy safeguards shall include minimisation, role-based access, segregation, retention limits, logging, encryption/pseudonymisation where feasible, and clear accountability.
4. DPIA outputs shall be classified or protected according to their content and shall not reveal sensitive security architecture in unclassified privacy records.

**Control references:** GDPR Articles 25 and 35; ISO Annex A 5.34, 8.11, 8.12; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RM, ID.RA, PR.DS.

### 8.13 Supplier, contractor, and supply-chain data protection

1. Suppliers and contractors handling detachment data shall be vetted according to security, contractual, and host-nation requirements.
2. Contracts shall specify classification handling, data protection, breach reporting, personnel screening, secure development, subcontractor control, audit rights, return/destruction of data, and incident cooperation.
3. NATO-approved equipment lists and approved cryptographic/COMSEC sources shall be used where required.
4. Host-nation contractors shall be screened before access to systems, sites, or data.
5. Supplier access shall be limited to defined support windows, monitored, logged, and removed when no longer required.
6. Software, firmware, patches, and hardware for classified or mission-critical systems shall be obtained through approved channels and validated before deployment.

NIS2 introduces risk-management and reporting obligations for more sectors and includes supply-chain-related national strategy requirements; NATO CIS security also requires lifecycle risk management for CIS and adequate resources for security throughout CIS stages. ([digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive))

**Control references:** NIS2 Article 21; ISO Annex A 5.19, 5.20, 5.21, 5.22, 5.23, 8.30; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC-01 through GV.SC-10; AC/35-D/2003 for classified project/industrial security where applicable.

### 8.14 Physical protection, SCIFs, and TEMPEST

1. NS and above activities shall be conducted only in authorised spaces, including SCIF rooms where required.
2. Physical access to classified spaces shall be controlled, logged, and limited to authorised personnel with need-to-know.
3. Visitors shall be escorted unless specifically authorised.
4. TEMPEST zoning and emanation controls shall be maintained for classified areas and equipment as directed by competent NATO/national authority.
5. Classified displays, briefings, printers, media, and waste shall be protected against observation, recording, removal, or reconstruction.

**Control references:** ISO Annex A 7.1–7.14; AC/35-D/2000-REV8 §2; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 §§19, 70–72. **SCIF and TEMPEST implementation requirements are based on elicited internal operational requirement; precise public clause citation not provided.**

### 8.15 Backup, resilience, and recovery

1. Each classification domain shall maintain backup and recovery arrangements appropriate to mission criticality and classification.
2. Backups shall be encrypted or physically protected consistent with source classification.
3. Restoration tests shall be conducted at least quarterly for mission-critical services and at least annually for all other managed services.
4. Recovery priorities shall align with operational command priorities and continuity plans.
5. Backup media shall not be moved between domains except through authorised transfer procedures.

GDPR Article 32 includes security of processing measures appropriate to risk, including confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, and restoration of access after incidents; NIS2 similarly imposes cybersecurity risk-management and reporting obligations for covered entities. ([gdpr-text.com](https://gdpr-text.com/en/read/article-32/?utm_source=openai))

**Control references:** GDPR Article 32; NIS2 Article 21; ISO Annex A 5.30, 8.13, 8.14; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.IR, RC.RP.

### 8.16 Training, exercises, and awareness

1. All personnel shall complete at least 40 hours of cybersecurity, data protection, classification handling, insider-threat, phishing, incident reporting, and role-specific security training annually.
2. Personnel with privileged, COMSEC, CDS, SOC, registry, release-authority, intelligence, or DPO/privacy roles shall complete additional role-specific training.
3. The detachment shall conduct six cybersecurity/data protection exercises per year, including at least:
   - one classified spillage exercise;
   - one personal-data breach tabletop;
   - one CDS misuse or misconfiguration scenario;
   - one insider-threat scenario;
   - one coalition information-release scenario;
   - one kinetic-cyber convergence scenario.
4. Two red-team engagements per year shall include data protection objectives and shall not intentionally move classified data across unauthorised domains unless explicitly approved in the rules of engagement and accreditation boundary.

NATO’s Cyber Defence Pledge commits to cyber education, training, exercising, information sharing, and ensuring the Alliance is cyber aware, cyber trained, cyber secure, and cyber enabled. ([ccdcoe.org](https://ccdcoe.org/uploads/2018/11/NATO-160708-CyberDefencePledge.pdf))

**Control references:** NATO Cyber Defence Pledge ¶4–5; ISO Annex A 6.3, 6.6, 8.29; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AT, ID.IM, GV.RR.

### 8.17 Continuous improvement and control testing

1. The ISSM shall maintain a control implementation statement mapping this policy to NATO, GDPR, NIS2/Romanian, NIST CSF 2.0, and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 references.
2. Control effectiveness shall be assessed through inspections, SOC metrics, vulnerability management, red-team findings, exercise after-action reports, audit logs, access reviews, and incident lessons learned.
3. Corrective actions shall be risk-ranked, assigned owners, and tracked to closure.
4. High-risk unresolved findings shall be reported to the Commander and relevant security authority.

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §§7.6–7.7; NIS2 Article 21(2)(f); ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 9.1, 9.2, 10.1, 10.2; ISO Annex A 5.35, 5.36, 8.8; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.OV, ID.IM, DE.CM.

## 9. Exceptions

1. Exceptions to this policy are prohibited unless formally approved.
2. Exceptions involving classified systems, domain separation, CDS, COMSEC, accreditation boundaries, or minimum NATO CIS security requirements require ISSM review, system owner concurrence, Security Office review, and approval by the competent security accreditation authority or designated NATO authority.
3. Exceptions involving personal data require DPO/Legal Adviser review before approval.
4. Exception requests shall include:
   - operational justification;
   - affected data, systems, domains, and users;
   - classification and personal-data impact;
   - risk assessment;
   - compensating controls;
   - expiry date;
   - rollback plan;
   - accountable owner.
5. No exception may authorise unauthorised disclosure, unauthorised declassification, unapproved COMSEC handling, unlawful personal-data processing, or bypass of originator control.
6. Emergency deviations required to protect life, mission, or force protection shall be reported to the ISSM and Commander as soon as practicable and regularised within 24 hours.

NATO CIS security permits exceptional deviations from minimum requirements only through authorised approval and formal risk assessment with mitigation; this policy adopts that approach for detachment exceptions. ([nsa.valisluureamet.ee](https://nsa.valisluureamet.ee/doc/regul/nato/Primary_Directive_on_CIS_Security_AC-35-D-2004-REV3.pdf))

**Control references:** AC/35-D/2004-REV3 §6.2; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 6.1 and 8.1; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RM, GV.OV.

## 10. Monitoring, Audit, and Review

1. The ISSM shall review this policy at least annually.
2. The ISSM shall review implementation evidence quarterly, including:
   - access-review completion;
   - privileged-account review;
   - CDS transfer logs;
   - spillage incidents;
   - personal-data breach register;
   - training completion;
   - unresolved audit findings;
   - supplier access records;
   - backup restoration tests;
   - vulnerability and patch status;
   - red-team and exercise corrective actions.
3. The DPO/Privacy Adviser shall review GDPR-relevant processing and DPIA status at least quarterly.
4. The Security Office shall review clearance, briefing, debrief, and visitor-control records at least quarterly.
5. The COMSEC Custodian shall reconcile COMSEC inventories according to national COMSEC authority requirements.
6. Policy review shall also occur after:
   - major incident or spillage;
   - change of framework nation;
   - major mission change;
   - new coalition partner or information-sharing agreement;
   - material change in NATO, Romanian, EU, or national law;
   - accreditation change;
   - significant red-team finding.

## 11. Compliance and Enforcement

1. Violation of this policy may result in suspension of system access, removal from duty, contractor remedies, disciplinary action, clearance review, legal action, or referral to national authorities.
2. Intentional unauthorised disclosure, negligent spillage, failure to report a breach, mishandling of COMSEC, or bypassing CDS controls shall be treated as serious misconduct.
3. Commanders and supervisors shall ensure personnel understand that operational urgency does not remove classification, COMSEC, privacy, or need-to-know obligations.
4. Contractors shall be held to contractual data protection and security obligations, including incident cooperation and return/destruction of data at termination.
