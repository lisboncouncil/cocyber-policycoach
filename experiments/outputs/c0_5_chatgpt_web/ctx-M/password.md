## 2. Purpose

This policy establishes mandatory requirements for the creation, use, storage, protection, reset, monitoring, and retirement of passwords, passphrases, PINs, activation secrets, recovery secrets, emergency credentials, service-account secrets, and privileged-account secrets used by the detachment.

The purpose is to reduce the likelihood and operational impact of credential compromise, insider misuse, credential reuse across classification domains, phishing, password spraying, offline password cracking, account recovery abuse, and spillage between NATO and mission classification domains.

This policy supports NATO cyber resilience expectations, including the NATO Cyber Defence Pledge commitment to strong and resilient cyber defences, deployable-network coverage, training, exercises, and information exchange. ([nato.int](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/official_texts_133177.htm))

---

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope

This policy applies to:

1. All military personnel, NATO civilian staff, cleared contractors, embedded national personnel, administrators, operators, analysts, developers, maintainers, and temporary users.
2. All user, privileged, emergency, break-glass, shared operational, application, database, service, automation, device-local, and infrastructure accounts.
3. All NATO and mission domains:  
   - **NU** — NATO UNCLASSIFIED  
   - **NR** — NATO RESTRICTED  
   - **NS** — NATO SECRET  
   - **MISSION SECRET**
4. All endpoint, server, network, security, cryptographic-support, monitoring, identity, directory, remote-access, virtualisation, storage, backup, cross-domain, and management systems.
5. Local activation secrets for smart cards, cryptographic tokens, hardware security modules, privileged-access workstations, removable encrypted media, and approved mobile or tactical devices.
6. Passwords or secrets used by vendors, host-nation contractors, coalition partners, and support organisations when accessing detachment systems.

### 3.2 Out of scope

The following are governed by separate COMSEC, classification, or cryptographic-key policy and are not treated as ordinary passwords:

1. NATO or national COMSEC keying material.
2. Cryptographic private keys, key-encryption keys, traffic-encryption keys, mission key variables, and HSM master keys.
3. CDS guard rules, release rules, and filtering rules.
4. ORCON release decisions and dual-key operational-release decisions.

Where a PIN, passphrase, or activation secret protects a cryptographic authenticator, this policy applies to that activation secret, but not to the cryptographic key itself. NIST SP 800-63B distinguishes centrally verified passwords from local activation secrets used to unlock authenticators. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

### 5.1 Detachment Commander

1. Approves this policy and material exceptions.
2. Ensures enforcement across national contingents, contractors, and mission partners operating on detachment systems.
3. Accepts residual operational risk for time-limited exceptions affecting mission delivery.

**References:** NATO Cyber Defence Pledge paragraphs 4–5 on cyber-aware, cyber-secure, adequately resourced defence organisations; NIST CSF 2.0 Govern / Protect concepts. ([nato.int](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/official_texts_133177.htm))

### 5.2 ISSM / J6

1. Owns this policy and maintains domain-specific implementation standards.
2. Defines password, passphrase, PIN, vaulting, reset, and lockout baselines per domain.
3. Ensures all password controls are technically enforced where possible.
4. Reviews exceptions, red-team findings, SOC trends, and compliance reports.
5. Maintains the authoritative mapping between this policy and NATO-controlled directives, including C-M(2002)49, AC/35-D/1015, AC/35-D/1029, and AC/322-D(2017)0009. NATO Archives metadata identifies C-M(2002)49 as NATO Security Policy; precise control paragraphs are to be maintained internally from controlled copies. ([archives.nato.int](https://archives.nato.int/security-within-the-north-atlantic-treaty-organisation-nato-corrigendum-amendment-1?utm_source=openai))

### 5.3 Domain System Owners

1. Implement this policy on NU, NR, NS, MISSION SECRET, and management systems.
2. Ensure password settings are applied through domain policy, configuration baselines, identity-provider policy, vault policy, or privileged-access tooling.
3. Maintain evidence of implementation and exception status.
4. Ensure no authentication data is replicated to a lower-classification domain.

**References:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01, PR.AA-03, PR.AA-05; PR.PS-01 configuration management. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

### 5.4 SOC / Theatre-Level Monitoring

1. Monitor password-related events 24/7, including failed logons, lockouts, impossible travel, spray patterns, privileged logons, password resets, break-glass use, service-account failures, and authentication from unauthorised hosts.
2. Escalate suspected compromise through the documented incident response plan aligned with NATO CIRC / NCIRC processes.
3. Coordinate notifications to NCSC and host-nation CERT where required and legally authorised.

**References:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA and detection/response functions; GDPR Article 32 for resilience and regular testing of security measures. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

### 5.5 Personnel, Staff, and Contractors

All users must:

1. Use only authorised credentials issued for their assigned domain and role.
2. Never reuse passwords across classification domains or with personal, national, coalition, or commercial systems.
3. Never share passwords, PINs, activation secrets, recovery codes, or vault entries unless a formally approved dual-control operational process requires it.
4. Report suspected credential disclosure, shoulder-surfing, phishing, token loss, smart-card loss, or account misuse immediately.
5. Complete required annual cyber training, including password hygiene and phishing-resistant authentication.

**References:** NIST SP 800-63B authenticator compromise and invalidation requirements; NATO Cyber Defence Pledge training commitment. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

### 5.6 Contractors and Vendors

1. Contractor and vendor accounts must be individually attributable unless a formally approved service process requires non-personal credentials.
2. Contractor credentials must expire automatically at contract end, clearance withdrawal, mission departure, or loss of need-to-know.
3. Vendor access to classified or mission systems must use domain-approved MFA and must not rely on vendor-managed passwords unless explicitly accredited.
4. Contractor personal data used in authentication and logging must be processed in accordance with GDPR and host-nation legal constraints, subject to SOFA limitations.

**References:** GDPR Article 32; NIS2 Article 21(2)(i) access control and human resources security; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-05. ([dataprivacymanager.net](https://dataprivacymanager.net/gdpr/art-32-security-of-processing/))

### 6.3 Password composition

1. Systems must allow long passphrases and must not require arbitrary mixtures of uppercase, lowercase, digits, and symbols where the system can enforce length, blocklisting, MFA, and rate limiting.
2. Systems should permit spaces and printable characters.
3. Systems should support at least 64 characters.
4. Unicode may be accepted where the domain identity system supports consistent normalisation, logging, and help-desk handling without creating operational ambiguity.
5. Passwords must be verified in full and must not be silently truncated.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires verifiers to support long passwords, not impose additional composition rules, accept spaces and printable characters, and verify the full submitted password. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.4 Password blocklisting

All password changes and password creation events must be checked against a domain-approved blocklist containing, at minimum:

1. Common passwords.
2. Previously breached passwords available to the detachment through approved threat-intelligence sources.
3. Dictionary words likely to be guessed.
4. Detachment, operation, unit, host-nation, NATO, domain, exercise, callsign, system, username, role, and seasonal terms.
5. Keyboard walks and predictable patterns.
6. Passwords found during internal red-team, purple-team, SOC, or incident-response activity.

If a proposed password appears on the blocklist, the system must reject it and require a different password. The helpdesk may explain that the password is too common, compromised, or contextually guessable, but must not disclose the full contents of the blocklist.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires comparison of new or changed passwords against a blocklist of known commonly used, expected, or compromised passwords and requires rejection where found. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.5 Password reuse

1. A password must be unique to the user, account, domain, and system.
2. Password reuse across NU, NR, NS, MISSION SECRET, national systems, coalition systems, contractor systems, personal systems, or internet services is prohibited.
3. Where technically feasible, password history controls must prevent reuse of the last **10** passwords for the same account. The value “10” is a local hardening parameter and is not externally prescribed.
4. Privileged, emergency, break-glass, service, automation, and application credentials must not be reused between systems or domains.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01 and PR.AA-05 require organisational management of credentials and least-privilege authorisations; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.17 addresses authentication information. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

### 6.6 Password expiration and forced change

1. User passwords must **not** be changed on a fixed periodic schedule solely because time has elapsed.
2. A password or activation secret must be changed, reset, invalidated, or the associated authenticator suspended when there is evidence or reasonable suspicion of compromise, including:
   - phishing;
   - password disclosure;
   - malware or keylogger exposure;
   - lost or stolen authenticator;
   - password hash disclosure;
   - suspicious login activity;
   - failed red-team or SOC credential-harvesting exercise;
   - role reassignment involving privileged access;
   - departure from mission or loss of need-to-know;
   - account recovery event;
   - break-glass use.
3. Service-account, application, automation, and emergency credentials must be rotated after use, administrator turnover, suspected exposure, system owner change, vault compromise, or maintenance by external vendors.
4. If a national system connected to the mission requires periodic password rotation by national rule, that rule may be followed on that national system, but the account must still comply with this policy’s domain-separation, MFA, and blocklisting requirements.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B states that verifiers must not require periodic password changes, but must force change when there is evidence of authenticator compromise; it also requires compromised authenticators to be suspended, invalidated, or destroyed promptly. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

### 6.7 Failed logon limits, throttling, and lockout

1. All centrally verified password systems must implement rate limiting.
2. Default lockout thresholds:
   - **Standard user accounts:** lock or progressive delay after **10** consecutive failed attempts within 15 minutes.
   - **Privileged accounts:** lock or SOC alert after **5** consecutive failed attempts within 15 minutes.
   - **Service accounts:** immediate SOC alert after **3** failed attempts, unless explicitly baselined for known application behaviour.
   - **Activation PINs for smart cards or tokens:** no more than **10** consecutive failed activation attempts before the authenticator is disabled or requires authorised reset.
3. The SOC must alert on password spraying, distributed guessing, repeated lockouts, failures across multiple accounts, failures against disabled accounts, and failures involving privileged groups.
4. Account unlock must require identity verification appropriate to the domain and may require supervisor, security officer, or two-person approval for privileged and classified-domain accounts.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires rate limiting for password verification and states that failed authentication attempts must be limited; NIST sets 100 attempts as an upper bound and permits agencies to impose lower limits. Activation secrets are limited to no more than 10 consecutive failed attempts. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.8 Password storage

1. Passwords must never be stored in plaintext.
2. Passwords must not be stored using reversible encryption unless explicitly required by an accredited legacy system and approved by exception.
3. Passwords must be salted and hashed using an approved password hashing scheme with a cost factor appropriate to the domain and system performance.
4. Salt values must be unique per stored password and at least 32 bits.
5. Where technically feasible, password verifiers should use an additional keyed hash or encryption step, with the key stored separately in a hardware-protected area such as an HSM, TPM, TEE, or domain-approved equivalent.
6. Password-hash databases, vault exports, reset-token stores, and credential backups must be protected at the classification level of the highest account or domain represented.
7. Password hashes from NS or MISSION SECRET systems must not be copied to NU or NR for troubleshooting, analytics, testing, or vendor support.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires password verifiers to store passwords in a form resistant to offline attacks, using salted password hashing, appropriate cost factors, and protected channels; it also recommends an additional keyed operation with the key stored separately in hardware-protected storage. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.9 Password transmission and entry

1. Passwords, activation secrets, recovery codes, and reset tokens must be transmitted only over authenticated protected channels.
2. Passwords must never be sent by email, chat, ticketing system, SMS, radio, unsecured voice, removable media, or printed document unless specifically part of an approved emergency or recovery process.
3. Initial passwords, if unavoidable, must be:
   - randomly generated;
   - single use;
   - time limited;
   - delivered through an approved out-of-band or in-person process;
   - changed immediately at first use.
4. Password entry must be masked on screens except where a user deliberately selects temporary reveal in a controlled environment.
5. Passwords must not be captured in logs, crash dumps, screenshots, screen recordings, command histories, scripts, debug output, telemetry, or helpdesk tickets.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires approved encryption and authenticated protected channels when requesting passwords and other look-up secrets. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.10 Privileged-account passwords

1. Privileged accounts must be separate from standard user accounts.
2. Privileged access must require phishing-resistant MFA or domain PKI smart-card authentication.
3. Privileged passwords must be:
   - unique per account and domain;
   - randomly generated where possible;
   - vaulted in the relevant classification domain;
   - checked out only for approved tasks;
   - time limited;
   - automatically rotated after checkout where technically supported;
   - monitored by the SOC.
4. Shared privileged passwords are prohibited except for approved emergency, appliance, local administrator, or break-glass scenarios.
5. Privileged accounts must not be used for email, web browsing, document handling, coalition chat, or routine administrative work.
6. Privileged password use on NS, MISSION SECRET, CDS, SOC, identity, COMSEC-support, and security-tooling systems must generate high-priority SOC telemetry.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-03 requires authentication of users, services, and hardware; PR.AA-05 requires least privilege and separation of duties. NIST SP 800-63B AAL2/AAL3 supports MFA and phishing-resistant authentication for higher assurance. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

---

### 6.11 Service, application, automation, and device-local credentials

1. Service and automation accounts must use managed identity, certificate-based authentication, Kerberos, SSH keys, or domain-approved cryptographic authentication wherever available.
2. Where passwords are unavoidable, they must be:
   - randomly generated;
   - at least 32 characters;
   - non-interactive unless explicitly approved;
   - restricted to the minimum required systems, commands, ports, and data;
   - stored only in an approved domain vault;
   - unavailable to ordinary users;
   - excluded from interactive logon unless operationally required;
   - rotated after suspected exposure, maintainer departure, vendor access, or system transfer.
3. Hard-coded passwords in scripts, source code, configuration files, container images, golden images, deployment templates, and documentation are prohibited.
4. CI/CD, orchestration, backup, monitoring, and security-tool credentials must be scoped per domain and per mission system.
5. Vendor default passwords must be changed before connection to any detachment network, including NU staging networks.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01 and PR.AA-03 cover management and authentication of identities and credentials for users, services, and hardware; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.17 and 8.5 cover authentication information and secure authentication. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

---

### 6.12 Break-glass and emergency credentials

1. Break-glass credentials must exist only where required for mission assurance, disaster recovery, loss of identity infrastructure, or operational continuity.
2. Each break-glass credential must:
   - be approved by the ISSM and system owner;
   - be randomly generated;
   - be at least 24 characters;
   - be stored in an approved vault or sealed physical container at the appropriate classification level;
   - require two-person access for NS, MISSION SECRET, CDS, identity, COMSEC-support, and SOC systems;
   - be logged and reconciled after use;
   - be rotated immediately after use;
   - trigger SOC and commander-designated notification.
3. Break-glass credentials must be tested during exercises without exposing the live secret; tests must validate process, availability, and logging.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-05 least privilege and separation of duties; GDPR Article 32 requires resilience and regular testing for systems processing personal data; NATO Cyber Defence Pledge supports exercises and cyber resilience. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

---

### 6.13 Account recovery and password reset

1. Password reset and account recovery must be performed only by authorised service desk, identity administrators, or domain security administrators.
2. The identity of the requester must be verified using domain-appropriate evidence. For NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET, verification must include at least one trusted channel independent of the possibly compromised account.
3. Knowledge-based authentication questions, such as “mother’s maiden name” or “first pet,” are prohibited.
4. Recovery codes must be single-use, time-limited, randomly generated, and protected at the classification level of the account.
5. Account recovery for privileged, NS, MISSION SECRET, CDS, SOC, or identity-administration accounts requires two-person approval.
6. After account recovery, the user must set a new password or activation secret, and the event must be logged and reviewed.
7. Account recovery must trigger notification to the user or command-designated notification path unless doing so would compromise a counterintelligence or incident-response investigation.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B prohibits password hints and knowledge-based authentication for password selection, defines account recovery requirements, and requires account recovery notification. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.14 Smart-card and cryptographic-token activation secrets

1. Smart-card, token, and hardware-authenticator PINs or activation secrets must be treated as sensitive authentication data.
2. Users must not write activation PINs on cards, token sleeves, workstations, keyboards, notebooks, badges, or inside SCIF spaces.
3. Activation secrets must be known only to the assigned user unless an approved dual-control process applies.
4. Token reset must require identity verification and must be logged.
5. For high-assurance systems, activation-secret verification must occur in a hardware-protected environment where the authenticator supports it.
6. Syncable authenticators must not be used for AAL3-equivalent classified-domain authentication unless specifically accredited for the domain and mission.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires activation secrets to remain within the authenticator and endpoint, recommends at least six characters, requires no more than 10 failed activation attempts, and requires AAL3 authenticators to use hardware-protected, non-exportable keys. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.15 Session reauthentication and inactivity

1. Session controls must be implemented independently of password policy and must not rely on password caching.
2. Default session limits:
   - **NU standard user:** maximum 24-hour reauthentication; 60-minute inactivity timeout.
   - **NR standard user:** maximum 12-hour reauthentication; 30-minute inactivity timeout.
   - **NS / MISSION SECRET standard user:** maximum 12-hour reauthentication; 15-minute inactivity timeout.
   - **Privileged sessions:** maximum 8-hour reauthentication; 15-minute inactivity timeout; shorter where technically feasible.
   - **CDS, SOC, identity, COMSEC-support administration:** maximum 8-hour reauthentication; 10-minute inactivity timeout where operationally tolerable.
3. Systems must terminate sessions on logout and must not use “remember me” or persistent browser sessions for classified-domain access.
4. Reauthentication must use the same assurance level required for the system, except where NIST-permitted AAL2 reauthentication conditions are explicitly implemented and approved.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B defines overall and inactivity timeouts and recommends AAL2 reauthentication within 24 hours and 1-hour inactivity, while AAL3 requires no more than 12 hours and recommends 15-minute inactivity. The stricter values above are local mission-risk parameters. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

### 6.16 Password managers and vaulting

1. Users may use only organisation-approved password managers or vaults operating within the relevant classification domain.
2. Password vault data must not cross classification-domain boundaries.
3. A NU vault must not contain NR, NS, MISSION SECRET, national SECRET, coalition SECRET, COMSEC-support, or privileged classified-domain credentials.
4. Privileged and service-account vaulting must be performed using an approved enterprise privileged-access-management or secrets-management capability.
5. Vault master credentials must require MFA or smart-card authentication.
6. Vault access must be logged and monitored.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01 and PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.17, 5.18, 8.5. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

---

### 6.17 Prohibited practices

The following are prohibited:

1. Reusing a password across domains or with personal systems.
2. Sharing passwords or PINs outside an approved dual-control process.
3. Sending passwords by email, chat, SMS, radio, or ticket.
4. Writing passwords on paper stored near the workstation, token, badge, or SCIF console.
5. Storing passwords in browser password stores unless explicitly approved for the domain.
6. Storing passwords in spreadsheets, documents, scripts, configuration files, source repositories, deployment templates, or wiki pages.
7. Using default vendor passwords.
8. Using password hints.
9. Using knowledge-based authentication questions.
10. Using personal password managers for official credentials.
11. Copying password hashes, vault exports, reset tokens, or authentication logs to a lower-classification domain.
12. Allowing contractors to retain credentials after contract end, clearance loss, or mission departure.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B prohibits hints and knowledge-based authentication for passwords, requires protected channels and secure storage, and requires invalidation of compromised authenticators. Domain-transfer prohibitions are local classification controls; exact NATO clause unsupported in this draft. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

---

## 7. Monitoring, Logging, and Alerting

### 7.1 Events to be logged

Each domain must log, at minimum:

1. Successful and failed authentication.
2. Password creation, reset, change, rejection, and administrative override.
3. Account lockout and unlock.
4. Privileged credential checkout and check-in.
5. Service-account authentication failures.
6. Break-glass credential access.
7. Smart-card or token PIN reset.
8. Authenticator binding, rebinding, suspension, invalidation, and recovery.
9. Authentication from unauthorised endpoints or unexpected network zones.
10. Authentication attempts across CDS, boundary, management, or remote-access systems.

Logs must not contain plaintext passwords, activation secrets, recovery codes, or complete reset tokens.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires lifecycle records for authenticators and recognises account-event notifications; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA supports management of identities and credentials. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))

### 7.2 SOC use cases

The SOC must maintain detections for:

1. Password spraying.
2. Credential stuffing.
3. Repeated failed attempts against privileged accounts.
4. Successful login after repeated failures.
5. Login from unexpected geography, enclave, host, or tactical network.
6. Impossible or improbable travel.
7. Authentication outside duty pattern or mission role.
8. Use of disabled, expired, or dormant accounts.
9. Sudden service-account failure spikes.
10. Break-glass use.
11. Cross-domain credential artefact movement.
12. Authentication attempts during kinetic-cyber contingency periods.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-03 and PR.AA-05; GDPR Article 32 where personal-data processing systems are involved. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

### 7.3 Privacy and minimisation

Authentication logs involving civilians, contractors, host-nation personnel, or EU personal data must be limited to what is required for security, legal accountability, mission assurance, and incident response. Access to such logs must be restricted, monitored, and retained only according to approved records schedules, NATO requirements, host-nation law, SOFA constraints, and operational necessity.

**Source / audit reference:** GDPR Article 32 requires risk-appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data and ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, and regular testing. ([dataprivacymanager.net](https://dataprivacymanager.net/gdpr/art-32-security-of-processing/))

---

## 8. Training and Awareness

1. All personnel must receive annual password and credential-security training as part of the 40-hour annual training requirement.
2. Training must include:
   - domain separation;
   - no password reuse;
   - phishing and credential harvesting;
   - smart-card and token PIN protection;
   - reporting lost tokens or suspected password exposure;
   - contractor and vendor credential obligations;
   - classified-domain spillage risks;
   - password-manager and vault use;
   - break-glass procedures for authorised personnel.
3. Administrators, helpdesk personnel, SOC analysts, and identity administrators must receive role-specific training on reset, recovery, vault, lockout, privileged-access, and evidence-handling procedures.
4. Password and credential compromise scenarios must be included in at least two annual cyber exercises and one annual red-team or purple-team engagement.

**Source / audit reference:** NATO Cyber Defence Pledge calls for cyber awareness, training, exercises, and adequate cyber capability; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AT-01 and PR.AT-02 cover cybersecurity awareness and role-based training. ([nato.int](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/official_texts_133177.htm))

---

## 9. Exceptions

### 9.1 Exception criteria

Exceptions may be granted only when:

1. The system cannot technically meet the requirement.
2. Mission impact of non-operation is greater than the credential risk.
3. Compensating controls are documented.
4. The exception is time-bound.
5. The exception does not permit uncontrolled cross-domain credential movement.
6. The exception is approved by the ISSM and relevant system owner; commander approval is required for material risk.

### 9.2 Maximum exception duration

| Exception type | Maximum duration |
|---|---:|
| NU legacy password setting | 180 days |
| NR legacy password setting | 90 days |
| NS / MISSION SECRET legacy password setting | 30 days unless commander-approved |
| Privileged or break-glass exception | 30 days |
| CDS-related credential exception | No standing exception; case-by-case accreditation only |

### 9.3 Required compensating controls

Compensating controls may include:

1. MFA or smart-card enforcement.
2. Network isolation.
3. Privileged-access workstation requirement.
4. Increased SOC monitoring.
5. Reduced session timeout.
6. Reduced failed-attempt threshold.
7. Manual dual-control approval.
8. Temporary account enablement only during approved maintenance window.
9. Immediate password rotation after use.
10. Additional commander or security-officer notification.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-05; GDPR Article 32 risk-based technical and organisational measures; NIS2 Article 21 risk-management measures including access control and MFA where appropriate. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

---

## 10. Compliance, Measurement, and Review

### 10.1 Compliance metrics

The ISSM must maintain monthly metrics for each domain:

1. Percentage of accounts using MFA / smart-card authentication.
2. Number of password-only exceptions.
3. Number of accounts with expired mission need.
4. Number of privileged accounts not vaulted.
5. Number of service accounts with interactive logon.
6. Password-reset volume by domain and account type.
7. Lockout and failed-authentication trends.
8. Break-glass tests and actual uses.
9. Blocklisted password rejection counts.
10. Red-team findings related to credentials.
11. Time to disable accounts after departure, clearance loss, or role change.
12. Time to rotate exposed or used emergency credentials.

### 10.2 Review triggers

This policy must be reviewed:

1. Annually.
2. After any credential-related incident.
3. After a red-team compromise path involving passwords, hashes, tokens, vaults, or reset processes.
4. After changes to NATO security directives.
5. After changes to Romanian cybersecurity law, NIS2 transposition, GDPR interpretation, or SOFA implementation affecting authentication or logging.
6. After deployment of new identity, PKI, PAM, vault, CDS, or remote-access capability.
7. Before major multinational interoperability exercises.

**Source / audit reference:** GDPR Article 32 requires regular testing, assessing, and evaluating of technical and organisational measures; NATO Cyber Defence Pledge supports continuous adaptation, training, exercises, and adequate resources. ([dataprivacymanager.net](https://dataprivacymanager.net/gdpr/art-32-security-of-processing/))

---

## 11. Enforcement

1. Violations may result in account suspension, removal of privileged access, disciplinary action, contract action, security investigation, counterintelligence referral, or national-chain reporting.
2. Suspected intentional credential misuse, credential sharing on classified systems, cross-domain password reuse causing spillage risk, or unauthorised disclosure of privileged credentials must be treated as a security incident.
3. Contractors who fail to comply may have system access suspended pending review by the contracting authority, security officer, and ISSM.
4. Any credential compromise affecting NATO, national, coalition, or host-nation systems must be reported through the incident-response chain.

**Source / audit reference:** NIST SP 800-63B requires prompt suspension, invalidation, or destruction of compromised authenticators; NATO Cyber Defence Pledge supports effective response to cyber threats. ([pages.nist.gov](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html))
