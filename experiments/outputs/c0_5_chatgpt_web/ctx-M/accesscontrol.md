# Access Control Policy  
**Organisation:** NATO Multinational Military Detachment — Romania  
**Document marking:** NATO UNCLASSIFIED / Releasable to authorised detachment personnel and approved auditors  
**Policy owner:** Information Systems Security Manager (ISSM), J6 Chain  
**Approving authority:** Detachment Commander, in coordination with SHAPE operational control and applicable National Security / CIS Security Authorities  
**Effective date:** 07 May 2026  
**Review cycle:** At least annually; additionally after major mission, framework-nation, system-accreditation, threat, incident, or legal/regulatory change  
**Supersedes:** New policy  
**Language:** English  

---

## 1. Purpose

This Access Control Policy establishes mandatory controls for granting, using, reviewing, monitoring, suspending, and revoking logical and related physical access to information, systems, services, networks, cryptographic assets, and classified mission spaces used by the detachment in Romania.

The policy is designed for a medium multinational military detachment operating NATO, national, and coalition classified environments, including NU, NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET domains. It implements the NATO principles of common protection for classified information, need-to-know, personnel clearance, originator control, CIS security, and security accreditation. NATO Security Policy C-M(2002)49-REV1 establishes basic principles and minimum standards for protecting NATO Classified Information and applies to NATO Nations and NATO Civil and Military bodies; it also requires classified information to be safeguarded against loss of confidentiality, integrity, and availability. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

This policy also aligns with NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 / 27002:2022, GDPR, applicable Romanian cybersecurity and data-protection requirements, NATO SOFA constraints, bilateral and multilateral information-sharing agreements, and the NATO Cyber Defence Pledge. NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 provides a flexible, risk-based catalogue of security and privacy controls addressing hostile attacks, human error, insider activity, foreign intelligence threats, and privacy risks. ([csrc.nist.gov](https://csrc.nist.gov/Pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/Final))

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

1. **Personnel**
   - Military active personnel.
   - NATO civilian staff.
   - Cleared contractors.
   - Framework-nation, host-nation, liaison, visiting, and coalition personnel when accessing detachment systems or information.
   - Privileged administrators, SOC personnel, COMSEC custodians, intelligence users, and cross-domain operators.

2. **Systems and information domains**
   - NATO UNCLASSIFIED / NU.
   - NATO RESTRICTED / NR.
   - NATO SECRET / NS.
   - MISSION SECRET national or coalition systems.
   - Supporting identity, PKI, directory, logging, monitoring, endpoint, network, COMSEC, CDS, and administrative systems.
   - Classified and unclassified workstations, servers, network devices, sensors, mobile or deployable CIS assets, removable media repositories, and approved maintenance interfaces.

3. **Access types**
   - Logical user access.
   - Privileged and administrative access.
   - Service, device, application, and machine-to-machine access.
   - Remote and maintenance access.
   - Cross-domain transfer access.
   - COMSEC-related access.
   - Physical access to areas where logical access to classified systems is possible, including SCIF rooms and TEMPEST-zoned spaces.

4. **Exclusions**
   - Offensive cyber operations authorities, rules of engagement, and intelligence collection authorities are outside this policy except where access to supporting systems is concerned.
   - National-only systems not connected to detachment networks remain under the owning nation’s policy, unless they process, store, transmit, or exchange NATO or coalition information through detachment infrastructure.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Detachment Commander

The Detachment Commander shall:

- Approve this policy and ensure command enforcement.
- Ensure access-control decisions support mission command, NATO operational control, host-nation obligations, and coalition information-sharing agreements.
- Approve mission-critical access exceptions and emergency access after ISSM risk advice.
- Ensure commanders and staff enforce need-to-know and separation of duties.

**Authority / references:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure B, Basic Principles and Responsibilities; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RR / GV.PO; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 5.1, 5.2, 5.3.

### 3.2 ISSM, J6

The ISSM shall:

- Own this policy and maintain access-control standards, procedures, and evidence.
- Approve access-control models for all domains.
- Ensure access controls are implemented in accreditation packages and system security plans.
- Coordinate with theatre SOC, NCIRC / NATO CIRC processes, host-nation CERT interfaces, NCSC, national security authorities, COMSEC authorities, and data-protection staff.
- Ensure privileged access, emergency access, and cross-domain access are logged, reviewed, and auditable.

**Authority / references:** NATO CIS Security Enclosure F requires CIS handling NATO classified information to undergo security accreditation and to apply a consistent set of security measures. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AC-1, AC-2, AC-3, AC-6, IA-2, AU-6; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.15, 5.16, 5.18, 8.2, 8.3, 8.5.

### 3.3 System Owners and Information Owners

System and information owners shall:

- Define authorised user communities, mission roles, data markings, release restrictions, and access conditions.
- Approve or deny access requests before provisioning.
- Confirm classification, caveat, ORCON, releasability, and mission need before information access is granted.
- Review access at the required frequency.
- Immediately notify J6 account managers and the ISSM of role changes, redeployment, termination, clearance changes, compromise indicators, or changes in need-to-know.

**Authority / references:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 requires classified information dissemination to be based on need-to-know and subject to originator control. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AC-2, AC-3, AC-4, AC-6; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.12, 5.13, 5.15, 5.18.

### 3.4 Account Managers / Directory Administrators

Account managers shall:

- Provision, modify, disable, and remove accounts only through the approved access request workflow.
- Maintain separate account stores per classification domain.
- Prevent unauthorised shared, dormant, duplicate, orphaned, emergency, and unauthorised service accounts.
- Reconcile account state against personnel, clearance, and mission-role data.
- Disable accounts immediately upon termination, loss of clearance, suspected compromise, or removal from mission role.

**Authority / references:** NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AC-2 requires account creation, modification, disabling, monitoring, review, and alignment with termination and transfer processes. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf)) ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.16, 5.18, 8.2.

### 3.5 Security Officer / Personnel Security Authority

The Security Officer shall:

- Verify clearance, NATO security briefing, written acknowledgement, caveat eligibility, and need-to-know before classified access is authorised.
- Coordinate aftercare, insider-threat referrals, and clearance suspension notifications.
- Support investigations of unauthorised access, attempted access, and spillage.

**Authority / references:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 requires appropriately cleared individuals for NATO CONFIDENTIAL and above, need-to-know, briefings, written acknowledgement, and ongoing personnel-security aftercare for insider threat management. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 PS-3, PS-4, PS-5, AC-2.

### 3.6 COMSEC Custodian

The COMSEC Custodian shall:

- Control access to COMSEC equipment, keying material, fill devices, crypto accounts, and COMSEC records.
- Enforce two-person integrity for high-grade keying material.
- Maintain end-to-end chain-of-custody records.
- Immediately report COMSEC access anomalies, loss, suspected compromise, or unauthorised handling.

**Authority / references:** NATO CIS Security Enclosure F identifies cryptographic products, mechanisms, COMSEC materials, directory services, and environmental facilities as supporting system services and resources for achieving CIS security objectives. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AC-3(2), AC-5, AC-6, SC-12, SC-13; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 8.24. Two-person integrity for this detachment’s high-grade COMSEC is mission-specific and is treated as mandatory under the provided organisational profile; no public clause citation is provided in this draft.

### 3.7 SOC / Monitoring Team

The theatre SOC and local security monitoring staff shall:

- Monitor authentication, authorisation, privileged activity, cross-domain transfer, boundary traffic, and abnormal access behaviour.
- Receive domain-boundary sensor logs from all domains.
- Escalate suspected unauthorised access, account compromise, insider activity, and spillage in accordance with the IR plan and NATO CIRC / NCIRC processes.
- Retain logs according to the accredited system security plan and legal requirements.

**Authority / references:** NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AU-2, AU-6, AU-12, SI-4, AC-2, AC-17; NATO Cyber Defence Pledge commits Allies to cyber-aware, cyber-trained, cyber-secure, and cyber-enabled forces, and to improving cyber-threat understanding and information sharing. ([nato.int](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/official_texts_133177.htm))

### 3.8 Users

All users shall:

- Access only systems and information for which they are authorised, cleared, briefed, and have current need-to-know.
- Protect smart cards, PINs, certificates, passwords, tokens, and other authenticators.
- Never share accounts, authenticators, sessions, credentials, removable media, or classified information.
- Lock or log out of sessions before leaving workstations.
- Immediately report lost credentials, suspected compromise, unauthorised access, spillage, social-engineering attempts, HUMINT approaches, or anomalous system behaviour.
- Comply with classification, marking, caveat, ORCON, release, and cross-domain transfer procedures.

**Authority / references:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 requires dissemination based on need-to-know and briefed personnel; it also requires suspected security breaches and compromises to be reported immediately. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AC-2, IA-5, AT-2, IR-6.

## 4. Access Control Principles

Access control in this detachment shall be governed by the following principles:

1. **Need-to-know and mission need.** Clearance alone never grants access. A positive determination of mission need is required before access is approved. NATO Security Policy defines need-to-know as a positive determination that a recipient requires access, knowledge, or possession of information to perform official tasks or services. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))  
   **References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure B; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 para. 44; NIST AC-3, AC-6; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.15, 5.18.

2. **Default deny.** Access shall be denied unless explicitly approved by authorised owners and technically enforced.

3. **Least privilege.** Users, services, processes, administrators, devices, and applications shall receive only the privileges necessary for assigned mission tasks. NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AC-6 defines least privilege as allowing only authorised access necessary to accomplish assigned organisational tasks. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))  
   **References:** NIST AC-6; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.15, 5.18, 8.2.

4. **Separation of duties.** Incompatible duties shall be separated, especially access approval, access administration, audit review, CDS release, COMSEC handling, operational order release, and security monitoring. NIST AC-5 requires identification and documentation of duties requiring separation and definition of access authorisations to support that separation. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))  
   **References:** NIST AC-5; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.3, 5.15, 5.18.

5. **Strong identity and authentication per domain.** Each classification domain shall maintain its own PKI, identity store, smart-card authentication, access roles, audit trail, and revocation process. No cross-domain identity federation is permitted unless separately accredited and authorised.

6. **Strict classification-domain separation.** NU, NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET domains shall remain logically and physically separated according to their accreditation. Information flow between domains shall occur only through accredited CDS, approved release procedures, and recorded human authorisation where required.

7. **Originator control and releasability.** Information subject to ORCON, caveats, national release restrictions, or bilateral/multilateral agreements shall not be disclosed, copied, transferred, downgraded, released, or re-marked without appropriate authority.

8. **Auditable accountability.** All access to classified and mission systems shall be attributable to an individual, service, or device identity. Administrative and high-risk actions shall be logged, monitored, reviewed, and retained.

9. **Privacy and proportionality.** Access to civilian, contractor, and other personal data shall be limited to authorised functions and processed under applicable EU, Romanian, NATO, SOFA, and mission requirements. GDPR Article 32 requires appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure security appropriate to risk, including confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, and regular testing of measures. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?qid=1494678401079&uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679&utm_source=openai))

10. **Operational resilience.** Emergency access may be provided only under controlled, logged, time-bound, commander-authorised procedures and must be reviewed after use.

## 5. Policy Requirements and Controls

### AC-01 — Access Control Governance

**Policy requirement:**  
The ISSM shall maintain a domain-specific access-control standard and access-control matrix for NU, NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET. The matrix shall define authorised roles, access conditions, clearance level, citizenship or caveat restrictions where applicable, approving authority, required training or briefing, authentication method, privileged status, review frequency, and logging requirements.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Maintain access-control matrices for all operational domains.
- Map each role to mission function, clearance, caveats, and need-to-know.
- Maintain separate matrices for privileged, CDS, SOC, COMSEC, intelligence, and system-administration roles.
- Include access-control evidence in accreditation and audit packages.
- Review the access-control matrix at least quarterly for classified domains and after each major mission change.

**Audit evidence:** Access-control matrix, approved role definitions, accreditation package, change records, quarterly review minutes.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosures B and F; NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 AC-1, AC-2, AC-3, AC-6; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.PO, PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 5, 6, 8 and Annex A 5.15, 5.18.

### AC-02 — Eligibility for Access to Classified Systems and Information

**Policy requirement:**  
Access to classified systems or information shall be granted only when all of the following are true:

1. The individual has an appropriate and current clearance or access authorisation for the classification level and caveats.
2. The individual has a documented mission need-to-know.
3. The individual has completed required NATO, national, coalition, domain-specific, and role-specific briefings.
4. The individual has acknowledged security responsibilities in writing or equivalent non-repudiable method.
5. The information owner or delegated approving authority has approved access.
6. The system owner has confirmed that the system is accredited for the classification and access mode.

NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 requires appropriate clearance for NATO CONFIDENTIAL and above, need-to-know, and security briefing obligations before access. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

**Minimum implementation:**

- Verify clearance before account creation for NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET.
- Record need-to-know approval in the access management system.
- Require annual re-briefing for classified domain access.
- Deny access when clearance, mission role, or need-to-know cannot be confirmed.
- Treat SIGINT-related access as requiring additional national and NATO SIGINT policy eligibility. Specific SIGINT access criteria are not reproduced in this policy.

**Audit evidence:** Clearance verification, access request, approval ticket, briefing record, user acknowledgement, account provisioning record.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosures B, C, E, F; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 para. 44; NIST PS-3, AC-2, AC-3; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.16, 5.18, 6.1, 6.2.

### AC-03 — Identity and Account Lifecycle Management

**Policy requirement:**  
All users, administrators, services, and devices shall have unique, attributable identities within each authorised domain. Accounts shall be created, modified, disabled, and removed only through approved joiner-mover-leaver procedures.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Use unique named accounts for all users in each domain.
- Maintain separate identity stores for NU, NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET.
- Prohibit account sharing except for explicitly approved break-glass or technical service cases.
- Assign account expiry dates for contractors, temporary staff, exercise participants, emergency accounts, and visitors.
- Disable user accounts:
  - Immediately upon termination, loss of clearance, confirmed compromise, or command direction.
  - Within 24 hours of redeployment, transfer, contract end, or role removal.
  - Automatically after 30 days of inactivity for NU and NR; after 15 days for NS and MISSION SECRET, unless mission exemption is approved.
- Review active accounts:
  - Monthly for NS, MISSION SECRET, privileged, CDS, SOC, and COMSEC roles.
  - Quarterly for NU and NR non-privileged accounts.
- Reconcile accounts against personnel rosters, contractor rosters, clearance status, and mission assignment.

NIST AC-2 requires account approval, creation, modification, disabling, monitoring, review, and alignment with termination and transfer processes. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Account inventory, joiner-mover-leaver records, automated disablement reports, inactivity reports, monthly and quarterly access reviews.

**References:** NIST AC-2, AC-2(2), AC-2(3), AC-2(7), IA-4, IA-5; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.16, 5.18, 8.2.

### AC-04 — Authentication and Credential Management

**Policy requirement:**  
Authentication shall be domain-specific, strong, replay-resistant where technically supported, and based on approved PKI and smart-card credentials. Password-only authentication is prohibited for interactive access to NR, NS, MISSION SECRET, privileged accounts, CDS consoles, SOC consoles, COMSEC support systems, and remote administration.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Enforce smart-card / CAC-equivalent authentication for all domain logons where supported.
- Maintain separate PKI, trust stores, certificate authorities, revocation lists, and credential issuance processes for each classification domain.
- Prohibit cross-domain credential reuse where it would enable identity federation or unauthorised transitive trust.
- Protect private keys on approved hardware tokens or smart cards.
- Revoke credentials immediately upon loss, compromise, termination, clearance loss, or role removal.
- Change default authenticators before first use.
- Require PIN complexity, retry limits, certificate revocation checking, and secure credential issuance procedures.
- Require MFA for all privileged, remote, maintenance, CDS, and SOC access.
- Report lost or suspected-compromised authenticators immediately.

NIST IA-2 requires unique identification and authentication of organisational users, and NIST IA-5 requires managing authenticators, protecting authenticator content, revocation procedures, and changing default authenticators before first use. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** PKI issuance records, revocation logs, smart-card inventory, authenticator exception register, credential compromise tickets.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure F para. 8.1(b); NIST IA-2, IA-2(8), IA-3, IA-5, IA-7; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01, PR.AA-03, PR.AA-04; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.17, 8.5.

### AC-05 — Access Authorisation, RBAC, ABAC, and Need-to-Know Enforcement

**Policy requirement:**  
Access shall be authorised using role-based access control supported by attributes relevant to classification, clearance, nationality or releasability, mission assignment, caveat, ORCON, operational cell, location, device posture, and time-bound operational need.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Use role groups approved by system and information owners.
- Bind access decisions to clearance and need-to-know attributes.
- Restrict intelligence and operational planning data by mission cell, caveat, and originator rules.
- Enforce deny-by-default authorisation.
- Require information-owner approval for access to ORCON, SIGINT, HUMINT-sensitive, national caveat, or coalition-restricted holdings.
- Remove access when the mission role changes, even if clearance remains valid.
- Require additional approval for bulk access, export, privileged search, database administration, and access to audit records.

**Audit evidence:** Role catalogue, attribute schema, approval workflows, entitlement reports, ORCON approval records.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure B need-to-know and ORCON; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 para. 44; NIST AC-3, AC-4, AC-6, AC-16, AC-24; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.15, 5.18, 8.3.

### AC-06 — Privileged Access Management

**Policy requirement:**  
Privileged access shall be strictly limited, separately approved, separately authenticated, logged, time-bound where feasible, and reviewed more frequently than ordinary user access.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Maintain a privileged-access register for all domains.
- Use named privileged accounts separate from ordinary user accounts.
- Prohibit administrators from using privileged accounts for email, web browsing, office productivity, or ordinary user activity.
- Require privileged session logging for NR, NS, MISSION SECRET, CDS, SOC, COMSEC support, and boundary devices.
- Require dual approval for new privileged access.
- Use just-in-time or time-bound elevation where supported by domain tooling.
- Review privileged access monthly.
- Remove privileged access within 24 hours when no longer required.
- Correlate privileged activity with change tickets, incident tickets, and maintenance windows.

NIST AC-6 requires least privilege, and AC-6 guidance recognises separate non-privileged accounts for nonsecurity functions. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Privileged-access register, approval tickets, PAM logs, session recordings where deployed, monthly review evidence.

**References:** NIST AC-2, AC-5, AC-6, AC-6(1), AC-6(2), AC-17, AU-6; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.15, 5.18, 8.2, 8.15.

### AC-07 — Separation of Duties and Dual-Key Actions

**Policy requirement:**  
The following duties and actions shall be separated and, where applicable, require dual authorisation:

- High-grade COMSEC keying material access, handling, loading, destruction, and inventory.
- Release of specified operational orders requiring dual-key procedure.
- CDS rule changes and high-risk cross-domain release actions.
- Creation of privileged accounts.
- Modification of audit logging, monitoring, or security sensor configuration.
- Emergency access activation.
- Account approval and account provisioning.
- System administration and audit review.
- Intelligence product release and originating-cell approval.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Maintain a separation-of-duties matrix.
- Configure technical enforcement where supported.
- Use procedural two-person control when technical enforcement is unavailable.
- Rotate dual-key participants where practical to reduce collusion risk.
- Document both authorising identities, time, purpose, and evidence for dual-key events.

NIST AC-3(2) recognises dual authorisation for privileged commands or defined actions and states that dual authorisation reduces insider-threat risk. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Dual-authorisation records, COMSEC chain-of-custody logs, operational release logs, CDS release records, SoD matrix.

**References:** NIST AC-3(2), AC-5, AC-6, AU-10, CP-9; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.3, 5.15, 5.18. COMSEC dual-key specifics are mission-mandated in the organisational profile; public clause citation not provided.

### AC-08 — Classification Domain Separation and Cross-Domain Access

**Policy requirement:**  
NU, NR, NS, and MISSION SECRET shall remain separate classification domains. Direct transfer, bridging, copy-paste, shared identity federation, shared storage, shared printing, shared removable media, or unauthorised network path between domains is prohibited.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Permit cross-domain transfers only through accredited NATO CDS or separately accredited national/coalition CDS.
- Maintain separate user accounts, PKI, admin roles, audit stores, and boundary controls per domain.
- Prohibit users from manually transferring data between domains using removable media unless an approved transfer procedure, inspection, release authority, and audit trail are in place.
- Enforce content inspection, metadata inspection, labelling, malware scanning, policy filters, and release approvals as required by the CDS accreditation.
- Require information-owner or release-authority approval before moving information from higher to lower classification or between caveated communities.
- Treat any unauthorised transfer or suspected transfer as a spillage incident.

NIST AC-4 addresses information-flow enforcement between systems and security or privacy domains and recognises one-way flows, regrading mechanisms, content filtering, and cross-domain solution needs. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** CDS accreditation, transfer logs, release approvals, filter rule change records, spillage tickets, media-transfer forms.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure F; AC/322-D(2017)0009 as applicable to NATO INFOSEC technical implementation; NIST AC-4, AC-4(1), AC-4(17), AC-4(21), AC-4(23), AC-4(24), SC-7, SC-7(22), SC-7(26); ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.14, 5.15, 8.22.

### AC-09 — Boundary Protection and Network Access

**Policy requirement:**  
Network access shall be controlled at all external and internal managed interfaces, including domain boundaries, enclave boundaries, remote-access gateways, wireless interfaces, maintenance interfaces, and CDS interfaces.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Route all inter-domain and external connections through managed boundary protection devices.
- Deploy sensors at every domain boundary.
- Deny inbound and outbound traffic by default except approved mission flows.
- Maintain separate subnets or enclaves for different classification domains and mission functions.
- Prohibit direct classified network connection to external networks without approved boundary protection and accreditation.
- Disable unauthorised split tunnelling for remote or maintenance access.
- Block unauthorised devices and unauthorised user-configured communication clients.
- Review boundary rules monthly for classified domains and after incidents or mission changes.

NIST SC-7 requires monitoring and controlling communications at managed interfaces and connecting external networks only through managed interfaces with boundary protection devices. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf)) NIST SC-7 also identifies separate subnets for different security domains and managed interfaces for classified national security systems. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Firewall and guard rule sets, boundary diagrams, sensor coverage map, monthly rule review, accreditation records.

**References:** NIST SC-7, SC-7(3), SC-7(7), SC-7(20), SC-7(21), SC-7(22), SC-7(26), AC-4; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 8.20, 8.22.

### AC-10 — Device Access and Workstation Control

**Policy requirement:**  
Only authorised, inventoried, configured, and domain-approved devices shall connect to detachment networks or access detachment information systems.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Maintain device identity and asset inventory per domain.
- Enforce device authentication where technically supported.
- Prohibit personal devices on NR, NS, MISSION SECRET, CDS, COMSEC, and SOC networks.
- Require approved build baselines, endpoint protection, configuration compliance, disk encryption where accredited, and host-based logging.
- Assign workstations to classification domains; multi-domain workstations shall require explicit accreditation and technical separation.
- Restrict USB and removable media according to classification and mission transfer procedures.
- Immediately quarantine or disconnect noncompliant devices.

NIST IA-3 requires device identification and authentication before establishing local, remote, or network connections where defined by the organisation. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Device inventory, NAC logs, endpoint compliance reports, quarantine records, exception register.

**References:** NIST IA-3, AC-19, CM-8, CM-7, SC-7, SI-4; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-01, PR.AA-03; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.9, 8.1, 8.9, 8.20.

### AC-11 — Remote Access and Nonlocal Maintenance

**Policy requirement:**  
Remote access and nonlocal maintenance to detachment systems shall be prohibited by default and permitted only when mission-approved, accredited, strongly authenticated, encrypted using approved mechanisms, logged, monitored, and time-bound.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Require commander or delegated system-owner approval for remote administrative access to classified or mission systems.
- Require ISSM approval for remote maintenance paths.
- Require MFA, named accounts, session recording where available, and SOC monitoring.
- Use approved jump hosts or bastions; direct remote administration to critical systems is prohibited unless accredited.
- Disable remote access immediately after maintenance completion or emergency period.
- For vendor or contractor maintenance, require screened personnel, approved equipment, comparable security controls, sanitisation where required, and escort or monitoring.
- Prohibit remote vendor access to NS or MISSION SECRET unless explicitly accredited by the appropriate authority.

NIST MA-4 requires controls for nonlocal maintenance, including logging and review, comparable security capability, replay-resistant authenticators, and separation of maintenance sessions. ([nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-53r5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Remote access approvals, maintenance tickets, session logs, vendor identity records, SOC monitoring records.

**References:** NIST AC-17, MA-4, MA-5, IA-2, IA-5, SC-8, SC-13; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.20, 5.22, 8.5, 8.20.

### AC-12 — Cross-Domain Solution Operators and Release Authorities

**Policy requirement:**  
CDS operators, guards, releasers, and release authorities shall be specially authorised, trained, monitored, and separated from ordinary users and routine administrators.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Maintain a named list of CDS operators and release authorities.
- Require domain-specific training before appointment.
- Require dual authorisation for high-risk or lower-domain release where mandated by CDS accreditation or information owner.
- Require release decisions to validate classification, caveats, ORCON, malware scanning, metadata, embedded content, and authorised recipients.
- Prohibit CDS rule changes without change control, ISSM review, and accreditation-impact assessment.
- Review CDS transfer logs daily during operations and formally at least weekly.

**Audit evidence:** CDS operator appointment orders, training records, release logs, daily review records, change records, accreditation-impact assessments.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure F; NIST AC-4, AC-5, AC-6, AC-16, AU-6, CM-3, CA-7, SC-7; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.14, 5.15, 5.18, 8.12, 8.15.

### AC-13 — COMSEC Access and Chain of Custody

**Policy requirement:**  
Access to COMSEC material, keying material, cryptographic devices, fill devices, and COMSEC records shall be restricted to appointed, trained, cleared, and authorised personnel with mission need-to-know.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Maintain an authorised COMSEC access roster.
- Enforce two-person integrity for high-grade keying material.
- Maintain end-to-end chain-of-custody records for keying material and controlled cryptographic items.
- Separate COMSEC custody from routine system administration unless specifically approved.
- Record issue, receipt, transfer, destruction, audit, and inventory actions.
- Report COMSEC anomalies immediately to the COMSEC authority, ISSM, and command chain.

**Audit evidence:** COMSEC appointment letters, access roster, custody logs, inventory records, destruction certificates, anomaly reports.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure F references cryptographic products, mechanisms, and COMSEC materials as supporting resources. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf)) NIST AC-3(2), AC-5, AC-6, AU-9, SC-12, SC-13; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 8.24. Specific national COMSEC authority procedures are controlled; public clause citation not provided.

### AC-14 — Physical Access to Classified CIS and TEMPEST Areas

**Policy requirement:**  
Physical access to areas where classified CIS can be accessed shall be limited to authorised, cleared, briefed, and need-to-know personnel. Physical access does not automatically confer logical access.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Control access to NS and above areas, SCIF rooms, server rooms, CDS rooms, SOC areas, COMSEC storage, and TEMPEST-zoned spaces.
- Use badge, escort, visitor, and access-list procedures appropriate to the classification level.
- Segregate personnel by clearance and need-to-know where classified information is visible, audible, or accessible.
- Maintain visitor logs and escort records.
- Review physical access rosters monthly for NS, MISSION SECRET, SCIF, COMSEC, and CDS areas.
- Immediately revoke physical access upon termination, transfer, clearance suspension, or insider-threat concern.

NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 physical security measures are required to support access control and enforcement of need-to-know, and to segregate personnel according to clearance and need-to-know. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Physical access lists, badge logs, visitor logs, escort records, monthly roster reviews.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosures C, D, F; NIST PE-2, PE-3, PE-6, AC-3; NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA-06; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 7.1, 7.2, 7.3, 7.4.

### AC-15 — Contractor, Host-Nation, and Non-NATO Entity Access

**Policy requirement:**  
Contractor, host-nation, and non-NATO entity access shall be limited to the contract, mission, sponsorship, clearance, briefing, and information-sharing authority under which access is granted.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Verify contractor screening and clearance before access.
- Limit contractor accounts by contract, site, domain, and expiration date.
- Require sponsor approval for host-nation and non-NATO personnel.
- Ensure non-NATO entity access follows NATO security-in-relation-to-non-NATO-entities procedures and bilateral or multilateral agreements.
- Require host-nation contractor access to personal data to comply with applicable GDPR, Romanian cybersecurity law, contract terms, and SOFA constraints.
- Remove contractor access immediately at contract completion, contract suspension, clearance suspension, or sponsor withdrawal.

NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 applies to classified information entrusted to individuals and organisations outside a government or NATO body, including consultants, industry, and universities, and includes security in relation to non-NATO entities. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Contractor access approvals, sponsor records, clearance verification, contract clauses, account expiry reports, access removal evidence.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosures B, G, H; NIST PS-3, PS-7, AC-2, AC-3, SA-9; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.19, 5.20, 5.22, 5.23.

### AC-16 — Personal Data Access

**Policy requirement:**  
Access to personal data of NATO civilian staff, contractors, host-nation personnel, visitors, and other individuals under EU or Romanian jurisdiction shall be limited to authorised personnel with an assigned function and lawful purpose.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Apply least privilege to HR, medical, vetting, access-badge, training, travel, disciplinary, and contractor records.
- Restrict export, bulk download, and privileged search of personal data.
- Log administrative access to personal-data repositories.
- Separate privacy-sensitive data from operational data where feasible.
- Support data minimisation, retention, and access-review requirements in coordination with legal and data-protection authorities.
- Notify the privacy or legal function and ISSM of suspected unauthorised personal-data access.

GDPR Article 32 requires technical and organisational measures appropriate to risk, and the European Commission describes NIS2 as requiring risk-management measures and incident reporting for relevant sectors. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?qid=1494678401079&uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679&utm_source=openai))

**Audit evidence:** HR and contractor system access lists, access logs, privileged search logs, privacy access reviews, incident records.

**References:** GDPR Articles 5(1)(f), 25, 32; NIS2 Directive Article 21 where applicable; Romanian Law No. 58/2023 and NIS2 transposition as applicable; NIST AC-2, AC-3, AC-6, PT-2, SC-7(24); ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.34, 5.15, 5.18, 8.3.

### AC-17 — Monitoring, Logging, and Review of Access

**Policy requirement:**  
All access to detachment systems shall be logged at a level commensurate with classification, mission impact, privacy risk, and threat profile. Logs shall be protected from unauthorised modification and reviewed by authorised personnel.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Log successful and failed authentication, account changes, privilege elevation, administrative actions, remote access, CDS transfers, COMSEC support-system activity, boundary events, and access to sensitive repositories.
- Forward security logs to theatre SOC or approved domain-specific monitoring capability.
- Protect logs from alteration by ordinary users and administrators whose actions are logged.
- Review:
  - Critical privileged activity daily for NS, MISSION SECRET, CDS, SOC, and COMSEC systems.
  - Access anomalies continuously through SOC tooling where available.
  - Formal access reports monthly for classified domains.
- Retain logs in accordance with accreditation, legal, operational, and evidentiary requirements.

**Audit evidence:** SIEM / SOC logs, audit configuration, log retention records, daily and monthly review evidence, anomaly tickets.

**References:** NIST AU-2, AU-6, AU-9, AU-11, AU-12, AC-2, SI-4; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 8.15, 8.16, 5.28. NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure F requires sufficient information to investigate deliberate, accidental, or attempted compromise of CIS security objectives. ### AC-18 — Emergency and Break-Glass Access

**Policy requirement:**  
Emergency access shall be used only when required to preserve mission capability, safety, classified information, operational continuity, or incident response. Emergency access shall be time-bound, logged, monitored, and reviewed after use.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Maintain a documented emergency-access procedure per domain.
- Require Detachment Commander, duty commander, ISSM, or delegated emergency authority approval.
- Use named emergency accounts where feasible; if shared break-glass credentials are unavoidable, store them under controlled dual-access procedures and rotate immediately after use.
- Notify SOC before or immediately upon emergency access activation, unless impossible under operational conditions.
- Review emergency access within 24 hours after use.
- Disable or rotate emergency access immediately after the event.
- Record mission justification, approving authority, start and end time, actions performed, and post-use review decision.

**Audit evidence:** Emergency access register, approval records, session logs, credential rotation records, post-use review.

**References:** NIST AC-2(2), AC-3, AC-6, AU-6, CP-2, IR-4; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.29, 5.30, 5.18.

### AC-19 — Access Control Training and User Briefing

**Policy requirement:**  
All personnel shall complete access-control, classification handling, cyber hygiene, insider-threat, HUMINT-awareness, spillage-prevention, and incident-reporting training before access and annually thereafter.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Provide at least 40 hours per staff member per year, including access-control and classification-handling components.
- Require role-specific training for administrators, CDS operators, SOC analysts, COMSEC custodians, intelligence users, release authorities, and system owners.
- Conduct six exercises per year and include access-control failure, spillage, insider threat, and cross-domain scenarios.
- Conduct two red-team engagements per year and include identity, privilege, and domain-boundary test objectives where authorised.
- Suspend access for personnel who fail mandatory training deadlines until training is complete, unless commander grants operational exception.

NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 requires security education and awareness programmes addressing security aspects, and the NATO Cyber Defence Pledge emphasises cyber skills, awareness, education, training, and exercising. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Training records, role-specific qualification records, exercise reports, red-team reports, suspension records.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure B; NATO Cyber Defence Pledge; NIST AT-2, AT-3, AC-2; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 6.3.

### AC-20 — Access Violations, Spillage, and Incident Response

**Policy requirement:**  
Suspected unauthorised access, attempted unauthorised access, credential compromise, insider activity, spillage, or unauthorised cross-domain transfer shall be reported immediately and handled as a security incident.

**Minimum implementation:**

- Users shall immediately report suspected violations to the service desk, SOC, security officer, chain of command, or ISSM.
- SOC shall triage access anomalies and escalate according to the IR plan.
- ISSM shall ensure account suspension, credential revocation, forensic preservation, and containment where required.
- Security Officer shall support clearance, aftercare, and counterintelligence referral.
- Spillage shall trigger containment, originator notification where required, and investigation.
- Incidents involving personal data shall be assessed for GDPR and host-nation notification obligations in coordination with legal and data-protection authorities.

AC/35-D/2002-REV5 requires security breaches or potential security breaches to be reported immediately to the appropriate security authority and investigated by suitably experienced and independent personnel. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6885/ac_35-d_2002_rev5.pdf))

**Audit evidence:** Incident ticket, initial report, account suspension record, containment actions, investigation report, lessons learned, notification records.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure B; AC/35-D/2002-REV5 paras. 78–85; NIST IR-4, IR-5, IR-6, AC-2, AU-6, SI-4; GDPR Articles 32–34 where personal data is involved; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.24–5.28.

## 6. Exceptions

1. Exceptions to this policy are prohibited unless formally approved.
2. Exception requests shall include:
   - Requested control deviation.
   - Mission justification.
   - Affected domain, system, users, and data.
   - Classification and caveats involved.
   - Risk assessment.
   - Compensating controls.
   - Expiry date not exceeding 90 days unless renewed.
   - Approvals from the system owner, information owner, ISSM, and commander or delegated authority.
3. Exceptions involving:
   - Cross-domain controls,
   - COMSEC,
   - NS or MISSION SECRET,
   - ORCON or intelligence caveats,
   - Personal data risk,
   - Host-nation or SOFA issues,
   - National caveats,
   - Accreditation boundary changes,  
   shall be escalated to the appropriate NATO, national, coalition, legal, data-protection, or COMSEC authority before implementation.
4. Exceptions shall not be used to bypass NATO Security Policy, classification rules, originator control, or accreditation requirements. NATO Security Policy states that risk management shall not be used to circumvent security policy. ([dksi.bg](https://www.dksi.bg/media/6881/c-m_2002_49_rev1.pdf))
5. Expired exceptions shall result in automatic reversion to the baseline control or documented access suspension.

**Audit evidence:** Exception register, risk assessment, compensating controls, approval record, expiry review, closure evidence.

**References:** NATO C-M(2002)49-REV1 Enclosure B; NIST RA-3, CA-7, PL-2, AC-1; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 6, 8, 9, 10.

## 7. Monitoring, Metrics, and Review

The ISSM shall maintain an access-control assurance programme proportionate to the detachment’s operational risk, size, threat profile, and resource envelope.

### 7.1 Required metrics

The following metrics shall be reported to the Detachment Commander and relevant governance forum at least monthly:

- Number of active users by domain.
- Number of privileged users by domain.
- Number of privileged accounts without current approval.
- Number of accounts disabled for inactivity.
- Number of accounts not matched to personnel or contractor roster.
- Number of emergency access activations.
- Number of failed logon anomalies and brute-force events.
- Number of cross-domain transfer attempts, releases, blocks, and violations.
- Number of access-control incidents, spillages, and credential compromises.
- Percentage completion of access-control and classification training.
- Timeliness of access removal after termination, transfer, or role change.
- Open exceptions by risk rating and age.

### 7.2 Review schedule

- **Daily:** SOC review of high-risk alerts, privileged anomalies, CDS anomalies, and boundary events.
- **Weekly:** CDS release log review and unresolved access anomaly review.
- **Monthly:** Privileged access review; NS, MISSION SECRET, COMSEC, SOC, CDS, and SCIF access review.
- **Quarterly:** NU and NR account review; role matrix review; contractor account review.
- **Annually:** Full policy review, access-control audit, training review, and alignment check against NATO, Romanian, EU, NIST, and ISO changes.
- **After event:** Review after significant incident, spillage, red-team finding, major exercise, mission change, framework-nation rotation, accreditation change, or threat-intelligence update.

### 7.3 Internal audit and assurance

The ISSM shall ensure that evidence is retained to demonstrate implementation and effectiveness. Internal audits shall sample:

- Clearance-to-account traceability.
- Need-to-know approvals.
- Privileged access.
- Contractor access.
- CDS transfers.
- Emergency access.
- COMSEC chain-of-custody access.
- Personal-data access.
- Physical-to-logical access consistency for classified spaces.

**References:** NIST CA-7, AU-6, AC-2, AC-6; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 9.1, 9.2, 9.3 and Annex A 5.35, 8.15, 8.16. NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 expressly includes assessment, authorisation, monitoring, audit and accountability, access control, identification and authentication, and privacy-related control families. ([csrc.nist.gov](https://csrc.nist.gov/Pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/Final))

## 8. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in immediate access suspension, credential revocation, removal from privileged role, command action, contract action, administrative or disciplinary measures, security investigation, counterintelligence referral, legal referral, or host-nation / national authority notification as applicable.

Any user who accesses, attempts to access, transfers, discloses, modifies, destroys, copies, or stores information without authorisation may be subject to investigation under NATO, national, host-nation, SOFA, contractual, and coalition authorities.
