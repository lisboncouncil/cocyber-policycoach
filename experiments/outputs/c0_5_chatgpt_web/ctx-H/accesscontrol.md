## 2. Purpose

This policy defines how access to the Hospital’s information systems, clinical applications, networks, devices, and physical ICT areas shall be requested, approved, provisioned, reviewed, monitored, and revoked.

The purpose is to:

1. protect patient safety and continuity of 24/7 clinical services;
2. prevent unauthorised access to health data, minors’ data, employee data, and administrative information;
3. reduce ransomware, credential theft, insider misuse, supply-chain, and mobile-device theft risks;
4. meet applicable Italian and EU public-health-sector obligations, including GDPR, the Italian Data Protection Code, NIS2, AgID minimum ICT security measures, Garante guidance for healthcare dossiers/FSE, and regional FSE rules;
5. establish a practical access-control baseline that can be implemented by a small hospital with limited internal IT capacity and MSP support.

GDPR requires appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure security of processing, including protection against unauthorised access and the ability to ensure confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience; NIS2 Article 21 requires essential and important entities to apply cybersecurity risk-management measures including cyber hygiene, access-control policies, asset management, and MFA or continuous authentication where appropriate. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=+CELEX%3A32016R0679&utm_source=openai))

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- all workforce members: clinical, administrative, technical/facility staff, temporary staff, trainees, consultants, and volunteers;
- all third parties with access to Hospital systems, including the MSP, medical-device vendors, PACS/RIS/LIS/EHR/FSE suppliers, pharmacy-system suppliers, and regional platform operators where access is under Hospital control;
- all information systems and assets used by or on behalf of the Hospital, including:
  - on-premises Active Directory;
  - SPID/CIE federation interfaces;
  - EHR / Cartella Clinica Elettronica and regional FSE functions;
  - PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, Pharmacy management;
  - VPN, UTM, network switches, VLANs, Wi-Fi, NAS backup infrastructure;
  - workstations, clinical kiosks, mobile point-of-care devices, servers, and legacy Windows kiosks;
  - administrative systems, shared folders, email, printers/scanners, and file repositories;
  - physical access to ICT rooms, network cabinets, backup media, and clinical kiosks.

This policy covers logical access, privileged access, emergency access, remote access, supplier access, service accounts, shared clinical devices, mobile devices, network access, audit logging, access reviews, and exceptions.

## 5. Access Control Principles

1. **Patient safety first, but not “security off.”** Controls must not block emergency care. Where emergency access is needed, it shall be permitted through documented “break-glass” procedures, logged, and reviewed after use. NIST CSF implementation examples explicitly recognise that authorised personnel must be able to access accounts essential for safety under emergency conditions. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/csf-20-implementations-pdf))

2. **Least privilege and need-to-know.** Users shall receive only the access required for their role, ward/unit, patient-care relationship, and authorised task. NIST CSF PR.AA-05 requires access permissions and authorisations to be defined, managed, enforced, reviewed, and based on least privilege and separation of duties. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

3. **Named accountability.** Access shall be attributable to an individual wherever technically possible. Shared accounts are prohibited except for controlled technical use cases such as restricted kiosk launch accounts or service accounts.

4. **Role-based access with clinical context.** Clinical access shall be based on role, ward/unit, care relationship, current admission/episode, consultation, on-call duty, or emergency need.

5. **Separation of duties.** Administrative, clinical, technical, and privileged functions shall be separated where feasible, particularly for domain administration, backup administration, clinical-record modification, pharmacy functions, and billing/administrative workflows.

6. **Strong authentication proportional to risk.** MFA is mandatory for privileged accounts, VPN, MSP/vendor access, and remote access. MFA shall be extended to clinical and administrative high-risk applications as procurement and system capability allow. AgID minimum ICT security measures require MFA for administrative access, including domain administration. ([agid.gov.it](https://www.agid.gov.it/sites/default/files/repository_files/documentazione/misure_minime_di_sicurezza_v.1.0.pdf))

7. **Logging and review.** Access to clinical dossiers/EHR and privileged activities shall be logged and reviewed. Garante healthcare-dossier guidance requires automatic log files for access and operations and requires logging of simple consultations. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/documents/10160/0/Linee%2Bguida%2Bin%2Bmateria%2Bdi%2Bdossier%2Bsanitario%2B-%2BAllegato%2BA.pdf/64853436-7a69-4b18-b0b5-9c1abd8e2304?version=1.4))

8. **Supplier access is Hospital-controlled.** Outsourcing does not transfer accountability. MSP and vendor access must be governed, time-bound, authenticated, logged, and reviewed.

9. **Compensating controls for legacy systems.** Legacy Windows clinical kiosks and systems that cannot support modern controls shall be isolated, restricted, physically protected, and prioritised for replacement or upgrade.

10. **Privacy by design and by default.** Access controls shall support GDPR principles of data minimisation, integrity, confidentiality, and accountability. GDPR Article 5 requires personal data to be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary and processed with appropriate security against unauthorised or unlawful processing. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=+CELEX%3A32016R0679&utm_source=openai))

## 6. Policy Requirements and Controls

### 6.1 Access-Control Register and System Ownership

**Requirement ACP-6.1.1 — Critical-system register.**  
The IT Manager shall maintain an access-control register for all critical systems: AD, VPN/UTM, EHR/FSE, ADT, PACS/RIS, LIS, Pharmacy, NAS backup, email, file shares, clinical kiosks, mobile-device management, and network administration platforms.

The register shall identify:

- application/system owner;
- data category processed;
- user roles/groups;
- privileged roles;
- supplier/vendor accounts;
- logging capability;
- access-review frequency;
- emergency-access method;
- system limitations or legacy exceptions.

**Minimum implementation:** spreadsheet or simple ITSM list maintained by IT/MSP; no GRC platform is required.

**References:** NIST CSF PR.AA-01, PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.9, 5.15, 5.16, 5.18; NIS2 Article 21(2)(i). ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

---

### 6.2 Identity Lifecycle Management

**Requirement ACP-6.2.1 — Authoritative identity source.**  
HR shall be the authoritative source for staff employment status. No account shall be created, modified, extended, or disabled without HR notification, department-manager approval, or documented emergency approval.

**Requirement ACP-6.2.2 — Joiner process.**  
New users shall receive access only after:

1. identity has been verified by HR or the responsible department;
2. employment/assignment start date is known;
3. department/ward manager approves the role;
4. mandatory onboarding privacy/security briefing has been completed or scheduled within the first working week;
5. IT/MSP provisions access using approved AD groups or application roles.

**Requirement ACP-6.2.3 — Mover process.**  
Role changes, ward transfers, changes from clinical to administrative work, temporary assignments, and secondments shall be communicated by HR or the department manager to IT/MSP within one working day. Old access shall be removed before or at the same time as new access is granted, unless documented overlap is clinically necessary.

**Requirement ACP-6.2.4 — Leaver process.**  
For planned departures, HR shall notify IT/MSP at least five working days before the end date. Accounts shall be disabled by the end of the user’s final working day. For involuntary termination, suspension, or suspected misuse, accounts shall be disabled immediately upon HR or management instruction.

**Requirement ACP-6.2.5 — Temporary and trainee access.**  
Temporary staff, trainees, students, contractors, and locums shall have expiry dates not exceeding the approved assignment period. Default maximum validity is 90 days unless the contract or rotation period is shorter.

**References:** NIST CSF PR.AA-01, PR.AA-02, PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.16, 5.18, 6.1, 6.2, 6.5; Garante dossier guidance on authorised persons and instructions. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

### 6.3 Unique User Accounts and Prohibition of Credential Sharing

**Requirement ACP-6.3.1 — Named accounts.**  
Each workforce member shall use a unique, named account for AD, clinical applications, administrative systems, and remote access. Accounts shall be traceable to one natural person.

**Requirement ACP-6.3.2 — No credential sharing.**  
Users shall not share passwords, MFA tokens, smart cards, badges, PINs, or clinical-application sessions. Department managers shall treat credential sharing as a patient-data security incident.

**Requirement ACP-6.3.3 — Shared workstation/kiosk distinction.**  
A workstation or kiosk may be shared physically by multiple users, but clinical and administrative system access from that device must still require individual authentication wherever technically supported. If a legacy kiosk uses a shared Windows session, that session shall be restricted to launching approved clinical applications, and the clinical application itself must require named user authentication.

**Requirement ACP-6.3.4 — Generic accounts.**  
Generic accounts such as “warduser,” “nurse,” “admin,” or “radiology” are prohibited for access to patient data. Existing generic accounts shall be replaced or technically restricted by 31 December 2026 unless an exception is approved.

**References:** NIST CSF PR.AA-01, PR.AA-02, PR.AA-03; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.16, 5.17, 5.18, 8.5; AgID ABSC 5.10 on named and accountable administrative accounts. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-csf-20-core-withdrawn-csf-11-elements))

### 6.4 Role-Based Access Control for Clinical and Administrative Systems

**Requirement ACP-6.4.1 — Standard roles.**  
Access shall be assigned through standard roles or AD groups aligned to the Hospital’s functions, including at minimum:

- internal medicine clinician;
- general surgery clinician;
- paediatrics clinician;
- long-term care clinician;
- emergency first-aid clinician;
- nursing staff;
- pharmacy staff;
- laboratory staff;
- radiology staff;
- admissions/ADT staff;
- administrative/billing staff;
- technical/facility staff;
- IT/MSP support;
- application administrator;
- read-only auditor/privacy reviewer.

**Requirement ACP-6.4.2 — Clinical care relationship.**  
Access to EHR/FSE/dossier information shall be limited to healthcare professionals involved in prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, consultation, on-call coverage, or emergency care for the patient. Access shall be limited in time to the care episode or documented need.

**Requirement ACP-6.4.3 — Administrative staff access.**  
Administrative staff may access only the demographic, scheduling, admission, billing, or document-request information strictly necessary for their function. Administrative staff shall not have unrestricted access to clinical notes, images, lab results, paediatric records, or sensitive clinical modules unless specifically approved and justified.

**Requirement ACP-6.4.4 — Depth of access.**  
Application owners shall configure roles to limit not only who may access a dossier or clinical record, but also the “depth” of access: view, create, modify, validate/sign, prescribe, dispense, export, print, delete, or administer.

**Requirement ACP-6.4.5 — Paediatric and highly sensitive records.**  
Access to paediatric records and records requiring higher confidentiality protections shall be restricted to authorised care teams and legally permitted administrative processes. Where applications support masking, segmentation, or special-consent flags, these functions shall be enabled in accordance with regional and Garante requirements.

Garante guidance states that healthcare dossier access must be limited to healthcare personnel involved in the patient’s care process and that administrative personnel may consult only information indispensable for their assigned administrative functions. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/documents/10160/0/Linee%2Bguida%2Bin%2Bmateria%2Bdi%2Bdossier%2Bsanitario%2B-%2BAllegato%2BA.pdf/64853436-7a69-4b18-b0b5-9c1abd8e2304?version=1.4))

**References:** GDPR Article 5(1)(c), 5(1)(f), Article 32; NIST CSF PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.15, 5.18, 8.3; Garante healthcare dossier guidance sections 6 and 6.1. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=+CELEX%3A32016R0679&utm_source=openai))

### 6.5 Authentication Requirements

**Requirement ACP-6.5.1 — MFA mandatory use cases.**  
MFA is mandatory for:

- all domain administrator and privileged AD accounts;
- VPN and remote access;
- MSP and vendor access;
- firewall/UTM, backup, network, and server administration;
- cloud or regional administrative portals under Hospital control;
- any internet-facing administrative console.

**Requirement ACP-6.5.2 — MFA extension roadmap.**  
MFA shall be extended, as systems and procurement allow, to EHR/FSE, email, pharmacy, and other high-risk clinical or administrative applications. Priority shall be given to users with broad clinical-data access, pharmacy functions, and administrative users processing employee or patient data.

**Requirement ACP-6.5.3 — Password baseline.**  
For systems not yet supporting MFA:

- passwords shall be at least 12 characters for standard users and at least 14 characters for privileged accounts;
- default vendor passwords shall be changed before production use;
- weak or commonly used passwords shall be blocked where technically possible;
- account lockout or throttling shall be enabled for repeated failed attempts where this does not create unacceptable emergency-care risk;
- passwords shall be changed immediately after suspected compromise.

**Requirement ACP-6.5.4 — Clinical workflow support.**  
To avoid unsafe workarounds in emergency areas, clinical systems may use badge tap, smart card, badge + PIN, fast user switching, or single sign-on where available, provided the individual user remains identifiable and sessions lock automatically.

**Requirement ACP-6.5.5 — SPID/CIE federation.**  
Where SPID/CIE federation is used for regional services, identity assertions shall be protected, verified, and mapped to the correct Hospital role before access is granted.

### 6.6 Privileged Access Management

**Requirement ACP-6.6.1 — Separate privileged accounts.**  
IT/MSP administrators shall have separate standard and privileged accounts. Privileged accounts shall not be used for email, web browsing, routine office work, or clinical record access.

**Requirement ACP-6.6.2 — Privileged access approval.**  
Privileged access shall be approved by the IT Manager or delegated ASL authority and recorded in the privileged-access register. Domain-admin, backup-admin, firewall-admin, and clinical-application-admin rights require explicit approval.

**Requirement ACP-6.6.3 — Minimum administrative workstations.**  
The Hospital shall maintain at least one hardened administrative workstation or jump host for domain, network, backup, and server administration. This workstation shall be patched, encrypted where supported, protected by endpoint security, not used for email or general browsing, and restricted to IT/MSP administrators.

**Requirement ACP-6.6.4 — Local administrator rights.**  
Standard users shall not have local administrator rights on workstations or kiosks. Exceptions require documented business need, time limit, and compensating controls.

**Requirement ACP-6.6.5 — Emergency administrator credentials.**  
Break-glass administrative credentials shall be stored securely in a sealed envelope in a locked location or in an approved password vault. Use shall require two-person authorisation where feasible and shall be logged and reviewed within five working days.

**Requirement ACP-6.6.6 — Privileged logging.**  
Creation, deletion, failed login, rights elevation, and use of administrative accounts shall be logged. Alerts shall be configured where existing AD/UTM/AV/MSP tooling supports them.

AgID minimum measures require logging additions and removals of administrative accounts, alerting on administrative account creation and rights elevation, logging failed administrative access attempts, MFA for administrative access, separation of privileged and non-privileged accounts, named administrative users, and secure storage of administrative credentials. ([agid.gov.it](https://www.agid.gov.it/sites/default/files/repository_files/documentazione/misure_minime_di_sicurezza_v.1.0.pdf))

**References:** AgID ABSC 5.4–5.11; NIST CSF PR.AA-01, PR.AA-03, PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.15, 5.16, 5.17, 5.18, 8.2, 8.5, 8.15. ([agid.gov.it](https://www.agid.gov.it/sites/default/files/repository_files/documentazione/misure_minime_di_sicurezza_v.1.0.pdf))

### 6.7 MSP and Vendor Remote Access

**Requirement ACP-6.7.1 — Named vendor accounts.**  
MSP and vendor access shall use named individual accounts. Shared vendor accounts are prohibited unless the system cannot technically support named access and an approved exception exists.

**Requirement ACP-6.7.2 — VPN and MFA.**  
All remote support access shall use the Hospital-approved VPN with MFA. Direct internet exposure of RDP, VNC, SSH, database consoles, PACS administration, or medical-device administration interfaces is prohibited.

**Requirement ACP-6.7.3 — Time-bound access.**  
Vendor access shall be enabled only for approved support windows, scheduled maintenance, or active incident tickets. Standing 24/7 vendor access is prohibited unless approved by the IT Manager and Direzione Sanitaria for a documented clinical availability requirement.

**Requirement ACP-6.7.4 — Ticket-based access.**  
Remote vendor sessions shall be linked to a ticket, change request, or incident number. The ticket shall state the system, reason, approver, start/end time, and whether patient data may be accessible.

**Requirement ACP-6.7.5 — Supplier contract clauses.**  
New and renewed contracts shall require MFA, named accounts, prompt account revocation, audit-log retention, incident notification, confidentiality, data-processing terms where applicable, and cooperation with access reviews.

NIS2 risk-management measures apply regardless of whether network and information systems are maintained internally or outsourced, and Article 21 includes supply-chain security and access-control policies. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj?utm_source=openai))

**References:** NIS2 Article 21(2)(d), 21(2)(i), 21(2)(j); NIST CSF GV.SC, PR.AA-01, PR.AA-03, PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.19, 5.20, 5.21, 5.22, 5.23, 5.18, 8.5; GDPR Article 28 and Article 32 where suppliers process or may access personal data. ([nis2resources.eu](https://nis2resources.eu/directive-2022-2555-nis2/article-21/?utm_source=openai))

### 6.8 Emergency Clinical Access / “Break-Glass”

**Requirement ACP-6.8.1 — Emergency access permitted.**  
Emergency clinical access is permitted where necessary to protect the vital interests, health, or safety of a patient or others, or to maintain emergency clinical workflow.

**Requirement ACP-6.8.2 — Mandatory reason.**  
Where application functionality allows, the user shall select or enter a reason for emergency access, such as emergency first aid, on-call consultation, patient transferred from another ward, system downtime, or urgent diagnostic review.

**Requirement ACP-6.8.3 — Logging and retrospective review.**  
All emergency access shall be logged and reviewed retrospectively. The review shall confirm whether the user, patient, time, location, and reason are consistent with a legitimate clinical need.

**Requirement ACP-6.8.4 — No punitive delay in emergencies.**  
Emergency-access controls shall not introduce delays that endanger patient safety. Misuse shall be handled after the event through audit, disciplinary, privacy, and incident processes.

**Requirement ACP-6.8.5 — Downtime access.**  
If EHR/FSE or ADT is unavailable, emergency access to downtime procedures, read-only extracts, paper forms, or printed emergency data sets shall be governed by the Business Continuity and Downtime Procedures and reconciled after restoration.

**References:** NIST CSF PR.AA-03 implementation example on emergency access for safety; Garante healthcare dossier guidance on emergency care and access limited to care purposes; GDPR Article 32 availability and resilience. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/csf-20-implementations-pdf))

### 6.9 Legacy Windows Clinical Kiosks

**Requirement ACP-6.9.1 — Compensating controls.**  
The 35 legacy Windows clinical kiosks shall be subject to compensating controls:

- placement only in clinical VLANs or approved restricted network segments;
- no direct internet browsing except approved clinical endpoints;
- no email or office productivity use;
- endpoint protection active and centrally monitored where technically possible;
- local administrator rights removed from clinical users;
- USB/removable media blocked or physically controlled where feasible;
- physical placement in supervised clinical areas;
- automatic screen lock or application timeout configured where clinically safe;
- application allow-listing or software restriction policies where feasible;
- documented replacement or upgrade plan reviewed annually.

**Requirement ACP-6.9.2 — Kiosk shared sessions.**  
Where a kiosk requires a shared Windows session for technical reasons, that session shall not grant access to patient data without individual clinical-application login.

**Requirement ACP-6.9.3 — Risk acceptance.**  
Any kiosk unable to meet the above baseline shall have a documented exception approved by the IT Manager, Direzione Sanitaria, and DPO, including patient-safety justification and target remediation date.

### 6.10 Mobile Point-of-Care Devices

**Requirement ACP-6.10.1 — Managed devices only.**  
Only Hospital-managed mobile devices may access clinical systems, unless a formally approved exception exists.

**Requirement ACP-6.10.2 — Device security.**  
Mobile point-of-care devices shall have:

- device PIN, biometric, or equivalent lock;
- encryption enabled where supported;
- automatic lock after inactivity;
- remote wipe or disable capability;
- inventory identifier;
- endpoint or mobile-device management profile where available;
- no local storage of patient data unless specifically approved and encrypted.

**Requirement ACP-6.10.3 — Loss or theft.**  
Loss or theft of a mobile device shall be reported immediately to the ward manager and IT/MSP. IT/MSP shall disable associated sessions/tokens and initiate remote wipe where available.

**Requirement ACP-6.10.4 — Shared clinical mobiles.**  
Shared ward mobile devices shall require individual application login or badge/PIN identification for clinical functions.

### 6.11 Network Access and Segmentation

**Requirement ACP-6.11.1 — Segmented access.**  
The Hospital shall maintain at minimum separate network segments for clinical systems, administrative systems, PACS/RIS island, server/backup infrastructure, guest access, and management interfaces. Firewall rules shall allow only necessary traffic between segments.

**Requirement ACP-6.11.2 — Management interfaces.**  
UTM, switch, server, backup, NAS, PACS/RIS administration, and medical-device management interfaces shall be accessible only from approved IT/MSP administrative devices or management networks.

**Requirement ACP-6.11.3 — New devices.**  
New workstations, medical devices, printers, scanners, and mobile devices shall be recorded before network connection and assigned to the correct VLAN.

**Requirement ACP-6.11.4 — 802.1X roadmap.**  
802.1X or equivalent network access control shall be considered for new Wi-Fi and wired deployments. Because of budget and legacy constraints, implementation may be phased, starting with administrative areas, Wi-Fi, and new clinical devices.

**Requirement ACP-6.11.5 — Guest and patient networks.**  
Guest or patient Wi-Fi shall be isolated from clinical, administrative, PACS/RIS, server, and management networks.

AgID minimum measures include using network-level authentication such as 802.1X to limit and control devices connected to the network, correlated with inventory to distinguish authorised from unauthorised systems. ### 6.12 Service Accounts, Application Accounts, and Technical Accounts

**Requirement ACP-6.12.1 — Register and owner.**  
All service accounts shall be recorded in the access-control register with owner, system, purpose, privileges, password/secret owner, rotation requirement, and whether interactive login is disabled.

**Requirement ACP-6.12.2 — Least privilege.**  
Service accounts shall have only the permissions required for the service. Domain-admin service accounts are prohibited unless technically unavoidable and approved as an exception.

**Requirement ACP-6.12.3 — No interactive login.**  
Service accounts shall not permit interactive desktop, VPN, email, or web login unless technically required and documented.

**Requirement ACP-6.12.4 — Secret management.**  
Service-account passwords or secrets shall be stored in an approved password vault or sealed administrative credential store. Secrets shall be changed after suspected compromise, vendor staff change affecting support access, MSP contract change, or system owner change.

**Requirement ACP-6.12.5 — Managed service accounts.**  
Where supported by the Windows environment, group Managed Service Accounts or equivalent managed-secret mechanisms shall be preferred.

### 6.13 Logging, Monitoring, and Audit Trails

**Requirement ACP-6.13.1 — Clinical access logs.**  
EHR/FSE/dossier, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, and Pharmacy systems shall log, at minimum where technically supported:

- user identifier;
- date and time;
- workstation or device identifier;
- patient identifier or record accessed;
- operation type: view, create, modify, validate/sign, print, export, delete, administer;
- emergency/break-glass reason where applicable.

Garante guidance specifies that dossier log files should record the authorised user identifier, date and time, workstation code, patient identifier, and type of operation, and that even consultation/inquiry access must be logged. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/documents/10160/0/Linee%2Bguida%2Bin%2Bmateria%2Bdi%2Bdossier%2Bsanitario%2B-%2BAllegato%2BA.pdf/64853436-7a69-4b18-b0b5-9c1abd8e2304?version=1.4))

**Requirement ACP-6.13.2 — Log retention.**  
Clinical-access logs shall be retained for at least 24 months, unless a longer period is required by law, litigation hold, incident investigation, ASL policy, or regional FSE requirement.

Garante guidance considers at least 24 months a congruent retention period for healthcare dossier operation logs. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/documents/10160/0/Linee%2Bguida%2Bin%2Bmateria%2Bdi%2Bdossier%2Bsanitario%2B-%2BAllegato%2BA.pdf/64853436-7a69-4b18-b0b5-9c1abd8e2304?version=1.4))

**Requirement ACP-6.13.3 — Administrative and security logs.**  
AD, VPN, UTM, server, backup, NAS, and privileged-account logs shall be retained centrally where technically possible, using a low-cost syslog/NAS repository or MSP-managed logging until a SIEM/SOC is procured.

**Requirement ACP-6.13.4 — Review cadence.**  
Given the Hospital’s limited internal IT capacity and absence of SIEM/SOC:

- MSP shall provide monthly reports of privileged logins, VPN logins, failed administrative attempts, new admin accounts, and endpoint-protection alerts;
- IT Manager shall review privileged and remote-access activity at least monthly;
- application owners and DPO/Privacy Office shall review clinical-access anomalies at least quarterly and after any alert, complaint, VIP/media-sensitive case, or suspected misuse;
- emergency/break-glass accesses shall be reviewed within five working days where application reporting allows.

**Requirement ACP-6.13.5 — Alert criteria.**  
Where tools allow, alerts shall be configured for:

- new domain-admin or application-admin account;
- privilege elevation;
- repeated failed privileged logins;
- VPN login from unusual source or outside approved support window;
- disabled user attempting access;
- large export/print of patient records;
- access to records outside ward/care relationship;
- access to own record, family member record, colleague record, or VIP/media-sensitive record where detectable.

Garante guidance requires audit-log systems capable of detecting anomalous behaviour and using alerts to guide later audit activity. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/documents/10160/0/Linee%2Bguida%2Bin%2Bmateria%2Bdi%2Bdossier%2Bsanitario%2B-%2BAllegato%2BA.pdf/64853436-7a69-4b18-b0b5-9c1abd8e2304?version=1.4))

**References:** Garante dossier guidance section 7; AgID ABSC 5.4 and 8.1; NIST CSF PR.AA-05, PR.PS-04, DE.CM-03; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.18, 8.15, 8.16. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/documents/10160/0/Linee%2Bguida%2Bin%2Bmateria%2Bdi%2Bdossier%2Bsanitario%2B-%2BAllegato%2BA.pdf/64853436-7a69-4b18-b0b5-9c1abd8e2304?version=1.4))

### 6.14 Access Reviews and Recertification

**Requirement ACP-6.14.1 — Privileged access reviews.**  
Privileged accounts shall be reviewed quarterly by the IT Manager with MSP support. Domain-admin, backup-admin, firewall-admin, VPN-admin, clinical-application-admin, and database-admin accounts are in scope.

**Requirement ACP-6.14.2 — Clinical application access reviews.**  
Access to EHR/FSE, ADT, PACS/RIS, LIS, and Pharmacy shall be reviewed at least twice per year by application owners and department managers. Reviews shall check whether users still belong to the ward/unit and role requiring access.

**Requirement ACP-6.14.3 — Administrative access reviews.**  
Access to HR, finance, billing, personnel, and shared administrative folders shall be reviewed at least annually and after organisational changes.

**Requirement ACP-6.14.4 — Leaver reconciliation.**  
IT/MSP shall perform a monthly comparison of active AD/application accounts against HR active staff lists, focusing on leavers, expired temporary contracts, trainees, and vendor accounts.

**Requirement ACP-6.14.5 — Evidence.**  
Access reviews shall produce evidence: date, reviewer, systems reviewed, exceptions found, removals made, and unresolved risks.

**Requirement ACP-6.14.6 — Review remediation.**  
Unneeded access identified during reviews shall be removed within five working days for standard access and within one working day for privileged access.

## 7. Exceptions

### 7.1 Exception Criteria

Exceptions may be granted only where:

- the control is not technically supported by a legacy clinical system;
- immediate enforcement would create unacceptable patient-safety risk;
- procurement or vendor remediation is pending;
- regional platform constraints prevent local enforcement;
- temporary emergency or incident-response conditions apply.

### 7.2 Exception Approval

Exceptions require written approval by:

- IT Manager / Acting Information Security Lead;
- relevant application owner or ward/department manager;
- Direzione Sanitaria where patient workflow is affected;
- DPO where personal or health data risk is material;
- ASL Executive Management where residual risk is high.

### 7.3 Exception Record

Each exception shall document:

- system and users affected;
- control not met;
- reason;
- risk assessment;
- compensating controls;
- expiry date;
- remediation owner;
- target remediation date;
- approval evidence.

### 7.4 Maximum Duration

- Standard exceptions: maximum 12 months.
- High-risk exceptions involving privileged access, remote vendor access, or unrestricted clinical-record access: maximum 90 days.
- Patient-safety emergency exceptions: reviewed within five working days.

### 7.5 Compensating Controls

Compensating controls may include VLAN isolation, firewall restrictions, physical supervision, additional logging, manual access review, shorter account validity, restricted VPN windows, removal of internet access, or documented clinical supervision.

**References:** GDPR Article 32 risk-based security; NIS2 Article 21 appropriate and proportionate measures; NIST CSF GV.RM, PR.AA-05, ID.IM; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clause 6.1.3 risk treatment and Annex A control selection. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=+CELEX%3A32016R0679&utm_source=openai))

## 9. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in:

- immediate account suspension;
- revocation of privileged or remote access;
- disciplinary action under applicable employment rules and public-sector procedures;
- supplier contractual remedies;
- privacy incident assessment by the DPO;
- notification under GDPR or NIS2 incident processes where required;
- referral to competent authorities where unlawful access or data disclosure is suspected.

Unauthorised access to patient records, credential sharing, misuse of emergency access, unauthorised export/printing of clinical records, or vendor access outside approved windows shall be treated as serious violations.

## 10. Implementation Priorities for First 12 Months

Because the Hospital has limited internal IT staffing, no CISO, no SIEM/SOC, and a constrained cybersecurity budget, implementation shall prioritise high-impact, low-complexity controls:

1. **Immediate — 0–3 months**
   - enforce MFA for VPN, MSP, and privileged accounts;
   - create privileged-access register;
   - disable or restrict known generic accounts;
   - define access request template;
   - implement leaver monthly reconciliation;
   - restrict vendor VPN to approved users and support windows;
   - identify all legacy kiosk exceptions.

2. **Short term — 3–6 months**
   - complete role matrix for EHR/FSE, ADT, PACS/RIS, LIS, Pharmacy;
   - configure or improve clinical access logs and reports;
   - establish quarterly privileged-access review;
   - create emergency-access review process;
   - secure one administrative workstation or jump host;
   - onboard mobile devices into management or equivalent inventory/control process.

3. **Medium term — 6–12 months**
   - reduce shared accounts to approved exceptions only;
   - implement central log repository using NAS/syslog or MSP-managed logging;
   - include access-control requirements in all renewals and procurements;
   - improve segmentation rules between clinical/admin/PACS/server networks;
   - document replacement plan for legacy Windows kiosks.

4. **Longer term — 12–24 months**
   - extend MFA to email and critical clinical applications where technically possible;
   - introduce stronger network access control for new devices;
   - improve anomaly detection for clinical access;
   - prepare access-control evidence for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 readiness.
