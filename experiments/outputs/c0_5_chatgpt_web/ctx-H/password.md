## 2. Purpose

This policy defines mandatory rules for creating, using, protecting, resetting, monitoring, and retiring passwords and other memorised secrets used to access hospital information systems, clinical systems, administrative systems, network services, mobile devices, privileged accounts, and supplier/MSP access.

The policy is designed for a small 24/7 public hospital processing health data, children’s data, employee data, and other personal data. It prioritises patient safety and continuity of care while reducing the risk of ransomware, unauthorised access to patient records, credential theft, supplier compromise, and misuse of shared or generic accounts.

This policy supports GDPR security-of-processing obligations, NIS2 cybersecurity risk-management expectations for essential health entities, AgID minimum ICT security measures for Italian public administrations, Garante Privacy expectations for healthcare access controls, NIST CSF 2.0 outcomes, NIST SP 800-63B password guidance, and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A access-control objectives. GDPR Article 32 requires appropriate technical and organisational measures taking account of risk, state of the art, implementation cost, and the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing; it also expressly refers to confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, restoration, and regular testing of measures. ([gdpr-info.eu](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-32-gdpr/?utm_source=openai))

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope

This policy applies to:

- All workforce users: clinical, administrative, technical/facility, temporary, trainee, agency, and locum staff.
- All accounts used to access hospital-managed ICT, including on-premises Active Directory, VPN, email, workstations, clinical kiosks, mobile point-of-care devices, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, Pharmacy, local clinical applications, backup consoles, NAS, UTM, AV console, and infrastructure management tools.
- Accounts managed by external suppliers, including the MSP, clinical application vendors, radiology/PACS vendors, laboratory system vendors, and medical device support vendors.
- Local administrator, domain administrator, service, emergency, break-glass, shared-device, and application accounts.
- Passwords, passphrases, PINs, recovery codes, temporary passwords, local device unlock secrets, service-account secrets, API/application secrets where managed by the hospital or MSP, and vendor remote-access credentials.

### 3.2 Out of scope

- Password requirements set directly by regional SPID/CIE identity providers for citizen-facing or regional federation authentication, except where hospital systems consume or rely on those identities.
- Cryptographic keys, certificates, and tokens are primarily governed by the Cryptography / Key Management Policy where one exists; however, their activation PINs or passwords are in scope.
- Patient portal credentials managed exclusively by regional platforms are outside operational control of the hospital, but security incidents involving such access must still be reported through normal incident channels.

## 5. Policy Principles

1. **Patient safety first, but not credential-free care.** Controls must not block emergency treatment, but emergency access must be accountable, time-limited, logged, and reviewed.
2. **Named accountability.** Personal accounts must be traceable to one individual. Generic or shared accounts are prohibited except for documented emergency or technical cases with compensating controls.
3. **Passphrases over complex short passwords.** Users should choose longer passphrases rather than hard-to-remember complexity patterns.
4. **MFA before password rotation.** MFA is the preferred control for privileged, VPN, remote, and high-risk access. Routine forced password rotation is avoided for ordinary users unless compromise is suspected, but privileged and shared technical secrets must be rotated as required by AgID and local risk.
5. **Least privilege.** Password controls must support role-based access, especially for health records, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, Pharmacy, and administrative systems.
6. **Low-cost, high-impact implementation.** Controls must be achievable using existing AD Group Policy, UTM/VPN capabilities, MSP procedures, Windows LAPS or equivalent, password vaulting, supplier contract clauses, and targeted awareness training.

NIST CSF 2.0 places identity management, authentication, and access control under Protect category PR.AA; it also states that access permissions and authorisations should be defined, managed, enforced, reviewed, and incorporate least privilege and separation of duties. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29))

## 7. Minimum Technical Baseline

The following baseline must be implemented within 12 months of policy approval, prioritised by risk and procurement feasibility.

| Priority | Control | Target implementation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AD password policy | 15-character minimum, password history, account lockout, no routine user expiry once blocklisting is live. |
| 1 | VPN MFA | MFA for all staff, MSP, and vendor VPN users. |
| 1 | Privileged password vault | Vault all domain, local admin, UTM, NAS, backup, AV console, and clinical system admin secrets. |
| 1 | Named admin accounts | Separate ordinary/admin accounts for ICT and MSP administrators. |
| 1 | Remove undocumented shared accounts | Inventory and either remove, replace, or exception-document shared/generic accounts. |
| 2 | Password blocklist | AD-compatible blocklist including breached/common/context-specific passwords. |
| 2 | Windows LAPS / equivalent | Unique local admin passwords for Windows workstations and servers. |
| 2 | Supplier access register | Named supplier accounts, expiry dates, MFA status, and last review. |
| 2 | Clinical kiosk compensating controls | Document legacy kiosks, restrict OS accounts, require application-level user identity where feasible. |
| 3 | Application password upgrades | Add 64-character support, paste support, secure reset workflows, and password hashing requirements into procurement and supplier roadmaps. |

This prioritisation is a local, resource-based implementation decision — **unsupported by a specific external clause** — but it is aligned with NIST CSF’s expectation that organisations prioritise actions according to mission, legal and regulatory requirements, risk, and governance expectations. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29))

## 8. User Rules

All users must:

1. Keep passwords secret and never share them with colleagues, supervisors, ICT, MSP staff, vendors, or family members.
2. Use different passwords for hospital systems and personal accounts.
3. Use passphrases that are long, memorable, and not guessable from hospital, family, social media, or seasonal information.
4. Not write passwords on visible notes attached to workstations, kiosks, medication carts, mobile devices, or monitors.
5. Not store hospital passwords in personal password managers unless explicitly approved.
6. Lock or sign out of workstations and mobile devices when leaving them unattended, unless an approved clinical kiosk workflow applies.
7. Report suspected phishing, credential disclosure, unexpected MFA prompts, lost devices, or suspicious login activity immediately.
8. Follow emergency-access procedures rather than borrowing another user’s account.

NIST CSF PR.AT-01 expects personnel to receive awareness and training so they can perform general tasks with cybersecurity risks in mind. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29))

## 9. Exceptions

### 9.1 Permitted exception categories

Exceptions may be approved only for:

- Legacy clinical systems that cannot technically enforce this policy.
- Emergency patient-care workflows where delay would create unacceptable patient-safety risk.
- Medical devices or vendor-managed systems where password settings are contractually or technically constrained.
- Regional systems or federated SPID/CIE services outside direct hospital control.
- Temporary incident-response conditions where normal identity services are unavailable.

### 9.2 Exception requirements

Each exception must include:

- System or account name.
- Business or clinical justification.
- Risk assessment, including patient safety and data protection impact.
- Compensating controls.
- Named risk owner.
- Expiry date, not exceeding 12 months unless renewed.
- DPO consultation where personal or health data risk is material.
- Approval by ICT Manager / RTD delegate and, for high-risk exceptions, General Director / ASL delegated authority.

### 9.3 Minimum compensating controls

Where password requirements cannot be met, the following must be considered:

- MFA where technically possible.
- Network restriction by VLAN, source IP, or VPN group.
- Read-only or least-privilege role.
- Stronger logging and monthly review.
- Shorter account validity period.
- Vaulted password and restricted disclosure.
- Rotation after each use.
- Supplier remediation plan or procurement replacement roadmap.

NIST CSF ID.RA-07 expects changes and exceptions to be managed, assessed for risk impact, recorded, and tracked. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29))

## 10. Monitoring, Evidence, and Review

### 10.1 Monitoring activities

The ICT Manager and MSP must maintain evidence of:

| Frequency | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Active privileged accounts, new/deleted admin accounts, failed privileged logins, VPN users without MFA, supplier accounts, generic accounts, and password-vault access report. |
| Quarterly | Review of privileged access rights, supplier access, service accounts, emergency accounts, clinical kiosk exceptions, and compromised-credential checks. |
| Semi-annually | Review of password policy technical settings against this policy and MSP contractual obligations. |
| Annually | Full policy review, password controls audit, exception renewal, and training content update. |
| After incident | Forced password changes, vault rotations, supplier credential review, and lessons learned. |

NIST CSF DE.CM includes monitoring personnel activity, technology usage, and external service-provider activity; GV.PO-02 expects policy review and updates to reflect changes in requirements, threats, technology, and mission. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.CSWP.29))

### 10.2 Key performance indicators

The following KPIs must be reported to management quarterly:

- Percentage of VPN users protected by MFA.
- Number of active privileged accounts.
- Number of privileged accounts without MFA.
- Number of generic/shared accounts and approved exceptions.
- Number of legacy systems unable to meet password baseline.
- Number of supplier accounts active beyond approved dates.
- Number of emergency/break-glass uses and whether reviewed.
- Number of suspected or confirmed credential compromises.
- Percentage of staff completing password/phishing module.
- Number of systems with local administrator passwords managed by LAPS or equivalent.

### 10.3 Audit retention

Password and authentication logs used for security monitoring must be retained according to the hospital logging and privacy policy. At minimum, privileged account creation/deletion, VPN access, emergency account use, and password vault access logs must be retained for 12 months unless a legal, incident, or ASL retention requirement requires longer. This specific 12-month retention target is a local auditability decision — **unsupported by a specific external clause** — and must be reviewed with the DPO.

## 11. Non-Compliance

Non-compliance may result in:

- Immediate account suspension or password reset.
- Mandatory retraining.
- Removal of privileged access.
- Supplier contract escalation.
- HR or disciplinary action for intentional sharing, misuse, or concealment.
- Incident or personal data breach assessment where patient or personal data may be affected.

Any enforcement action must consider patient safety, proportionality, labour law, professional obligations, and data-protection requirements.
