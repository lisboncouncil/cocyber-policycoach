## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to protect patient safety, continuity of care, confidentiality, integrity and availability of hospital information systems and data, including special-category health data, minors’ personal data and employee personal data.

This policy establishes minimum cybersecurity governance, technical and operational requirements for the hospital’s clinical, administrative and outsourced ICT environment. It supports compliance with GDPR security and breach obligations, Italian data protection law, NIS2 as implemented in Italy by D.Lgs. 138/2024, AGID security measures for public administrations, Garante Privacy healthcare guidance, and the hospital’s longer-term target of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 alignment. GDPR Article 32 requires security measures appropriate to risk, including confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience and ability to restore access to personal data in a timely manner. ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN-DE-ES/ALL/?from=EN&uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679&utm_source=openai))

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- all workforce members: clinical, administrative, technical/facility staff, temporary staff, trainees, consultants and volunteers;
- all hospital-owned or hospital-managed ICT assets, including approximately 220 workstations, 35 legacy Windows clinical kiosks, 60 point-of-care mobile devices, servers, network equipment, backup media and removable media;
- all clinical and administrative systems, including Cartella Clinica Elettronica / regional FSE access, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, Pharmacy management, Active Directory, VPN, email, file shares, NAS backup, off-site tape backup and UTM;
- all third-party systems and services used to provide or support hospital services, including the MSP, medical device vendors, regional platforms and suppliers accessed through MEPA/CONSIP procurement;
- all information processed by or for the hospital, whether digital, paper, verbal, imaging, laboratory, pharmacy, administrative or log data.

Patient safety has priority. Cybersecurity controls must be designed so that they do not unreasonably impede emergency clinical workflows. Where strict controls may delay urgent care, controlled “break-glass” procedures with logging and post-event review must be used.

## 4. Policy Principles

1. **Patient safety and continuity of care first.** Security controls must reduce cyber risk without preventing emergency treatment.
2. **Risk-based and proportionate control selection.** Controls must reflect hospital size, 24/7 clinical operations, limited internal IT staffing, legacy clinical kiosks, current budget and NIS2 proportionality requirements. ([gazzettaufficiale.it](https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/atto/serie_generale/caricaArticolo?art.codiceRedazionale=24G00155&art.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=2024-10-01&art.flagTipoArticolo=0&art.idArticolo=24&art.idGruppo=4&art.idSottoArticolo=1&art.idSottoArticolo1=10&art.progressivo=0&art.versione=1))
3. **Least privilege and need-to-know access.** Users receive only the access necessary for their clinical, administrative or support role.
4. **Defence in depth.** No single security tool is sufficient; the hospital will combine identity controls, segmentation, backup, monitoring, training and incident response.
5. **Privacy and security by design/default.** New or changed systems must consider data minimisation, confidentiality, access control, logging and resilience from the start, consistent with GDPR Article 25 and Article 32.
6. **No-blame reporting culture.** Staff must report suspected phishing, malware, lost devices, misdirected data or unsafe practices immediately; timely reporting is encouraged and will not be penalised when made in good faith.
7. **Evidence-based compliance.** Controls must produce auditable evidence: inventories, access reviews, logs, test results, incident records, training completion and supplier security records.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

### 5.1 Hospital Management / Direzione

Hospital Management is accountable for cybersecurity governance and risk acceptance. It shall:

- approve this policy and major exceptions;
- assign resources within the annual cybersecurity budget and public procurement constraints;
- ensure NIS2, GDPR and AGID obligations are addressed;
- review cyber risk status at least quarterly;
- ensure the hospital has a named cybersecurity operational coordinator even where a formal CISO is not yet appointed.

**References:** NIS2 Directive Article 21; D.Lgs. 138/2024 Article 24; NIST CSF 2.0 Govern function; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 5, 6, 9 and Annex A 5.1, 5.2, 5.4.

### 5.2 IT Responsible Officer / Cybersecurity Coordinator

Until a CISO is appointed, the internal IT responsible person acts as Cybersecurity Coordinator. This role shall:

- maintain the ICT asset inventory, risk register and remediation plan;
- coordinate the MSP and clinical system vendors;
- monitor implementation of this policy;
- manage technical exceptions and compensating controls;
- coordinate cyber incident handling with the MSP, DPO, Medical Directorate and Management;
- maintain evidence for audits and regulatory requests.

**References:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.2, 5.4, 5.8, 5.9; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RR, GV.RM, ID.AM.

### 5.3 Data Protection Officer

The DPO shall advise on GDPR, Italian data protection law, healthcare privacy measures, DPIAs, data breach risk assessments, patient access-log requests and communications with the Garante where required. The DPO must be involved promptly in any suspected personal data breach.

**References:** GDPR Articles 25, 32, 33, 34; Garante Privacy healthcare dossier guidance requires controls over dossier access and patient visibility of access events. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/garante/doc.jsp?ID=4084632))

### 5.4 Managed Service Provider

The MSP shall implement and operate assigned technical controls under contract, including patching, endpoint protection, firewall/UTM administration, backup support, incident escalation, VPN administration and log provision. The MSP must use named accounts, MFA for remote and privileged access, secure remote access methods, agreed incident notification timelines and maintain audit evidence.

**References:** NIS2 / D.Lgs. 138/2024 supply-chain requirements; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.19–5.23, 5.37, 8.2, 8.15.

### 5.5 Medical Directorate and Department Heads

Clinical leadership shall ensure that security controls and downtime procedures are compatible with safe clinical practice. Department heads shall ensure staff complete training, follow access procedures and report incidents.

**References:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.4, 6.3, 6.8; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.RR, PR.AT.

### 5.6 All Workforce Members

All users shall:

- use only assigned accounts and approved devices;
- protect credentials and badges;
- report suspected phishing, malware, device loss or data disclosure immediately;
- access patient information only for authorised care or administrative purposes;
- comply with this policy, related procedures and privacy requirements.

**References:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 5.10, 5.15–5.18, 6.3, 6.8.

## 7. Implementation Priorities

Given the stated resource envelope, the following priorities shall guide annual planning:

1. **First priority — ransomware resilience:** backup hardening, restore testing, endpoint protection, patching, MFA for VPN/admin, and incident runbooks.
2. **Second priority — identity and supplier risk:** privileged access control, MSP security clauses, quarterly access reviews and vendor remote-access control.
3. **Third priority — monitoring uplift:** central log collection from AD, UTM/VPN, AV, backup and critical servers before procurement of a full SIEM.
4. **Fourth priority — legacy risk reduction:** isolate and progressively replace or contain legacy Windows clinical kiosks.
5. **Fifth priority — staff resilience:** targeted training for high-turnover clinical groups and phishing reporting.

This prioritisation is an organisational risk-treatment decision intended to meet NIS2 proportionality, GDPR risk-based security and AGID baseline expectations within the available annual cybersecurity and consulting budget.

## 8. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy are permitted only where justified by clinical safety, technical infeasibility, vendor constraint, procurement delay or disproportionate cost.

Each exception must include:

- affected system, data and owner;
- reason for non-compliance;
- risk assessment and patient-safety impact;
- compensating controls;
- approval by the IT Responsible Officer and relevant business/clinical owner;
- DPO review where personal data or health data is affected;
- expiry date not exceeding 12 months, or 6 months for legacy clinical kiosk exceptions;
- remediation plan.

High-risk exceptions affecting EHR/FSE access, ADT/PS, PACS/RIS, LIS, Pharmacy, Active Directory, VPN, backups or MSP access require Management approval.

**References:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 6.1.3, 8.3, 9.1; Annex A 5.8, 5.36; D.Lgs. 138/2024 Art. 24(4).

## 9. Monitoring, Metrics and Review

The IT Responsible Officer shall maintain a monthly cybersecurity dashboard for Management and quarterly steering review. At minimum, the dashboard shall include:

- number of critical/high vulnerabilities open beyond target;
- endpoint protection coverage percentage;
- backup success rate and last restore-test result;
- MFA coverage for VPN, privileged and MSP accounts;
- number of open exceptions and overdue exceptions;
- access reviews completed;
- incidents and near misses reported;
- phishing/training completion rate;
- legacy kiosk count and containment status;
- critical supplier security issues.

This policy must be reviewed annually and after major incidents, serious audit findings, significant legal/regulatory changes, major clinical system changes, or changes to the MSP contract.

**References:** D.Lgs. 138/2024 Art. 24(2)(f); NIST CSF GV.OV, ID.IM; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses 9 and 10; Annex A 5.36.

## 10. Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with this policy may result in corrective action, access suspension, disciplinary action under applicable employment rules, supplier contractual remedies, or regulatory notification where required. Good-faith reporting of incidents, mistakes or suspected phishing is encouraged and will not be treated as misconduct solely because the user reported it.
