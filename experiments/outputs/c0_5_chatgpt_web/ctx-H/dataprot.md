## 2. Purpose

This policy defines how the Hospital protects personal data, with particular emphasis on health data, minors’ data, employee data, and data processed through clinical systems including the regional Electronic Health Record / FSE, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, pharmacy management, point-of-care mobile devices, and administrative systems.

The policy is designed for a small 24/7 public hospital with limited internal IT capacity and a managed services provider. Controls are therefore risk-based, auditable, and implementable using existing platforms wherever possible: on-premises Active Directory, regional SPID/CIE federation, the UTM firewall/VPN, AV management console, existing backup media, MSP support, and low-cost governance measures.

This policy supports compliance with GDPR, the Italian Data Protection Code, health-sector measures issued by the Garante, AGID minimum ICT security measures for public administrations, NIS2 obligations for the health sector, and recognised references including NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 / ISO/IEC 27002:2022. GDPR requires appropriate technical and organisational measures proportionate to risk, including confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, restore capability, and regular testing; NIS2 Article 21 similarly requires risk-management measures including incident handling, business continuity, supply-chain security, cyber hygiene, cryptography, access control, asset management, and MFA/secure communications. ([auditfront.com](https://www.auditfront.com/frameworks/gdpr/controller-obligations/art-32/?utm_source=openai))

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 In scope

This policy applies to:

1. All Hospital staff: clinical, administrative, technical/facility, temporary, agency, trainees, volunteers, and consultants.
2. All personal data processed by or on behalf of the Hospital, including:
   - patient identification and contact data;
   - health data and clinical records;
   - personal data of minors;
   - emergency department / first-aid records;
   - diagnostic images and radiology data;
   - laboratory data;
   - pharmacy and medication data;
   - employee personal data;
   - access logs, audit logs, and security logs containing personal data.
3. All systems and infrastructure used to process such data:
   - regional FSE / Cartella Clinica Elettronica;
   - PACS/RIS;
   - LIS;
   - ADT;
   - pharmacy system;
   - Active Directory and identity federation;
   - workstations, including legacy Windows clinical kiosks;
   - point-of-care mobile devices;
   - network, VPN, UTM, backup NAS, off-site tapes;
   - MSP-managed tools and services.
4. All data processing by suppliers, processors, sub-processors, MSPs, cloud or hosted service providers, medical device vendors, and regional platforms where the Hospital acts as controller, joint controller, processor, or user of a regional service.

### 3.2 Out of scope

This policy does not replace:

- clinical care protocols;
- medical record retention laws;
- regional FSE rules;
- incident response runbooks;
- acceptable use, access control, backup, or supplier policies.

Where there is a conflict, emergency patient safety takes priority, but privacy and security controls must be restored and documented as soon as clinically safe.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

### 5.1 Hospital Management / Direzione Strategica

Hospital Management shall:

1. Approve this policy and allocate proportionate resources to maintain essential data protection controls.
2. Ensure data protection risk is considered in clinical, administrative, procurement, and ICT decisions.
3. Accept or reject significant residual risks, including risks relating to legacy Windows kiosks, limited segmentation, backup recovery gaps, and supplier dependencies.
4. Ensure NIS2-relevant cybersecurity governance and risk-management measures are documented and reviewed. NIS2 Article 21 requires essential and important entities to implement appropriate and proportionate risk-management measures, including policies, incident handling, business continuity, supply-chain security, security in acquisition/development, cyber hygiene, encryption, access control, asset management, and MFA/secure communications. ([nis-2-directive.com](https://www.nis-2-directive.com/NIS_2_Directive_Article_21.html?utm_source=openai))

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5(2), 24, 25, 32; NIS2 Art. 21; NIST CSF 2.0 GV.OC, GV.RM, GV.RR, GV.PO, GV.OV; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clauses 5, 6, 9; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.1, 5.2, 5.4.

### 5.2 DPO/RPD

The DPO/RPD shall:

1. Advise the Hospital on GDPR, Italian data protection law, Garante measures, FSE privacy obligations, and privacy-by-design requirements.
2. Monitor compliance with this policy and applicable privacy rules.
3. Advise on DPIAs for high-risk processing, including new clinical systems, major FSE integrations, telemedicine, remote access, AI-enabled services, biometric systems, or large-scale monitoring.
4. Be involved promptly in all suspected personal data breaches.
5. Act as contact point for the Garante and data subjects.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 37–39; GDPR Art. 35; NIST CSF GV.RR, GV.OV; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.34, 5.35.

### 5.3 IT Responsible

The IT Responsible shall:

1. Maintain technical controls required by this policy, with support from the MSP.
2. Maintain inventories of workstations, servers, network devices, clinical kiosks, mobile devices, applications, and backup assets.
3. Ensure access control, logging, backup, patching, malware protection, and secure configuration are implemented in line with this policy.
4. Escalate risks and incidents to Hospital Management and the DPO/RPD.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(a), (b), (c), (e), (i), (j); NIST CSF ID.AM, PR.AA, PR.DS, PR.PS, PR.IR, DE.CM, RS.MA, RC.RP; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.9, 5.14, 5.15, 5.16, 5.18, 8.8, 8.13, 8.15, 8.16.

### 5.4 MSP

The MSP shall:

1. Process personal data only under written instructions and contractual obligations.
2. Maintain agreed security controls, including privileged access protection, remote access logging, patch support, backup support, malware console monitoring, and incident escalation.
3. Notify the Hospital immediately of actual or suspected incidents affecting Hospital systems or data.
4. Support evidence collection for GDPR, NIS2, AGID, internal audit, and Garante enquiries.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 28, 32, 33; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d); NIST CSF GV.SC, PR.AA, DE.CM, RS.CO; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.19, 5.20, 5.21, 5.22.

### 5.5 Clinical and Administrative Managers

Managers shall:

1. Approve access rights for staff in their units based on job role and need to know.
2. Ensure staff complete onboarding privacy and cybersecurity training before unsupervised system access.
3. Notify the DPO/RPD and IT Responsible of role changes, departures, suspected inappropriate access, data quality errors, or data disclosures.
4. Ensure emergency access is clinically justified and documented.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5(1)(c), 5(1)(f), 24, 32; NIST CSF PR.AA, PR.AT; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.15, 5.18, 6.3, 6.5.

### 5.6 All Workforce Members

All workforce members shall:

1. Access patient or employee data only when required for assigned duties.
2. Protect credentials, badges, devices, printouts, and screens from unauthorised access.
3. Report suspected phishing, lost devices, wrong-recipient disclosures, malware, ransomware indicators, or inappropriate access immediately.
4. Never export, photograph, message, or copy patient data through unauthorised channels.
5. Respect patient rights, FSE consent/visibility rules, and minors’ confidentiality requirements.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5, 9, 32; Garante FSE and dossier sanitario guidance; NIST CSF PR.AT, PR.AA, DE.CM; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.10, 6.3, 6.8.

## 6. Data Protection Principles

The Hospital shall process personal data according to the following principles:

1. **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency.** Processing must have a valid legal basis and clear information must be provided to patients, staff, and other data subjects.
2. **Purpose limitation.** Data shall be collected for specified healthcare, administrative, legal, employment, public health, or institutional purposes and not reused incompatibly.
3. **Data minimisation.** Only data necessary for the stated purpose shall be collected, accessed, shared, or retained.
4. **Accuracy.** Clinical and administrative data must be kept accurate and corrected where errors are identified.
5. **Storage limitation.** Data shall be retained only for required clinical, medico-legal, public-interest, employment, or statutory periods.
6. **Integrity and confidentiality.** Data must be protected against unauthorised or unlawful processing, accidental loss, destruction, or damage.
7. **Accountability.** The Hospital must be able to demonstrate compliance through documented decisions, records, logs, training evidence, supplier files, DPIAs, and incident records.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 5; GDPR Arts. 24, 25, 30, 32; NIST CSF GV.PO, GV.OV, PR.DS; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.31, 5.34, 5.35.

## 7. Policy Requirements and Controls

### DP-01 — Record of Processing Activities

The DPO/RPD, with unit managers and IT support, shall maintain a Record of Processing Activities (“RoPA”) covering at minimum: patient care, emergency first aid, FSE interactions, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, pharmacy, billing/administration, HR, staff scheduling, CCTV/security where applicable, backups, logs, and supplier processing.

The RoPA shall identify: purpose, categories of data subjects, categories of personal data, special-category data, recipients, processors, transfers, retention, systems used, security measures, and responsible owner. The RoPA shall be reviewed at least annually and before any new clinical system or major supplier change.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 30; GDPR Arts. 5(2), 24; NIST CSF GV.OC, ID.AM; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.9, 5.12, 5.34. Article 30 requires controllers to maintain records of processing activities. ([dataprotection.ie](https://dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/records-of-processing-article-30-guidance?utm_source=openai))

---

### DP-02 — Lawful Basis and Special-Category Health Data

All processing shall have a documented lawful basis under GDPR Article 6 and, for health data, an Article 9 condition. For ordinary clinical care, the usual basis shall be provision of healthcare, medical diagnosis, treatment, health system management, public interest in public health, legal obligations, or public task, as applicable. Consent shall not be used as the default legal basis for necessary public-hospital clinical care unless specifically required by law or FSE rules.

Data of minors shall be processed with heightened confidentiality, role-based access, and age-appropriate communication where relevant. Sensitive categories requiring special anonymity or “oscuramento” under health/FSE rules shall follow the stricter FSE or Garante requirement.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 6, 9, 12–14; Italian Data Protection Code; Garante FSE guidance; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 control 5.34. The Garante states that FSE information notices must be clear and include GDPR Article 13 elements and that FSE data relates to the patient’s health. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/temi/fse))

---

### DP-03 — Privacy Notices and Patient Rights

The Hospital shall provide clear privacy information in Italian to patients, legal guardians, staff, and other data subjects. Notices shall be available at reception/emergency first-aid areas, wards, administrative offices, intranet, and where practical through digital channels.

Patients shall be informed of rights relevant to FSE and local dossier use, including access, rectification, limits/objection where applicable, FSE consultation consent/revocation where applicable, obscuring of documents, and the ability to see access logs where applicable. Requests shall be logged and routed to the DPO/RPD or Privacy Office.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 12–15, 16–22; Garante FSE guidance; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 control 5.34. The Garante states that the FSE information notice must include the right to obscuring and the right to know which accesses were made to the FSE; it also states that patients may consult the list of accesses because FSE operations are logged. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/temi/fse))

---

### DP-04 — Data Subject Request Handling

The Hospital shall maintain a simple request-handling workflow:

1. Requests received by wards, reception, clinical staff, or administration shall be forwarded to the Privacy Office/DPO within one working day.
2. Identity and authority of the requester shall be verified before disclosure.
3. Requests involving minors, guardianship, deceased patients, psychiatric/paediatric contexts, or conflicts between relatives shall be escalated to the DPO/RPD and Medical Director.
4. Responses shall be tracked, including date received, verification performed, decision, response date, and records provided/refused.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 12–23; Italian Data Protection Code; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 control 5.34.

---

### DP-05 — Data Protection Impact Assessments

A DPIA shall be performed before introducing or materially changing processing likely to create high risk, including:

- new or substantially changed EHR/FSE integrations;
- new PACS/RIS/LIS/ADT/pharmacy platforms;
- telemedicine or remote monitoring;
- AI-assisted clinical decision support;
- large-scale analytics or secondary use of health data;
- biometric access;
- new cloud or MSP-hosted clinical services;
- systematic monitoring of staff or patients;
- major changes to emergency access, logging, or patient visibility controls.

The DPIA shall include processing description, necessity/proportionality assessment, risks to rights and freedoms, technical/organisational controls, residual risk, DPO advice, and management approval.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 35; GDPR Arts. 25, 32, 36; NIST CSF GV.RM, ID.RA; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.4, 5.34, 8.27.

---

### DP-06 — Access Control and Least Privilege

Access to systems containing personal data shall be role-based and limited to the minimum necessary for duties. The IT Responsible and MSP shall maintain standard access profiles for: emergency first-aid, internal medicine, surgery, paediatrics, long-term care, radiology, laboratory, pharmacy, administration, IT/MSP, and management.

Minimum requirements:

1. Unique user accounts are mandatory; shared accounts are prohibited except documented, technically unavoidable clinical kiosk scenarios with compensating controls.
2. New access requires manager approval.
3. Privileged access requires IT Responsible approval and shall be separate from normal user accounts.
4. Departing staff accounts shall be disabled within 24 hours of HR/manager notification; urgent terminations shall be disabled immediately.
5. Access rights shall be reviewed quarterly for high-risk systems: EHR/FSE, ADT, PACS/RIS, LIS, pharmacy, VPN, and privileged AD groups.
6. Legacy Windows kiosks shall be restricted to required clinical applications, network destinations, and physical locations.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(i), (j); NIST CSF PR.AA; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.15, 5.16, 5.17, 5.18, 8.2, 8.3, 8.5. NIS2 Article 21 includes human resources security, access control policies, asset management, and MFA/continuous authentication among risk-management measures. ([glocertinternational.com](https://www.glocertinternational.com/resources/guides/nis2-article-21-risk-management-measures-explained/?utm_source=openai))

### DP-07 — Authentication and MFA

MFA shall be required for:

1. VPN access;
2. privileged administrator accounts;
3. MSP remote access;
4. externally accessible administrative portals;
5. FSE/regional services where supported by the regional platform;
6. email or collaboration services if technically available under current licensing.

Where MFA is not technically feasible for legacy clinical kiosks, the Hospital shall apply compensating controls: kiosk lockdown, local physical supervision, no internet browsing except allow-listed clinical sites, network restrictions, session timeout where clinically safe, and audit logging.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(j); NIST CSF PR.AA-03, PR.AA-05; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.17, 8.5.

---

### DP-08 — Emergency Access / Break-Glass

Emergency access is permitted only where necessary for patient safety, continuity of emergency care, or immediate risk to life or health. Emergency access shall:

1. be technically logged;
2. require the user to select or record an emergency reason where supported;
3. be reviewed retrospectively by the clinical unit manager or delegated privacy contact;
4. be reported to the DPO/RPD if suspicious, excessive, or unjustified;
5. never be used for convenience, curiosity, family/acquaintance lookup, or administrative shortcuts.

Clinical workflow must not be blocked in emergency first-aid, ADT/PS, or life-threatening contexts. Where privacy controls fail open for patient safety, the event shall be logged and reviewed.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5(1)(f), 9(2)(c), 9(2)(h), 32; Garante FSE emergency access guidance; NIST CSF PR.AA, DE.CM; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.15, 8.15. The Garante states that FSE can be accessed in emergency by healthcare personnel for strictly necessary care, except for obscured data/documents, and only for the time necessary to provide emergency care. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/temi/fse))

### DP-09 — Logging and Monitoring

The Hospital shall maintain audit logs for access to EHR/FSE, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, pharmacy, VPN, privileged AD activity, backup jobs, AV alerts, and UTM security events.

Given the absence of a SIEM/SOC, the following pragmatic monitoring model shall apply:

1. AV console alerts: checked daily on working days by IT/MSP; critical alerts escalated immediately.
2. UTM/VPN logs: reviewed weekly for failed logins, unusual remote access, and denied traffic to clinical networks.
3. Clinical access logs: sampled monthly for high-risk access patterns, including VIP/local staff records, paediatric records, emergency access, and access outside shift patterns where reports are available.
4. Privileged account logs: reviewed monthly and after each major change.
5. Logs shall be retained for a period defined in the retention schedule, balancing legal/security needs and data minimisation.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5(1)(f), 30, 32; Garante FSE/dossier guidance; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(b), (f); NIST CSF DE.CM, DE.AE; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 8.15, 8.16, 5.28. The Garante states that all FSE operations are logged and that patients can consult access lists; the Garante dossier guidance highlights the need to protect against unauthorised access and data breach risks. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/temi/fse))

---

### DP-10 — Data Security, Encryption, and Mobile Devices

The Hospital shall protect personal data at rest and in transit using proportionate controls:

1. Point-of-care mobile devices shall use device encryption, screen lock, remote wipe where available, and asset registration.
2. Laptops and removable media used for personal data shall be encrypted.
3. Unencrypted USB drives are prohibited for patient data unless explicitly approved as an exception for a documented emergency or legal transfer.
4. Email transmission of patient data shall be minimised; where necessary, messages shall use approved institutional accounts and encryption or secure portal where available.
5. Data transfers to regional systems, suppliers, or public authorities shall use approved secure channels.
6. Backups containing personal data shall be protected from unauthorised access and physically secured, including off-site tape handling.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32(1)(a); NIS2 Art. 21(2)(h); NIST CSF PR.DS; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 8.24, 8.12, 7.10, 5.14. GDPR Article 32 includes pseudonymisation/encryption, confidentiality, integrity, availability, resilience, restore capability, and regular testing as examples of appropriate security measures. ([auditfront.com](https://www.auditfront.com/frameworks/gdpr/controller-obligations/art-32/?utm_source=openai))

---

### DP-11 — Data Minimisation, Local Storage, and Exports

Personal data shall not be downloaded, exported, printed, photographed, copied to spreadsheets, copied to local drives, or transferred to messaging apps unless necessary for a documented clinical, administrative, legal, or operational purpose.

Specific rules:

1. Clinical staff shall use official clinical systems rather than local notes or shadow files.
2. Local exports from EHR/FSE, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, or pharmacy systems require manager approval unless part of routine authorised workflows.
3. Bulk extraction of patient data requires DPO/RPD review and, where high risk, DPIA or documented risk assessment.
4. Screenshots or photos of patient data on personal phones are prohibited.
5. Paper printouts shall be collected immediately, stored securely, and disposed of in confidential waste.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5(1)(b), 5(1)(c), 5(1)(e), 25, 32; NIST CSF PR.DS; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.10, 5.12, 5.14, 7.7, 8.12.

---

### DP-12 — Data Quality and Clinical Integrity

Clinical and administrative users shall ensure patient identifiers, clinical documentation, diagnostic results, prescriptions, and discharge/transfer data are accurate and entered into the correct patient record. Suspected record mismatch, duplicate patient identity, wrong-patient result, or incorrect upload to FSE shall be treated as a patient safety and data protection issue.

Corrections shall follow clinical governance rules, preserve audit trails, and avoid deletion of clinically relevant history unless legally authorised.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 5(1)(d); GDPR Art. 16; NIST CSF PR.DS; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.33, 8.15.

---

### DP-13 — Retention and Secure Disposal

The Hospital shall maintain a retention schedule covering clinical records, diagnostic images, laboratory reports, pharmacy records, emergency records, administrative documents, HR files, logs, backups, and supplier records. Retention shall follow applicable Italian health, public administration, medico-legal, employment, archival, and regional FSE requirements.

Disposal shall be secure:

1. paper records through locked confidential waste bins or certified destruction;
2. storage media through secure wiping or physical destruction;
3. decommissioned mobile devices through wipe and asset-register update;
4. backup tapes according to lifecycle and documented destruction/return process.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 5(1)(e), 17, 30, 32; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.33, 7.14, 8.10, 8.11. The Garante notes that FSE index data/documents are subject to specific retention rules, including deletion after a defined period following the patient’s death. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/temi/fse))

---

### DP-14 — Backup, Restore, and Continuity for Personal Data

The Hospital shall ensure personal data required for clinical care remains available during incidents.

Minimum requirements:

1. Nightly NAS backups shall be monitored for success/failure.
2. Weekly off-site tape backups shall continue until replaced by an approved equivalent.
3. At least one backup copy shall be protected from ransomware through offline, immutable, or otherwise isolated storage.
4. EHR restore target shall support the 4-hour RTO; ADT/PS restore target shall support the 1-hour RTO where technically feasible with regional dependencies.
5. A restore test shall be performed at least annually, with priority on ADT/PS and EHR access, and a tabletop ransomware recovery exercise shall be performed at least annually.
6. Given the last DR test was 18 months ago, the next test shall be scheduled within 90 days of this policy’s effective date.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32(1)(b), 32(1)(c), 32(1)(d); NIS2 Art. 21(2)(c); NIST CSF PR.IR, RC.RP, RC.CO; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 8.13, 5.29, 5.30. NIS2 Article 21 includes business continuity, backup management, disaster recovery, and crisis management among required risk-management areas. ([glocertinternational.com](https://www.glocertinternational.com/resources/guides/nis2-article-21-risk-management-measures-explained/?utm_source=openai))

---

### DP-15 — Network and Endpoint Protection

The Hospital shall maintain baseline network and endpoint protections proportionate to its size and risk:

1. Clinical, administrative, PACS/RIS, server, backup, and guest networks shall remain logically separated.
2. Firewall rules between clinical, administrative, PACS, and backup networks shall be reviewed at least annually and after major system changes.
3. Legacy Windows clinical kiosks shall be isolated as far as technically possible, restricted to required clinical destinations, blocked from email and general browsing where possible, and prioritised in the replacement plan.
4. All supported endpoints shall run centrally monitored anti-malware.
5. Critical patches shall be applied based on risk, clinical availability, and vendor certification constraints.
6. Medical device vendor remote access shall be disabled by default and enabled only for approved support windows where feasible.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(e), (i); AGID minimum ICT security measures; NIST CSF PR.PS, PR.IR, DE.CM; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 8.7, 8.8, 8.9, 8.20, 8.21, 8.22. AGID describes its minimum ICT security measures for public administrations as a practical reference to assess and improve security and counter common cyber threats, implementable gradually based on organisational complexity. ([agid.gov.it](https://www.agid.gov.it/it/sicurezza/misure-minime-sicurezza-ict?utm_source=openai))

---

### DP-16 — Supplier, MSP, and Medical Device Vendor Data Protection

Before new suppliers process personal data or access systems containing personal data, the Hospital shall ensure:

1. data processing agreement or equivalent Article 28 terms are in place;
2. supplier security responsibilities are documented;
3. remote access method, MFA, logging, and support window rules are defined;
4. sub-processors are declared where required;
5. breach notification obligations are contractually defined;
6. exit/return/deletion obligations are defined;
7. procurement files include basic security/privacy evaluation proportionate to the contract value and risk.

For MEPA/CONSIP or regional procurement, privacy and security requirements shall be included as technical specifications where legally and operationally possible.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 28, 32; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(d); NIST CSF GV.SC; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.19, 5.20, 5.21, 5.22, 5.23. AGID’s ICT procurement security guidance is intended for public administrations and suppliers to ensure ICT goods and services acquired through public procurement have appropriate security levels. ([agid.gov.it](https://www.agid.gov.it/it/agenzia/stampa-e-comunicazione/notizie/2020/05/20/sicurezza-procurement-ict-online-linee-guida?utm_source=openai))

### DP-17 — Personal Data Breach and Cyber Incident Handling

All suspected personal data breaches or cyber incidents shall be reported immediately to the IT Responsible, DPO/RPD, and MSP service desk. Examples include ransomware, malware, lost mobile device, misdirected email, inappropriate patient-record access, stolen credentials, unauthorised FSE access, supplier compromise, or lost paper records.

The Hospital shall maintain a breach register and an incident runbook including:

1. triage and containment;
2. patient safety impact assessment;
3. personal data risk assessment;
4. evidence preservation;
5. DPO/RPD assessment of Garante notification;
6. communication to affected data subjects where required;
7. NIS2 incident reporting pathway where applicable;
8. post-incident corrective actions.

GDPR supervisory authority notification shall be assessed without undue delay and, where required, made within 72 hours of awareness. NIS2 significant-incident reporting shall follow the applicable national CSIRT/authority process, using the NIS2 sequence of early warning, incident notification, and final report where applicable.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 33, 34; NIS2 Art. 23; NIS2 Art. 21(2)(b); NIST CSF RS.MA, RS.AN, RS.CO, RS.MI, RC.CO; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.24, 5.25, 5.26, 5.27, 5.28. GDPR Article 33 requires breach notification to the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours when notification is required; NIS2 Article 23 introduces significant-incident reporting obligations for entities in scope. ([recordinglaw.com](https://www.recordinglaw.com/world-laws/world-data-privacy-laws/eu-data-privacy-laws/gdpr-breach-notification-72-hour-rule/?utm_source=openai))

### DP-18 — Training and Awareness

All workforce members shall complete at least two hours per year of combined privacy and cybersecurity training. Given limited training time, training shall focus on the highest risks:

1. phishing and credential theft;
2. ransomware indicators and immediate reporting;
3. appropriate access to patient records;
4. FSE/dossier confidentiality and emergency access;
5. handling minors’ data;
6. secure use of mobile devices;
7. misdirected email and paper disclosure prevention;
8. reporting lost devices and suspected breaches.

New starters shall receive a short privacy/security briefing before access to clinical systems. High-turnover clinical units shall use a 15-minute onboarding checklist plus annual e-learning or classroom refresher.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32(4); NIS2 Art. 21(2)(g); NIST CSF PR.AT; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 6.3, 6.6.

---

### DP-19 — Physical Protection of Personal Data

Patient data shall be protected in clinical and administrative areas:

1. screens in public/patient areas shall be positioned or privacy-filtered where feasible;
2. unattended workstations shall be locked unless clinically unsafe;
3. printed patient lists, labels, prescriptions, and handover sheets shall be controlled and disposed of securely;
4. point-of-care mobile devices shall be stored securely when not in use;
5. areas containing servers, network equipment, backup NAS, tapes, or paper archives shall be access-controlled.

**Audit references:** GDPR Art. 32; NIST CSF PR.DS, PR.PS; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 7.1, 7.2, 7.7, 7.8, 7.9, 7.10, 7.14.

---

### DP-20 — Secondary Use, Research, Audit, and Reporting

Use of patient data for research, quality improvement, audit, public health, epidemiology, management reporting, or regional planning shall be reviewed for lawful basis, minimisation, pseudonymisation/anonymisation, and ethics or institutional approvals where applicable.

Identifiable data shall be used only where necessary. Pseudonymised or aggregated data shall be preferred for internal reporting and governance. External disclosure for research or reporting must be approved by the appropriate institutional process and reviewed by the DPO/RPD where required.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5, 6, 9, 25, 32, 89; Garante FSE guidance; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.34, 8.11, 8.12. The Garante describes FSE purposes including care, scientific research in medical/biomedical/epidemiological fields, governance, prevention, and international prophylaxis, with different access levels and safeguards. ([garanteprivacy.it](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/temi/fse))

## 8. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy are permitted only where:

1. compliance is technically impossible with existing clinical systems;
2. immediate patient safety requires temporary deviation;
3. a regional platform or supplier constraint prevents direct implementation;
4. public procurement timelines make immediate remediation impracticable;
5. the cost is disproportionate to risk and compensating controls are approved.

All exceptions shall be documented with:

- description of exception;
- affected systems/data;
- risk assessment;
- compensating controls;
- owner;
- expiry/review date;
- DPO/RPD opinion where personal data risk is material;
- approval by Hospital Management or delegated authority.

Emergency clinical exceptions may be approved retrospectively but must be documented within five working days.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 24, 25, 32; NIS2 Art. 21 proportionality principle; NIST CSF GV.RM, GV.OV, ID.RA; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clauses 6.1, 8.1, 9.3.

The following minimum evidence shall be maintained:

| Approved Data Protection Policy | DPO/RPD | Annual review |
| RoPA | DPO/RPD / Privacy Office | Annual and on change |
| DPIA register | DPO/RPD | Ongoing |
| Access review records for EHR/FSE, ADT, PACS/RIS, LIS, pharmacy, VPN, privileged AD | IT Responsible / Unit Managers | Quarterly |
| Training completion records | HR / DPO/RPD | Annual |
| Supplier data protection agreements and security schedules | Procurement / DPO/RPD / IT | On contract and renewal |
| Backup success/failure logs | IT/MSP | Daily/weekly operational review |
| Restore test evidence | IT/MSP | Annual minimum |
| AV and UTM review records | IT/MSP | Daily/weekly as defined |
| Breach and incident register | DPO/RPD / IT | Ongoing |
| Exception register | DPO/RPD / IT | Quarterly review |

The policy shall be reviewed at least annually and after:

- ransomware or major security incident;
- personal data breach requiring notification;
- new or replaced EHR/FSE, PACS/RIS, LIS, ADT, or pharmacy system;
- major MSP or supplier change;
- significant Garante, AGID, NIS2, or regional FSE update;
- DR test failure or material continuity issue.

NIST CSF 2.0 is suitable for organisations of any size, sector, or maturity and provides outcomes for governing, identifying, protecting, detecting, responding, and recovering from cybersecurity risk, which is why it is used here as a lightweight governance and audit mapping reference. ## 10. Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with this policy may result in:

1. access suspension;
2. retraining;
3. disciplinary action under employment or professional rules;
4. supplier contract remedies;
5. notification to regulatory or professional authorities where required;
6. patient safety or clinical governance review.

Intentional unauthorised access to patient records, disclosure of health data, credential sharing, or concealment of incidents shall be treated as serious misconduct.

**Audit references:** GDPR Arts. 5(2), 24, 32, 33, 34; Italian Data Protection Code; NIST CSF GV.OV, RS.MA; ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.36, 6.4, 6.5, 5.24–5.28.
