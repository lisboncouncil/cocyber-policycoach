## 1. Purpose
This Access Control Policy establishes the framework for managing and controlling access to the information systems, applications, and data of [Hospital Name], a public hospital in Italy. Its purpose is to ensure that access to information assets is granted based on the principle of least privilege, in strict compliance with the Italian Codice in materia di protezione dei dati personali (D.Lgs. 196/2003 as amended), the EU GDPR (2016/679), the NIS2 Directive, and AGID security guidelines. This policy is a critical component of the hospital's information security management system.

## 2. Scope
This policy applies to all individuals and systems that interact with the hospital's information assets, including:
- All employees, contractors, consultants, and third-party personnel.
- All information systems, applications, and data owned or managed by the hospital.
- All devices, whether hospital-managed or personal (BYOD) when used to access hospital resources.
- All data, with particular emphasis on special category data (health data) as defined by GDPR.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities
- **Hospital Director / Data Controller**: Ultimately responsible for data protection and policy approval.
- **Head of IT / Information Security Officer (ISO)**: Owns this policy, ensures its implementation, and reports on its effectiveness. Acts as the primary authority for access control decisions.
- **IT Department / Managed Service Provider (MSP)**: Implements, configures, and maintains the technical controls (Active Directory, IAM systems) as per this policy. Manages user lifecycle.
- **Department Heads / Ward Managers**: Act as data owners for their domains (e.g., Head of Radiology for PACS). They approve access requests for their areas and conduct periodic access reviews.
- **All Staff (Clinical & Admin)**: Responsible for safeguarding their credentials, reporting suspicious activity, and completing mandatory security training.

## 4. Policy Principles
1.  **Principle of Least Privilege:** Users and systems are granted the minimum level of access—to systems, applications, and data—necessary to perform their job functions. No user should have default or permanent access to sensitive systems without explicit, role-based justification.
2.  **Need-to-Know:** Access to patient data and critical systems is granted strictly on a need-to-know basis, determined by the user's role and current assignment.
3.  **Separation of Duties (SoD):** Critical functions (e.g., requesting access and approving access) are separated to prevent fraud or error.
4.  **Accountability:** All access and actions within systems must be attributable to a unique, named user. Shared or generic accounts are prohibited for individual user activities.
5.  **Lifecycle Management:** Access rights are promptly granted, modified, and revoked in line with the user's employment lifecycle (onboarding, role change, termination).

## 5. Access Control Requirements

### 5.1. User Account Management
*   **Account Creation:** All user accounts (for employees, contractors, third-parties) must be requested via the official **Account Creation and Modification Form (ACMF)**, signed by the requester's department head and approved by the IT department. The MSP/IT team provisions access based on the approved role.
*   **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC):** Access rights are assigned based on predefined roles (e.g., "Nurse - Internal Medicine," "Radiologist," "HR Administrator"). The MSP will maintain the role definitions and associated permissions.
*   **Privileged Accounts:** Administrative or "privileged" accounts (e.g., Domain Admins, System Administrators, Database Admins) are strictly controlled. Their use is logged, and sessions are monitored. Just-in-Time (JIT) or Privileged Access Management (PAM) principles should be explored as the security program matures.
*   **Shared Accounts:** The use of shared accounts (e.g., a generic "nurse" login for a ward) is **prohibited** for accessing systems containing patient data. Exceptions for non-critical, non-patient systems require written approval from the ISO and must include detailed logging of the shared account's use.
*   **Third-Party Access:** All third-party vendors (e.g., MSP, medical device vendors) must use unique, named accounts. Access is time-bound and monitored. All third-party access must be documented and approved by the ISO.

### 5.2. Authentication & Authorization
*   **Strong Authentication:** All access to hospital systems containing patient or sensitive administrative data requires Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA). This is mandatory for remote access (VPN, webmail, cloud apps) and for all privileged accounts.
*   **Password Policy:** Passwords must comply with the hospital's Password Policy (e.g., minimum 12 characters, complexity). The use of a hospital-approved password manager is encouraged.
*   **Federated Identity:** Where possible, leverage the regional SPID/CIE federation for external users (e.g., GPs, external specialists) to access shared platforms, avoiding the creation of local accounts.

### 5.3. Remote Access
*   All remote access to the hospital network (e.g., for MSP, on-call staff) must use the hospital's approved VPN, which enforces MFA.
*   Remote desktop or application access must be secured with MFA and, where possible, use virtual desktop infrastructure (VDI) to prevent data exfiltration.

### 5.4. Access Reviews & Recertification
*   Department Heads, in conjunction with the ISO, must conduct a quarterly review of all user access rights within their domain, focusing on privileged and high-risk accounts.
*   The IT Department/MSP will conduct a semi-annual audit of all user accounts, flagging dormant accounts (inactive >90 days) and accounts with excessive privileges for review and cleanup.
*   Access to critical systems (EHR, PACS, Pharmacy) must be re-certified by the data owner every 6 months.

### 5.5. Access Revocation
*   The HR department must notify IT and the ISO of all staff terminations or role changes **on the day they are effective**.
*   The IT Department/MSP must disable (not delete) all associated accounts and access rights immediately upon notification. A formal de-provisioning checklist must be followed.

## 6. Special Considerations for Clinical Systems
*   **Break-the-Glass (BTG):** In an emergency, clinical staff must be able to override standard access controls to access critical patient data. All such "break-the-glass" events are logged, generate an immediate alert to the ISO, and require mandatory post-incient justification and review.
*   **Legacy Systems:** For legacy clinical systems (e.g., legacy kiosks) that cannot support modern authentication, compensating controls (network segmentation, strict firewall rules, enhanced monitoring) must be documented and approved by the ISO.

## 7. Exceptions
Any exception to this policy requires a documented risk assessment and written approval from the Hospital Director and the ISO. Exceptions are time-bound and subject to review.

## 8. Monitoring and Enforcement
*   The IT Department/MSP will implement logging and monitoring for critical access events (failed logins, privilege escalation, access to sensitive data).
*   All access to patient records in the Electronic Health Record (EHR) and PACS systems will be logged and the logs retained for a minimum of 10 years, as required by Italian law.
*   Violations of this policy will be treated as a disciplinary matter and may result in sanctions, up to and including termination and legal action, in line with Italian employment law (Statuto dei Lavoratori) and the hospital's disciplinary code.
