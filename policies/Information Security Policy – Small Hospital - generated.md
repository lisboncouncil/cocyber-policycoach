# Information Security Policy – Small Hospital (Italy)
by our RAG
# 1. Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to establish a structured framework to protect the hospital’s information systems and sensitive data. The objective is to ensure patient safety, maintain the confidentiality and integrity of healthcare data, and guarantee continuity of clinical and administrative operations.
# 2. Scope
This policy applies to all staff, contractors, and third-party service providers who access or manage the hospital’s information systems. It covers:
* Clinical and administrative IT systems and networks.
* Electronic health records (EHRs) and patient data.
* Medical devices and legacy systems that support patient care.
* Systems and processes required for business continuity in a hospital environment.

⠀Given the hospital’s small scale (10 doctors, 20 nurses, 100 beds, with a turnover of approximately 10 million euros), the measures outlined herein are designed for a resource-constrained but mission-critical environment.
# 3. Governance and Risk Management
### 3.1 Security Governance
* The hospital shall adopt an Information Security Management System (ISMS) that is approved by senior management and overseen by a designated security officer. This governance framework ensures that policies are aligned with the hospital’s risk profile and operational needs fileciteturn2file0.
* Executive leadership, including hospital management, is responsible for ensuring that cybersecurity is integrated into overall risk management and operational strategies. This integration is essential even in smaller environments, where resources are limited, but risks remain significant fileciteturn2file7.

### 3.2 Risk Analysis
* A risk analysis must be conducted periodically to identify vulnerabilities, including outdated systems and potential exposure to attacks such as ransomware, DDoS, and phishing.
* Based on the risk analysis, a set of mitigation measures will be defined and regularly reviewed. This analysis incorporates both technical risks and operational impacts on patient safety fileciteturn2file7.
## 4. Access Control and Authentication
* Access to information systems is granted based on the principles of need-to-know and least privilege. Only authorized staff (e.g., specific doctors, nurses, and administrative personnel) will have access to sensitive data.
* A regular review of user access rights will be conducted, with adjustments made as roles change. Authentication mechanisms must ensure that default credentials are replaced prior to entering operational use fileciteturn2file1.

## 5. Incident Response
* An incident response plan is established to rapidly identify, contain, and remediate any cybersecurity incident. The plan includes procedures for incident logging, analysis, and communication with relevant authorities.
* Regular drills and training sessions will be held to familiarize staff with incident response procedures, in keeping with industry guidance for healthcare organizations fileciteturn2file3.

## 6. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery
* The hospital shall maintain contingency plans and a disaster recovery strategy to ensure that critical services remain available during or after a cybersecurity incident.
* Regular backups of patient data and critical systems will be performed, stored securely, and tested to confirm their integrity and restoration capability. In the event of an incident, defined recovery processes will guide the swift resumption of services fileciteturn2file10.

## 7. Patch Management and IT Security Maintenance
* All hardware, software, and network devices must be kept up-to-date with the latest security patches and updates. A central patch management process is to be established—even in a small hospital setting—to mitigate vulnerabilities.
* Legacy systems must be critically assessed, and if they cannot be updated, compensatory controls must be applied to minimize risk exposure fileciteturn2file2.

## 8. Awareness and Training
* All hospital staff, including doctors, nurses, and administrative personnel, will receive regular cybersecurity awareness and training.
* Training will focus on topics such as phishing, social engineering, password management, and safe handling of sensitive patient information. Regular reminders (e.g., warning banners in emails) are encouraged to reinforce these practices fileciteturn2file3.

## 9. Vendor and Third-Party Management
* Third-party vendors and service providers with access to the hospital’s systems or data must adhere to strict cybersecurity standards.
* Regular audits and assessments will be carried out on vendor security practices to ensure they align with the hospital’s policies and risk management requirements.

## 10. Compliance and Monitoring
* Compliance with this Information Security Policy will be continuously monitored through regular audits and assessments.
* The hospital will ensure adherence to relevant national and EU cybersecurity directives, including provisions of the NIS Directive, and take prompt action to address any compliance gaps.
* Continuous monitoring of security events through logging and periodic assessments will help verify the effectiveness of the implemented controls fileciteturn2file7.
