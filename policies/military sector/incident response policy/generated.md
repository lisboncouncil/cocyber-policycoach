# Incident Response Policy
Military organization
# 1. Purpose
This policy establishes a framework for detecting, reporting, triaging, and responding to cybersecurity incidents within our military organization. Its objective is to mitigate threats to our critical systems, ensure continuity of operations, and comply with national and European guidance on incident management.
# 2. Scope
This policy applies to all Information and Communication Systems (ICS) operated by the organization, including systems used by the 100 personnel in Italy. It covers the incident detection, analysis, containment, recovery, and lessons learned phases. As this organization does not yet have an established Incident Response Team (IRT), this policy also defines interim roles and procedures until such a team is formally designated.
# 3. Definitions
* **Cybersecurity Incident**: Any event or series of events that compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the systems.
* **SIEM**: Security Information and Event Management system used for centralized logging, real-time analysis, and alerting.
* **Triage**: The process of evaluating incident alerts to determine their legitimacy, severity, and required response.
* **Crisis Management**: Policies and procedures to manage large-scale incidents to ensure continuity of operations.

⠀4. Incident Response Phases
### 4.1 Detection and Monitoring
* **Logging and SIEM Integration**: All critical systems must be configured to generate logs capturing events such as user authentications, account management, and configuration changes. These logs must be time-stamped with synchronized sources and archived for at least six months. An automated SIEM system (or similar tool) should be considered to correlate and analyze logs, detect anomalies, and raise alerts for further investigation (see fileciteturn2file0, fileciteturn2file1).
* **Situational Awareness**: Stay updated with threat intelligence from both government and commercial sources to understand relevant cyber threats.

⠀4.2 Triage and Assessment
* **Alert Evaluation**: Any incident alert from the SIEM must be promptly reviewed by designated personnel or specialists. Alerts which are validated as incidents should trigger the incident response procedure (see fileciteturn2file9).
* **Incident Classification**: Determine the scope, severity, and potential impact on critical systems and operations. This classification guides the escalation and response action.

⠀4.3 Response Procedures
* **Containment**: In cases where an incident is confirmed, immediate steps must be taken to contain the threat and prevent further damage. This involves isolating affected systems and cutting off unauthorized access.
* **Eradication and Recovery**: Following containment, efforts are directed toward removing the threat, eradicating malware or vulnerabilities, and restoring systems to normal functioning.
* **Reporting and Communication**:
  * **Internal Reporting**: Document all incident-related information. Reporting procedures must specify who within the organization is to be notified and the communication channels to be used.
  * **External Communication**: If required, coordinate with national Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs) and relevant competent authorities while ensuring that the information sharing does not compromise ongoing incident response efforts (refer to fileciteturn2file3).

⠀4.4 Crisis Management and Escalation
* **Escalation Plan**: For major or catastrophic incidents, activate the crisis management procedures. This includes coordinating with pre-identified contact lists and implementing contingency plans (see fileciteturn2file4).
* **Exercises and Training**: Conduct regular table-top exercises and technical drills to test the effectiveness of incident response and crisis management procedures. Training should include all relevant stakeholders, even if the incident response team is not yet formally established (refer to fileciteturn2file5).

⠀5. Roles and Responsibilities
* **Interim Incident Response Coordinator**: Until a dedicated Incident Response Team is established, a designated officer (or small group) must be responsible for coordinating all incident-related activities.
* **Technical Specialists**: Identify, analyze, and respond to alerts generated by the SIEM and logging systems. These roles are critical for the triage, containment, and recovery phases.
* **Communications Officer**: Oversees internal and external communications during an incident, ensuring timely escalation and appropriate notification in accordance with national directives (see fileciteturn2file8).

⠀6. Reporting and Documentation
* **Incident Report**: For each incident, a detailed report must be created that documents:
  * The incident’s nature, scope, and impact.
  * Actions taken during each phase of the response.
  * Communications with internal personnel and external authorities.
  * Lessons learned and recommendations for enhancing security controls.
* **Post-Incident Review**: Review each incident to identify gaps in detection, response, and recovery. Adapt policies and update procedures as needed based on these lessons (see fileciteturn2file9).

⠀7. Policy Review
This policy must be reviewed at least annually or in response to significant incidents to ensure its ongoing relevance and effectiveness. Changes to the operational environment or threat landscape should prompt an immediate review.
